# Evidence packet K04
Label: Biocide X, an organometallic compound used in antifouling paints on ships and boats
De-identification: de-identified
t0 (first credible early warning): 1970
Scoring point: t0 + 5 = 1975. Every item below is dated at or before this year.

Names replaced: [Biocide X] = the organometallic antifouling compound; [Compound family X] = its chemical family; [Bay B] = a French Atlantic oyster-farming bay. "EEA case chapter" = EEA (2001) *Late lessons from early warnings* I, case chapter on Biocide X (Santillo, Johnston & Langston). Note: the effect described in Q7–Q9 is highly specific to this compound; informed coders may recognise the case.

## Evidence
Q1. [paraphrase] Compounds of [Compound family X] were first developed as moth-proofing agents in the 1920s and later used more widely as bactericides and fungicides. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2 (citing Moore et al. 1991); Date: 1920s–1940s
Q2. [paraphrase] [Biocide X] and a related compound have been produced since the late 1940s. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2 (citing Laughlin & Linden 1985); Date: late 1940s
Q3. [paraphrase] About 217 people in France were poisoned, and about 100 died, after taking an oral drug contaminated with a related compound of [Compound family X]; the pharmacist was tried for involuntary homicide. — Source: ATSDR, *Toxicological Profile for Tin and Tin Compounds*, ch. 2; US Fish & Wildlife Service Biological Report 85(1.15) (1989); Date: 1954
Q4. [paraphrase] Use of [Biocide X] in marine antifouling paints dates from the 1960s, at first as a booster biocide in copper-based paints. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2; Date: 1960s
Q5. [paraphrase] Without some form of control, fouling on hulls increases drag and fuel consumption; [Biocide X] compounds are extremely effective and relatively economical antifouling biocides. — Source: EEA case chapter, §1; Date: property as of the 1970s
Q6. [paraphrase] Owing to its superior effectiveness over copper, use by private and commercial users rose rapidly and captured a major share of the antifouling market; users included the shipping industry and small-boat owners. — Source: EEA case chapter, §1–2 and Table; Date: early 1970s
Q7. [paraphrase] Early "free association" paints released the biocide rapidly at first and needed frequent reapplication. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2; Date: early 1970s
Q8. [paraphrase] Toxicity to fouling organisms was the intended effect; early testing focused on acute effects, especially mortality, which could not identify sub-lethal effects of prolonged exposure. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2 (citing Laughlin & Linden 1987); Date: 1960s–1970s
Q9. [paraphrase] A penis was recorded in female dogwhelks in Plymouth Sound (UK), far more prevalent close to the harbour than further away. — Source: Blaber 1970, Proc. Malac. Soc. Lond., cited in EEA case chapter, §2; Date: 1970
Q10. [paraphrase] The phenomenon (male sexual structures developing in females, "imposex") was described in the American mud-snail near harbours on the US east coast. — Source: Smith 1971, cited in EEA case chapter, §2 and Table; Date: 1971
Q11. [paraphrase] Despite the severity of the phenomenon, its causal agent remained unknown; analytical techniques sensitive enough to measure the compound in the environment did not yet exist. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2–3; Date: 1970–1975
Q12. [paraphrase] Imposex was observed in [Bay B] in a predatory snail (the oyster drill), leading rapidly to its near extirpation from the bay. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3 (citing Gibbs 1993); Date: 1970
Q13. [paraphrase] The oyster drill was considered a pest by the shellfish industry because it damages oyster stocks. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1970
Q14. [paraphrase] Until the mid-1970s [Bay B] was an important oyster-culture area producing 10 000–15 000 tonnes a year over substantial tidal mudflats. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3 (citing Evans 2000); Date: up to mid-1970s
Q15. [paraphrase] [Bay B] was also popular with leisure craft, with about 7 500 vessels in the mid-1970s; many boat owners were local. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: mid-1970s
Q16. [paraphrase] Snail imposex was reported first around harbours (Plymouth, US east-coast ports), where boats were concentrated. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2; Date: 1970–1971
