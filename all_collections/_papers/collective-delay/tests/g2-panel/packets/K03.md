# Evidence packet K03
Label: Fuel additive X, an ether used in petrol as a substitute for a toxic metal-based anti-knock additive
De-identification: de-identified
t0 (first credible early warning): 1990
Scoring point: t0 + 5 = 1995. Every item below is dated at or before this year.

Names replaced: [Additive X] = the ether additive. "EEA case chapter" = EEA (2001) *Late lessons from early warnings* I, case chapter on Additive X (Krayer von Krauss & Harremoës).

## Evidence
Q1. [paraphrase] Studies showed very low biodegradability of the ether chemical family. — Source: Mills & Stack 1954, cited in EEA case chapter, §8.1 and Table; Date: 1954
Q2. [paraphrase] The resistance of ethers to biological degradation, and information on taste and odour in water, were textbook knowledge. — Source: Sawyer 1960, cited in EEA case chapter, §8.1 and Table; Date: 1960
Q3. [paraphrase] After *Silent Spring* (1962), and incidents of river foaming from non-biodegradable detergents in Europe in the early 1960s, industry and the OECD worked on criteria and methods for testing persistence. — Source: EEA case chapter, §8.1; Date: 1962–1970
Q4. [paraphrase] An OECD working group on test methods for degradation and accumulation was formed; governments, industry and NGOs developed standard biodegradability tests through the 1970s and early 1980s. — Source: EEA case chapter, §8.1; Date: 1978–1984
Q5. [paraphrase] Under the 1979 sixth amendment to the EC classification and labelling directive, new substances had to be notified with data in which persistence could trigger a "particularly dangerous to the environment" classification; [Additive X], marketed before 18 September 1981, was an "existing substance" not bound by these rules. — Source: EEA case chapter, §8.1; Date: 1979–1981
Q6. [paraphrase] Commercial production started in Europe in 1973 and in the United States in 1979; Italy introduced it into petrol in the late 1970s and other European countries mainly in the mid-1980s. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1973–1985
Q7. [paraphrase] It was chosen to replace the phased-out metal anti-knock additive because it is low-cost, easily produced at the refinery, blends without separating, and can be moved through existing pipelines. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: late 1970s–1980s
Q8. [paraphrase] As an oxygenate it allows more complete combustion, cutting CO and ozone-precursor emissions, and raises octane, an alternative to benzene and other aromatics. — Source: EEA case chapter, §4; Date: 1980s–1990
Q9. [paraphrase] EC Directive 85/536/EEC on petrol additives meant no Member State could prevent the use of organic oxygenates below set limits; for [Additive X] the limit was 15% by volume. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1985
Q10. [paraphrase] There were no obvious harmful effects of [Additive X] known when it was chosen; carcinogenicity was not investigated in detail before marketing as a high-production-volume chemical. — Source: EEA case chapter, §5.3 and §8.1; Date: 1980s
Q11. [paraphrase] Studies indicated [Additive X] moves at about the same speed as groundwater, making it very mobile. — Source: Barker et al. 1990, cited in EEA case chapter, §5.1; Date: 1990
Q12. [paraphrase] The first warnings about its persistence in groundwater, based on laboratory-scale experiments, were published. — Source: Barker et al. 1990; Jensen & Arvin 1990 (Technical University of Denmark), cited in EEA case chapter, §8.1; Date: 1990
Q13. "In 1990 The Danish EPA received information from Professor Erik Arvin ... that there might be a problem in relation to groundwater, because of the mobility and apparent persistency in groundwater." — Source: Danish Ministry of Environment and Energy report (1998), quoted in EEA case chapter, §6; Date: 1990
Q14. [paraphrase] The Danish EPA did not consider this a problem at the time; its reason concerned how leakage from storage tanks was regarded (sentence truncated in the source text). — Source: EEA case chapter, §6; Date: 1990
Q15. [paraphrase] The 1990 amendments to the US Clean Air Act required petrol "oxygenates", such as [Additive X] or ethanol, to reduce atmospheric CO or ozone, and US use rose sharply. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3 and Table; Date: 1990
Q16. [paraphrase] Its solubility is about 50 000 mg/l; petrol containing 10% gives an equilibrium water concentration of about 5 000 mg/l. — Source: EEA case chapter, §5.1; Date: physical property, known by 1990
Q17. [paraphrase] It has a potent terpene-like odour detectable in water at very low concentrations; when taste and odour thresholds are exceeded, contaminated drinking water is normally not used. — Source: EEA case chapter, §5.2; Date: property (taste and odour data available by 1960)
Q18. [paraphrase] Inhalation bioassays reported kidney and testicular tumours in male rats and liver tumours in mice. — Source: Chun et al. 1992; Burleigh-Flayer et al. 1992, cited in EEA case chapter, Box 1; Date: 1992
Q19. [paraphrase] The EU adopted regulations for evaluating and controlling existing substances; a risk assessment of [Additive X] under them was assigned to a Member State institute. — Source: EEA case chapter, §8.1; Date: 1993
Q20. [paraphrase] Epidemiological reports indicated increased asthma and other respiratory and inflammatory conditions in the years after the additive was introduced to New York; a combustion product entering ambient air was suggested as a cause. — Source: Crain et al. 1994; Leikauf et al. 1995, cited in EEA case chapter, §5.4; Date: 1994–1995
Q21. [paraphrase] In January 1995 "reformulated gasoline", containing more [Additive X], was introduced in US metropolitan areas with severe ozone pollution. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1995
Q22. [paraphrase] In 1995 [Additive X] was the third most produced organic chemical in the United States, at about 8.0 million tonnes a year. — Source: Johnson et al. 2000, cited in EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1995
Q23. [paraphrase] An oral study reported significantly increased lymphomas and leukaemias in female rats and testicular tumours in male rats. — Source: Belpoggi, Soffritti & Maltoni 1995, Toxicol. Ind. Health 11:119–49, cited in EEA case chapter, Box 1; Date: 1995
Q24. [paraphrase] [Additive X] was detected in drinking-water supply wells in Santa Monica, California; the wells had to be closed and the city lost 71% of its local water supply. — Source: EEA case chapter, Table "Early warnings and actions"; Date: 1995
Q25. [paraphrase] Main petrol-industry users: refiners producing the additive at the refinery and blending it into petrol; the petrochemicals industry selected and supplied it as the substitute. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2–3; Date: 1980s–1995
