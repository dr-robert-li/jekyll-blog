# Evidence packet K01
Label: Substance X, a soil-fumigant pesticide used against nematodes on tropical crops
De-identification: de-identified
t0 (first credible early warning): 1958
Scoring point: t0 + 5 = 1963. Every item below is dated at or before this year.

Names replaced: [Substance X] = the pesticide; [Company A], [Company B] = the two producing chemical companies; [Consultant T] = a university toxicologist working under contract to the producers. Source "EEA case chapter" = EEA (2013) *Late lessons from early warnings* II, case chapter on Substance X.

## Evidence
Q1. [paraphrase] [Substance X] was first produced in the United States in 1955 and used as a soil fumigant to control nematode worms. — Source: EEA case chapter, §1; Date: 1955
Q2. [paraphrase] It was used mainly to protect pineapples, bananas, sugar cane and other produce, mostly in the tropics, applied at 10–125 kg/ha by injection into soil or in irrigation water. — Source: EEA case chapter, §1; Date: 1955–1961
Q3. [paraphrase] The earliest research on the compound's toxicity was carried out by two chemical companies producing it as a nematocide, [Company A] and [Company B]. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2; Date: 1958
Q4. [paraphrase] By 1958 both companies had rat data showing the compound was absorbed through the skin and by inhalation and affected the liver, lung, kidney and testes. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2; Date: 1958
Q5. [paraphrase] [Consultant T], under contract to [Company A], reported that at 5 ppm vapour the testes of male rats shrank, at 10 ppm most had testes half normal size, and at 20 ppm all male rats were sterile. — Source: EEA case chapter, §2; Date: 1958
Q6. "We understand that [Company B] have similar data and are very upset by the effects noted on the testes" — Source: internal [Company A] memorandum (Lykken 1958), quoted in EEA case chapter, §2; Date: 1958
Q7. "If anyone wants to use a male birth control drug, I think we have identified one, but it is not very pleasant to use." — Source: remark by [Consultant T] at a party, recalled by epidemiologist J. Goldsmith (1997), quoted in EEA case chapter, §2; Date: c. 1960
Q8. [paraphrase] Two rodent studies document testicular toxicity. — Source: EEA case chapter, Table "Key early warnings"; Date: 1958
Q9. [paraphrase] Industry toxicologists published their experimental data, showing two outstanding toxic effects: an antispermatogenic effect in males and kidney damage in both sexes of the rat. — Source: Torkelson et al. 1961, as summarised in EEA case chapter, §2; Date: 1961
Q10. [paraphrase] Rats exposed 7 h/day, 5 days/week for 10 weeks: 5 ppm gave an 18.6% (not statistically significant) fall in mean testis weight; 10 ppm a significant 49% fall and a 31.7% rise in kidney weight. — Source: Torkelson et al. 1961, EEA case chapter, Box 1; Date: 1961
Q11. [paraphrase] At 12 ppm, degenerative changes occurred in sperm-forming tissue, with fewer and more abnormal sperm cells; guinea pigs and rabbits also showed significant falls in testis weight. — Source: Torkelson et al. 1961, EEA case chapter, Box 1; Date: 1961
Q12. [paraphrase] [Consultant T], as expert consultant to both companies, supported their request to the US Food and Drug Administration to register the compound; his report called for workplace concentrations below 1 ppm and impermeable protective clothing where skin contact was likely. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1958–1961
Q13. [paraphrase] In discussions with the two companies, the FDA noted that adverse effects occurred at the lowest level studied (5 ppm) after repeated exposure, so current precautions appeared inadequate; the [Company A] representative considered the consultant's recommendations impractical. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1958–1961
Q14. [paraphrase] The 1961 study authors recommended keeping airborne concentration below 1 ppm and stressed that analytical methods, not sensory perception, should be relied on; men briefly exposed to 1.7 ppm described a "definite, not unpleasant odour". — Source: Torkelson et al. 1961 via NIOSH 1978, EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1961
Q15. [paraphrase] The FDA approved and registered [Substance X] as a pesticide and recommended an exposure limit of 1 ppm. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1961
Q16. "Do not breathe vapours, use only in a well-ventilated area and avoid prolonged breathing" — the approved label text, which made no reference to testicular damage. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1961
Q17. [paraphrase] The US Department of Agriculture initially had reservations about the label warnings; [Company A] argued that no adverse effects had been reported at 5 ppm and that the 1.7 ppm odour threshold gave adequate warning; USDA accepted this, although no studies had tested whether the approach was safe. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3 (citing Thrupp 1991); Date: 1961
Q18. [paraphrase] The fumes are only a mild irritant, so workers could be exposed to toxic amounts without being aware of it. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1961 (property as described from the 1961 odour data)
Q19. [paraphrase] Neither manufacturing workers nor pesticide sprayers were placed under medical surveillance. — Source: EEA case chapter, §3; Date: 1961–1963
Q20. [paraphrase] A medical examination of workers at a production plant took place, but testicular function was not examined. — Source: EEA case chapter, Table "Key early warnings"; Date: 1961
Q21. [paraphrase] Data on the compound's persistence and water solubility were published. — Source: EEA case chapter, Table "Key early warnings"; Date: 1961
