# Evidence packet K20

**Label:** Persistent organochlorine compounds (pesticides and industrial chemicals) contaminating a large freshwater basin shared by two countries.
**De-identification:** Not feasible. The basin, the book of 1962 and the chemicals are recognisable, so coders may know the outcome. Score only from the items below.
**t0:** 1962, publication of a book compiling evidence on the effects of organochlorine pesticides (Carson, *Silent spring*).
**Scoring point:** t0 + 5 = 1967. Every item below is dated at or before this year.

## Evidence

Q1. [paraphrase] *Silent spring* compiles "the existing evidence of previous early warnings of the effects of organochlorine pesticides on fish and wildlife". It warns especially of the threat these chemicals pose for cancer in humans. — Source: Carson 1962, as described in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.1; Date: 1962

Q2. [paraphrase] The US and Canada share a boundary in four of the five Great Lakes. Early warnings of contamination come from chemical analyses of residues and from effects seen in wild populations, especially predatory birds. — Source: EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.1; Date: 1962–1967

Q3. [paraphrase] Before the book, UK surveys had examined the risks to workers applying pesticides (1951), residues in food (1953) and risks to wildlife (1955). — Source: Cook 1964 (HMSO review), cited in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2; Date: 1951–1955 (review published 1964)

Q4. [paraphrase] The decline and reproductive failure of Florida bald eagles had been published. Research on Great Lakes bald eagles does not start until the mid-1960s, by which time most of that population has gone. — Source: Broley 1952, 1958; Sprunt et al., cited in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.1; Date: 1952–1966

Q5. [paraphrase] Serialisation of the book in a magazine draws a letter from the general counsel of a pesticide manufacturer to the publisher, threatening legal action unless the last instalment is cancelled. — Source: Lear 1997, as described in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2; Date: 1962

Q6. [paraphrase] After publication, the industry trade association (National Agricultural Chemicals Association) distributes a critical booklet, "Fact and fancy". It sends the booklet with letters to magazine and newspaper editors suggesting that future advertising revenue might suffer if the book were reviewed favourably. — Source: Lear 1997, as described in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2; Date: 1962

Q7. [paraphrase] Intense controversy surrounds organochlorine pesticides in the 1950s and 1960s. A US National Academy of Sciences–National Research Council report, "Pest control and wildlife relationships", is described as partisan and highly controversial. — Source: Lear 1997, as described in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2; Date: 1962–1963

Q8. [paraphrase] The President's Science Advisory Committee issues a report, *Use of Pesticides*, reviewing hazards of persistent pesticides. — Source: PSAC, *Use of Pesticides* (May 1963) (contemporary report; text not opened this session); Date: 1963

Q9. [paraphrase] Naturalists visiting an island in Lake Ontario find two soft-shelled eggs in the nest of a pair of double-crested cormorants. It is the first observation of changed eggshell quality in the basin. — Source: Edwards et al. 1963, cited in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.1 and Table 12.1; Date: 1963

Q10. [paraphrase] In November 1963 a massive fish kill in the lower Mississippi River is eventually traced to the organochlorine pesticide endrin and to its manufacture by a chemical company at Memphis. — Source: Lear 1997, as described in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2; Date: 1963–1964

Q11. [paraphrase] A pesticide manufacturer states: "The implication of endrin in the 1963 Mississippi River fish kill has not been verified by recent studies". — Source: Shell Chemical Company 1967, quoted in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2; Date: 1967

Q12. [paraphrase] The US Department of Agriculture, responsible for registering pesticides, "tended to align itself with the pesticide manufacturers and the farmers". US authorities are described as reluctant to act. — Source: EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2; Date: 1962–1967

Q13. [paraphrase] A manufacturer writes of UK bird declines: "the correlations between the time of usage of aldrin/dieldrin and the declines in populations are difficult to assess as there is insufficient quantitative data". It adds that the relationship cannot be established "in a rigorous manner". — Source: Robinson 1967 (Shell Chemical Company), quoted in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2; Date: 1967

Q14. [paraphrase] In the mid-1960s an outbreak of adult and kit mortality in ranch mink fed on Great Lakes fish seriously affects the mink-ranching industry. — Source: Hartsough 1965, cited in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.1; Date: 1965

Q15. [paraphrase] The first analytical results on organochlorine compounds in Great Lakes organisms are published. They document the bioaccumulation of DDT and its metabolites, and of dieldrin, in a Lake Michigan food chain. — Source: Hickey et al. 1966, cited in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.1 and Table 12.1; Date: 1966

Q16. [paraphrase] University researchers document effects of DDT, its metabolites and dieldrin on reproduction and mortality in Lake Michigan herring gulls. — Source: Keith 1966; Ludwig & Tomoff 1966, cited in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.1; Date: 1966

Q17. [paraphrase] PCBs are discovered in a white-tailed sea eagle in Sweden, prompting work on analytical methods to detect PCBs. — Source: Jensen 1966, cited in EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.1; Date: 1966

Q18. [paraphrase] A lawyer brings a suit for a town natural-resources committee on Long Island to stop a county mosquito-control commission from using DDT. — Source: EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2 (Yannacone suit); Date: 1966

Q19. [paraphrase] The suit leads to the founding in 1967 of the Environmental Defense Fund, a group of lawyers, scientists and economists. — Source: EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2; Date: 1967

Q20. [paraphrase] The two national governments have referred pollution of the lower Great Lakes to their joint boundary commission (the International Joint Commission). In the 1960s the primary concern is eutrophication, particularly of Lake Erie, not organochlorines. — Source: EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.2 (reference year 1964 from IJC records, not stated in EEA); Date: 1964

Q21. [paraphrase] Organochlorine compounds are produced and used widely across North America in the 20th century, resulting in global contamination with "a wide variety of toxic and persistent organochlorine residues". — Source: EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.1; Date: up to 1962

Q22. [paraphrase] The documented effects are in wildlife: fish-eating birds, eagles and ranch mink. EEA's account cites no study of the health of people in the basin up to this date. — Source: EEA 2001, ch. 12 §12.1; Date: 1967
