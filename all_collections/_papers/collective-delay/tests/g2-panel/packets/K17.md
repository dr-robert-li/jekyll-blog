# Evidence packet K17

**Label:** A light metal (beryllium) and its compounds, handled by workers in extraction, alloy and lamp-making plants.
**De-identification:** Not feasible; the metal has to be named for the evidence to make sense. Coders may know the outcome. Score only from the items below.
**t0:** 1933. EEA dates the first reported cases to "the 1930s". The year used is the first published report: Weber & Engelhardt, Germany, 1933.
**Scoring point:** t0 + 5 = 1938. Every item below is dated at or before this year.
**Note to coders:** Little evidence survives from this early window, so this packet is thinner than the others. Use "insufficient evidence" where it applies.

## Evidence

Q1. [paraphrase] German investigators publish a study of dusts arising from beryllium extraction: "Untersuchung von Stäuben aus der Berylliumgewinnung". — Source: Weber & Engelhardt, Zentralbl. Gewerbehyg. Unfallverhüt. 10:41–47 (1933), as cited in later reviews; Date: 1933

Q2. [paraphrase] The 1933 German work reports toxic effects of beryllium dusts on guinea pigs. Later reviews credit it with the first description of acute chemical pneumonitis in beryllium workers. — Source: Van Ordstrand et al.'s review, as summarised in an NLM-archived research proposal; pulmonology textbook chapter "Chronic beryllium disease and hard-metal lung diseases"; Date: 1933

Q3. [paraphrase] The 1933 report appears in an occupational hygiene and accident-prevention journal, i.e. inside the industrial-hygiene literature rather than general medicine. — Source: citation record of Weber & Engelhardt 1933; Date: 1933

Q4. [paraphrase] A report titled "Poisoning by vapors of beryllium oxyfluoride" is published in the US *Journal of Industrial Hygiene and Toxicology*. — Source: Gelman, J. Ind. Hyg. Toxicol. 18:371–379 (1936); Date: 1936

Q5. [quote] "The mechanism of poisoning by beryllium oxyfluoride is not represented simply by the sum of the actions of beryllium and fluorine." The author ties the protracted action to fixation of the poison in the alveoli and bronchioles. — Source: Gelman 1936, quoted on an advocacy website (Fluoride Action Network; non-neutral source); Date: 1936

Q6. [paraphrase] Beryllium-related pneumonitis is first described in Germany and Russia in the 1930s. The cause is not known, and early researchers attribute the illness to airborne acid anions such as fluorides and sulphates rather than to beryllium itself. — Source: Borak 2006, J. Occup. Environ. Med. 48:109–116, describing 1930s understanding; Date: 1933–1938

Q7. [paraphrase] European researchers are the first to link beryllium exposure to occupational lung disease. — Source: review in Int. J. Environ. Res. Public Health 7:115 (2010), citing Gelman 1936; Date: 1936

Q8. [paraphrase] The Brush Beryllium Company is incorporated in Cleveland, Ohio, on 9 January 1931 with $500 of capital. Its founders developed a process for extracting beryllium from beryl ore. — Source: Materion corporate history; Date: 1931

Q9. [paraphrase] The founders' laboratory had been researching uses for beryllium since 1926. — Source: Materion corporate history; Date: 1926

Q10. [paraphrase] The company's first electrically heated beryl furnace is built two years after incorporation. Production operations move to Lorain, Ohio, in 1935. — Source: Materion corporate history; Date: 1933–1935

Q11. [paraphrase] Beryllium metal output is tiny: about 60 grams in 1936. — Source: Materion corporate history (production figures); Date: 1936

Q12. [paraphrase] The first significant industrial use of beryllium in the US is in the production of fluorescent lamp tubes. — Source: EEA 2013, ch. 6 §6.2; Date: 1930s

Q13. [paraphrase] The people exposed are production workers in a small number of extraction, alloy and lamp-making plants. — Source: EEA 2013, ch. 6 §6.2; Materion corporate history; Date: 1930s

Q14. [paraphrase] Occupational lung disease from dust is already a public issue in the US. The Hawk's Nest tunnel silicosis deaths (1930–31) lead to congressional hearings, and the US Department of Labor convenes a National Silicosis Conference. — Source: US House Committee on Labor hearings (Jan 1936); US Department of Labor National Silicosis Conference (1936) (contemporary records; text not opened this session); Date: 1936

Q15. [paraphrase] Radium-induced jaw and bone disease in luminous dial painters had been identified by a New Jersey pathologist in the late 1920s. It is a precedent for occupational poisoning by a novel industrial material. — Source: Martland 1925–29, as described in EEA 2001, ch. 3 §3.2; Date: 1925–1929

Q16. [paraphrase] No exposure limit for beryllium compounds has been published, and there is no agreed method for measuring beryllium in workplace air. — Source: inference from EEA 2013, ch. 6 §6.2 and Borak 2006 (no earlier limit is mentioned); Date: 1938
