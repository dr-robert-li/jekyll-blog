# Evidence packet K02
Label: Carbon dioxide from burning fossil fuels (coal) and its possible effect on the Earth's temperature
De-identification: not feasible: names kept; coders may know the outcome
t0 (first credible early warning): 1897
Scoring point: t0 + 5 = 1902. Every item below is dated at or before this year.

Note: EEA's chapter text dates the first credible warning to 1897; its table lists Arrhenius's calculation under 1896. "EEA ch. 14" = EEA (2013) *Late lessons from early warnings* II, ch. 14 (Grassl & Metz). "AIP" = S. Weart, *The Discovery of Global Warming*, American Institute of Physics, essay "The Carbon Dioxide Greenhouse Effect" (history.aip.org/climate/co2.htm), used only for events before 1903.

## Evidence
Q1. [paraphrase] Fourier, drawing on Saussure's observations, understood the atmosphere's vertical temperature gradient as similar to the heating produced by a glass plate over an insulated box; until then the absorption properties of atmospheric gases were completely unknown. — Source: EEA ch. 14, §14.2 (citing Fourier 1824); Date: 1824
Q2. [paraphrase] Eunice Foote measured that carbon dioxide absorbs heat in sunlight and speculated that a period with more of the gas would have been warmer; the note received little attention. — Source: AIP; Date: 1856
Q3. [paraphrase] Tyndall's laboratory work identified gases that trap heat rays: chiefly water vapour, but also carbon dioxide and hydrocarbon gases, even though CO2 is only a few parts in ten thousand of the air. — Source: AIP; Date: 1859
Q4. "the atmosphere admits the entrance of the solar heat but checks its exit, and the result is a tendency to accumulate heat at the surface of the planet" — Source: Tyndall 1863, quoted in EEA ch. 14, §14.2; Date: 1863
Q5. [paraphrase] The Stockholm Physics Society, interested in the cause of the ice ages, discussed these questions; A. Högbom lectured on the forces that might change the atmospheric level of CO2. — Source: AIP; Date: early 1890s
Q6. [paraphrase] Högbom calculated that factories and other industrial sources were adding CO2 at a rate roughly comparable to the natural geochemical processes that emit or absorb it; the CO2 from coal burned in 1896 would raise the atmospheric level by "scarcely a thousandth part". — Source: AIP; Date: 1890s–1896
Q7. [paraphrase] Arrhenius used Langley's published CO2 absorption bands to argue that increased combustion of coal, "at that time mainly in Great Britain", could lead to higher surface temperatures. — Source: EEA ch. 14, §14.2; Date: 1896
Q8. "if the quantity of carbonic acid increases in geometric progression, the augmentation of the temperature will increase nearly in arithmetic progression" — Source: Arrhenius 1896, quoted in EEA ch. 14, §14.2; Date: 1896
Q9. [paraphrase] Arrhenius estimated that doubling atmospheric CO2 would raise global mean temperature by several degrees (3–5 °C in EEA's account; 5–6 °C averaged over latitudes in AIP's). — Source: EEA ch. 14, Table 14.1; AIP; Date: 1896
Q10. [paraphrase] The calculation was a laborious numerical computation done to explain the ice ages: halving CO2 could lower European temperatures by 4–5 °C. — Source: AIP; Date: 1896
Q11. [paraphrase] Arrhenius reckoned that at the 1896 rate of fuel burning it would take perhaps three thousand years for CO2 to rise that high. — Source: AIP; Date: 1896
Q12. [paraphrase] Högbom doubted the level would ever rise that much; a simple calculation suggested the oceans would absorb five-sixths of any added gas. — Source: AIP; Date: 1896
Q13. [paraphrase] Arrhenius was not alarmed by warming from an enhanced greenhouse effect: his initial concern was the harm to Scandinavia if cooling occurred. — Source: EEA ch. 14, §14.2; Date: 1896
Q14. [paraphrase] A few degrees' warming "hardly sounded like a bad idea in chilly Sweden". — Source: AIP (characterisation of contemporary attitudes); Date: 1896
Q15. [paraphrase] In the 1896 article, future warming was mentioned only in passing within a detailed discussion of the cause of the ice ages. — Source: AIP; Date: 1896
Q16. [paraphrase] The American geologist T. C. Chamberlin and a few others studied how CO2 cycles through seawater, minerals and living matter, but were pursuing climate shifts over millions of years; "Nobody took much interest in the hypothetical future warming caused by human industry." — Source: AIP; Date: late 1890s
Q17. [paraphrase] Brückner raised human-caused climate change via deforestation and cultivation altering land-surface reflectivity and evaporation, not via the greenhouse effect. — Source: EEA ch. 14, §14.2 (citing Penck & Brückner 1901–1909); Date: late 1890s–1901
Q18. [paraphrase] Apart from a few follow-up papers by Arrhenius (e.g. 1899), the lack of accurate measurements of trace-gas concentrations prevented a continuing debate. — Source: EEA ch. 14, §14.2; Date: 1899
Q19. [paraphrase] Experts could dismiss the calculation as oversimplified, e.g. for ignoring how cloudiness might change in a warmer, more humid world. — Source: AIP; Date: c. 1900
Q20. [paraphrase] K. Ångström had an assistant measure infrared passage through a tube of CO2; cutting the gas by a third scarcely changed absorption, suggesting a trace "saturated" absorption and that more CO2 would have negligible effect; water-vapour bands also appeared to overlap CO2's. — Source: AIP; Date: 1900
Q21. [paraphrase] The early work was done by academic physicists, chemists and geologists in Stockholm and the United States, motivated by the ice-age riddle. — Source: AIP; Date: 1890s–1900
