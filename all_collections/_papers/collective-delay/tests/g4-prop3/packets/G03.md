# Evidence packet G03

- **Domain:** credit ratings of structured-finance securities (residential mortgage-backed securities and collateralised debt obligations); securities regulation and bank capital
- **What the model does:** quantitative models and criteria run by credit rating agencies that estimate loss and default on pools of mortgages and other debt, and set how much protection each tranche needs to earn a given letter rating; the ratings were relied on by investors and written into financial regulation (capital rules, investment limits).
- **Scale:** tens of thousands of securities rated; investors and institutions across many countries relied on the ratings.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = start of the period in which the agencies' quantitative structured-finance models were in routine use as the basis for ratings that investors and regulations relied on, and from which later government settlements describe the agencies' conduct (see notes).
- **De-identification:** names of agencies, firms, regulators, legislatures, courts, countries and statutes tokenised; not fully feasible (the case is unmistakable).

## Context (not episodes)
- Before A+0: ratings by agencies recognised by [REGULATOR-1] had been written into [COUNTRY-A] securities and banking rules for decades (S10).
- A+3 to A+4: mass downgrades of mortgage-backed securities and debt obligations rated at or near the top grade; large investor losses; press and legislative criticism of the issuer-pays model and conflicts of interest (S8, S9).
- A+3: [AGENCY-B] discovered a coding error in a model for one structured product that had raised ratings, and a committee chose not to downgrade (see E14) (S11).
- A+6 to A+9: academic and press criticism continued; [AGENCY-A] alleged that the federal government's later suit was retaliation for its downgrade of the national government's own debt (S16).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+2] National legislation on rating agencies ([STATUTE-1], [LEGISLATURE-1], [COUNTRY-A]).**
- Brought by: [LEGISLATURE-1]. Funded or run by: legislature.
- Sought: to replace informal recognition of rating agencies with a registration and oversight scheme run by [REGULATOR-1], aimed at transparency, competition and conflicts of interest.
- Outcome: enacted; [REGULATOR-1] adopted implementing rules the next year. The statute barred the regulator from regulating the methods and procedures by which agencies set ratings.
- Stated reasons: concern about limited competition and accountability among rating agencies across all rating types; not specific to structured finance.
- Relief applied to: all recognised rating agencies.
- Sources: S12.

E2. **[A+4] Policy statement of a national inter-agency financial markets working group ([BODY-1]).**
- Brought by: [BODY-1] (heads of the treasury, central bank and market regulators). Funded or run by: government.
- Sought: review of causes of market turmoil, including the role of ratings of mortgage-backed securities and debt obligations.
- Outcome: recommended that agencies disclose more about their structured-finance methods, distinguish structured from corporate ratings, and that [REGULATOR-1] review agency practices; recommended investors not rely solely on ratings.
- Stated reasons: flaws in rating agencies' assessments of subprime mortgage-backed securities and related products were a principal cause of the turmoil.
- Relief applied to: recommendations to agencies, regulators and investors.
- Sources: S13.

E3. **[A+4] International report to finance ministers and central bankers ([ORG-2]).**
- Brought by: [ORG-2], an international body of national authorities. Funded or run by: member authorities.
- Sought: recommendations to strengthen markets after the turmoil.
- Outcome: recommended that agencies improve the quality of ratings of structured products, use different symbols or added information for structured-finance ratings, and manage conflicts; asked investors and authorities to reduce mechanical reliance on ratings.
- Stated reasons: poor performance of structured-product ratings and weaknesses in the agencies' models and assumptions.
- Relief applied to: recommendations to agencies and member authorities.
- Sources: S14.

E4. **[A+4] Revision of an international code of conduct for rating agencies ([ORG-1]).**
- Brought by: [ORG-1], the international organisation of securities regulators, after a task-force report on agencies' role in structured finance. Funded or run by: [ORG-1].
- Sought: changes to the voluntary code governing agency conduct.
- Outcome: code revised to add provisions on the quality of rating methods, review of models, prohibition on analysts recommending deal structures they rate, and distinguishing structured-finance ratings.
- Stated reasons: findings that structured-finance ratings had performed poorly and that agencies' practices raised conflict and quality concerns.
- Relief applied to: agencies adopting the code (voluntary).
- Sources: S15.

E5. **[A+4] Agreement between a state attorney general ([AG-1], [STATE-1]) and the three main agencies ([AGENCY-A], [AGENCY-B], [AGENCY-C]).**
- Brought by: [AG-1], after an investigation of the agencies' ratings of mortgage-backed securities. Funded or run by: state.
- Sought: changes to how agencies are paid and how they review mortgage pools.
- Outcome: agencies agreed to charge for initial reviews of mortgage deals whether or not they were hired to rate them, to disclose information about all deals they reviewed, and to set due-diligence standards for loan data from originators. No penalty reported.
- Stated reasons: the issuer-pays model and "rating shopping" gave issuers incentives to pick the most favourable rating.
- Relief applied to: all mortgage-backed deals rated by the three agencies.
- Sources: S17.

E6. **[A+4] Examination report by the national securities regulator ([REGULATOR-1]) of three agencies.**
- Brought by: [REGULATOR-1] staff. Funded or run by: [REGULATOR-1].
- Sought: examination of the three agencies' ratings of subprime mortgage-backed securities and related debt obligations.
- Outcome: report found that the agencies struggled with the volume and complexity of deals; that significant aspects of rating processes and model changes were not always disclosed or documented; that policies for handling conflicts of interest were inadequate; that surveillance of existing ratings was under-resourced; and that analysts were aware of fee considerations. Agencies were asked to take remedial steps; no enforcement action.
- Stated reasons: as found above.
- Relief applied to: the three agencies.
- Sources: S18.

E7. **[A+4] Hearing of a national legislative oversight committee ([COMMITTEE-2]).**
- Brought by: [COMMITTEE-2], lower house of [LEGISLATURE-1]. Funded or run by: legislature.
- Sought: inquiry into rating agencies' role in the crisis; the committee obtained and published internal agency documents and heard testimony from agency executives.
- Outcome: documents released showed internal staff concerns about model inadequacy and competitive pressure; no formal report or sanction from this hearing.
- Stated reasons: as above.
- Relief applied to: none.
- Sources: S19.

E8. **[A+4 to A+5] Rule-making by the national securities regulator ([REGULATOR-1]).**
- Brought by: [REGULATOR-1]. Funded or run by: [REGULATOR-1].
- Sought: rules on conflicts and disclosure for agencies, aimed at structured-finance ratings.
- Outcome: rules adopted in stages: barred analysts from rating deals they helped structure and from fee negotiations; required public disclosure of rating histories and of whether and how models were used; required issuers who hire one agency to share deal information with other agencies so they could issue unsolicited ratings. Further proposals to remove rating references from the regulator's own rules were made.
- Stated reasons: conflicts of interest and lack of transparency identified in E6.
- Relief applied to: all recognised agencies and issuers of structured products.
- Sources: S20.

E9. **[A+4 to A+9] Investor lawsuit over ratings of structured investment vehicle notes, federal trial court ([COURT-1]); settled.**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-1], a foreign bank, and other institutional investors (14 plaintiffs by the end), against [AGENCY-A], [AGENCY-B] and the arranging bank [BANK-1]. Funded or run by: private plaintiffs; not otherwise reported.
- Sought: damages for losses on top-rated notes of a structured investment vehicle that collapsed.
- Outcome: at A+5 the court refused to dismiss the fraud claims; at A+8 it refused summary judgment; at A+9, shortly before trial, all defendants settled (amount not reported in sources consulted).
- Stated reasons: the court held that ratings distributed to a small group of selected investors, and produced in close collaboration with the arranger, were not protected as public speech; and that the ratings could be actionable misstatements rather than mere opinions if the agencies did not genuinely or reasonably believe them or they lacked a basis in fact.
- Relief applied to: the plaintiff investors only.
- Sources: S21.

E10. **[A+4 to A+5] Investor lawsuit over ratings of another structured investment vehicle, same federal trial court ([COURT-1]).**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-2], a local government in [COUNTRY-A], and other investors, against [AGENCY-A], [AGENCY-B] and the vehicle's sponsor. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: damages for losses on top-rated notes.
- Outcome: the same judge applied the reasoning in E9 and refused to dismiss; final outcome not reported in sources consulted.
- Stated reasons: as in E9.
- Relief applied to: the plaintiffs only.
- Sources: S21.

E11. **[A+3 to A+9] Shareholder securities-fraud action against the parent of [AGENCY-B], federal trial court ([COURT-2]).**
- Brought by: shareholders of [AGENCY-B]'s parent company (pension and union funds as lead plaintiffs). Funded or run by: private plaintiffs.
- Sought: damages for alleged misstatements about the independence and methods of [AGENCY-B]'s structured-finance ratings, which allegedly inflated the share price.
- Outcome: at A+7 the court refused to let the plaintiffs proceed on behalf of all shareholders; at A+9 it granted summary judgment to the company; the remaining claims were then settled on terms the company described as favourable (amount not reported).
- Stated reasons: not reported in detail in sources consulted.
- Relief applied to: none beyond the private settlement.
- Sources: S22.

E12. **[A+5 to A+8] State pension funds' lawsuit against three agencies, federal trial court then federal appeals court ([COURT-3 (appellate)]).**
- Brought by: five state pension and investment funds of [STATE-2], led by a police and fire pension fund, with the state attorney general's office acting for them. Funded or run by: state funds.
- Sought: hundreds of millions of dollars in damages for losses on mortgage-backed securities rated by [AGENCY-A], [AGENCY-B] and [AGENCY-C], under state securities law and negligent misrepresentation.
- Outcome: dismissed by the trial court at A+7; dismissal affirmed by [COURT-3 (appellate)] at A+8.
- Stated reasons: the agencies were not sellers of the securities as the state statute requires; the funds' pleading lacked the needed specificity; the agencies owed no duty of care to the very large group of eligible investors who had no close relationship with them. (The trial court had also treated the ratings as predictive opinions.)
- Relief applied to: none.
- Sources: S23.

E13. **[A+5 to A+12] State public pension fund's lawsuit against three agencies, state courts ([COURT-4], [COURT-5 (appellate)]); settled.**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-3], a large state public employees' pension fund in [STATE-3]. Funded or run by: the fund.
- Sought: damages (losses reported in the hundreds of millions of dollars) for top-rated notes of three structured investment vehicles, for negligent misrepresentation and interference with economic advantage.
- Outcome: interference claim dismissed early; at A+10 the state appeals court upheld refusal of the agencies' motion to strike the suit under a free-speech protection statute, letting the negligent-misrepresentation claim proceed; [AGENCY-A] settled at A+11 for over a hundred million dollars; [AGENCY-B] settled later (amount not verified in sources consulted).
- Stated reasons: the appeals court accepted that the claims arose from speech on a public issue but found the fund had shown a probability of succeeding on the merits.
- Relief applied to: the plaintiff fund only.
- Sources: S24.

E14. **[A+6] Report of investigation by the national securities regulator ([REGULATOR-1]) concerning [AGENCY-B].**
- Brought by: [REGULATOR-1] enforcement staff. Funded or run by: [REGULATOR-1].
- Sought: investigation of whether [AGENCY-B] committed fraud when a rating committee abroad declined to correct ratings of a structured credit product after finding a coding error in the model behind them, and whether its registration statements were accurate.
- Outcome: the regulator issued a public report instead of an enforcement action, citing uncertainty about its jurisdiction over conduct abroad; warned agencies to maintain effective internal controls over rating methods and accurate filings, noting that new legislation (E15) widened jurisdiction.
- Stated reasons: the model error had raised the ratings by between one and a half and three and a half notches; the committee's decision not to downgrade was based partly on concern for the agency's reputation.
- Relief applied to: none; guidance to all agencies.
- Sources: S11.

E15. **[A+6] National financial reform legislation ([STATUTE-2]), rating-agency part.**
- Brought by: [LEGISLATURE-1]. Funded or run by: legislature.
- Sought: reform of rating agencies after the crisis.
- Outcome: enacted. Provisions: legislative findings that ratings of structured products had been inaccurate and contributed to the crisis; created a dedicated rating-agency office at [REGULATOR-1] with annual examinations; required internal controls over rating methods and disclosure of methods and assumptions; eased the pleading standard for private suits against agencies and exposed them to liability as experts in registration statements; removed agencies' exemption from fair-disclosure rules; required every federal agency to remove references to and reliance on credit ratings from its regulations; ordered studies, including on how agencies are assigned to rate structured products.
- Stated reasons: the statute's findings say ratings of structured financial products had proved inaccurate, contributing to the mismanagement of risk and the crisis, and that agencies' gatekeeper role justified oversight and accountability like that of auditors and analysts.
- Relief applied to: all recognised agencies; all federal regulators' rules.
- Sources: S25.

E16. **[A+6 to A+7] National commission of inquiry into the financial crisis ([COMMISSION-1]).**
- Brought by: [COMMISSION-1], established by [LEGISLATURE-1]. Funded or run by: government.
- Sought: inquiry into causes of the crisis; held a hearing on [AGENCY-B] and examined it as a case study.
- Outcome: final report (A+7) concluded that failures of the rating agencies were essential to the crisis: securities could not have been marketed and sold without their ratings. It found [AGENCY-B]'s models relied on flawed and outdated assumptions, that it faced pressure from issuers who paid for ratings, that its resources were inadequate, and that it downgraded massive numbers of securities once losses emerged. Dissenting commissioners gave different weight to causes.
- Stated reasons: as found above.
- Relief applied to: none (findings only).
- Sources: S9.

E17. **[A+6 to A+7] Inquiry by an investigations subcommittee of the national upper house ([COMMITTEE-1]).**
- Brought by: [COMMITTEE-1]. Funded or run by: legislature.
- Sought: investigation of the crisis, including a case study of [AGENCY-A] and [AGENCY-B]; public hearing at A+6.
- Outcome: report (A+7) found the agencies issued inaccurate top ratings using models that relied on limited and outdated data, failed to update models when they knew performance was deteriorating, were subject to conflicts from the issuer-pays model and market-share pressure, and lacked staff; recommended that the regulator rank agencies by accuracy, help investors hold them accountable, and strengthen oversight.
- Stated reasons: as found above.
- Relief applied to: none (findings and recommendations).
- Sources: S26.

E18. **[A+7] Investor lawsuit over mortgage-backed securities, federal appeals court ([COURT-6 (appellate)]).**
- Brought by: investors in mortgage pass-through certificates issued by trusts of a failed investment bank ([PLAINTIFF-4], pension funds as lead plaintiffs). Funded or run by: private plaintiffs.
- Sought: damages from [AGENCY-A], [AGENCY-B] and [AGENCY-C] under federal securities law as underwriters or controlling persons of the issuers.
- Outcome: dismissal affirmed.
- Stated reasons: the agencies did not purchase or sell the securities and so were not underwriters, and their role in shaping deal structures to obtain ratings did not make them controlling persons of the issuers.
- Relief applied to: none.
- Sources: S27.

E19. **[A+6 to A+13] Lawsuits by state attorneys general against [AGENCY-A] and/or [AGENCY-B], state courts.**
- Brought by: attorneys general of more than a dozen states and one federal district (first filed by [STATE-4] at A+6, then others including [STATE-5] and [STATE-6]; many states filed against [AGENCY-A] alongside E22). Funded or run by: state governments.
- Sought: penalties and other relief under state consumer-protection laws for misrepresenting the independence and objectivity of their structured-finance ratings.
- Outcome: the suits against [AGENCY-A] were resolved in the joint settlement in E22 (A+11); pending suits against [AGENCY-B] by three states were resolved in the joint settlement in E27 (A+13).
- Stated reasons: see E22 and E27.
- Relief applied to: see E22 and E27.
- Sources: S16, S28.

E20. **[A+8 to A+10] Investor lawsuit in a foreign national court ([COURT-7], [COUNTRY-B]) over the rating of a leveraged structured credit product.**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-5], a group of about a dozen local government councils in [COUNTRY-B], against [AGENCY-A], the arranging bank [BANK-2] and the financial adviser that sold them the notes. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: damages for losses of most of their investment in notes rated at the top grade.
- Outcome: at A+8 the trial judge found [AGENCY-A] liable for misleading and deceptive conduct and negligence; liability was shared among the three defendants. At A+10 the full appeals court unanimously dismissed the appeals. 
- Stated reasons: the top rating had no reasonable basis: a reasonably competent agency could not have given the product that rating, because the agency's model used an unjustified assumption about the volatility of the underlying credit index and other inputs supplied by the arranger without checking them; [AGENCY-A] owed a duty of care to the identifiable group of investors the rating was intended to reach. On appeal the agency accepted that the rating was flawed.
- Relief applied to: the plaintiff councils only.
- Sources: S29.

E21. **[A+8] Study report by the national securities regulator ([REGULATOR-1]) on assignment of structured-finance ratings.**
- Brought by: [REGULATOR-1], as required by [STATUTE-2]. Funded or run by: [REGULATOR-1].
- Sought: study of the conflicts in the issuer-pays system for structured products and of alternatives, including a system in which a public or private body assigns an agency to each deal.
- Outcome: report described the options and their drawbacks and recommended a public roundtable rather than adopting an assignment system.
- Stated reasons: each alternative raised practical and legal concerns.
- Relief applied to: none.
- Sources: S30.

E22. **[A+9 to A+11] Federal civil fraud suit by the national justice department ([AGENCY-1]) against [AGENCY-A] and its parent, federal trial court ([COURT-8]); settled.**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1] under [STATUTE-3], a banking-fraud civil penalty statute; parallel state suits (E19). Funded or run by: federal government.
- Sought: civil penalties for defrauding investors (including federally insured institutions) by issuing ratings of mortgage-backed securities and debt obligations that were said to be objective and independent but were influenced by business considerations.
- Outcome: the court refused to dismiss (A+9). At A+11 [AGENCY-A] agreed to pay over a billion dollars, half to [AGENCY-1] and half to about twenty states and one federal district, and separately settled the [STATE-3] pension fund's suit (E13). It agreed to a statement of facts acknowledging conduct in its ratings of these securities over a four-year period, without admitting violation of law, and withdrew its allegation that the suit was retaliation.
- Stated reasons: per the agreed facts and the government's account, the agency delayed or adjusted updates to its debt-obligation model and default assumptions in response to competitive and market-share pressure, and declined to downgrade some deteriorating assets out of concern for business effects.
- Relief applied to: payments to the federal and state governments; no individual investor relief beyond E13.
- Sources: S16, S31.

E23. **[A+11] Enforcement actions by the national securities regulator ([REGULATOR-1]) and two states against [AGENCY-A].**
- Brought by: [REGULATOR-1], with parallel actions by [AG-1] and another state attorney general. Funded or run by: government.
- Sought: sanctions for misconduct in ratings of commercial mortgage-backed securities and for failures in surveillance of residential mortgage-backed securities.
- Outcome: settled; [AGENCY-A] paid tens of millions of dollars to the regulator plus over ten million to the two states, and was barred for one year from rating one type of commercial mortgage deal.
- Stated reasons: the agency publicly described one method for setting credit protection on certain commercial mortgage deals but used a different, more favourable one; and internal-control failures in its surveillance of residential mortgage-backed securities, including a change in an assumption that made ratings less conservative.
- Relief applied to: the agency (sanctions); affected rating type.
- Sources: S32.

E24. **[A+9] Revision of the regional rating-agency regulation of [BLOC-1] ([STATUTE-5]).**
- Brought by: legislature of [BLOC-1]. Funded or run by: [BLOC-1] institutions.
- Sought: to reduce reliance on ratings, increase competition and make agencies liable.
- Outcome: adopted. Required financial institutions and authorities to reduce sole or mechanical reliance on ratings; gave investors a civil-liability claim against agencies for intentional or grossly negligent breaches; required rotation of agencies for certain re-securitisation ratings and use of at least two agencies for structured products. Builds on the first regional regulation at A+5 (registration and supervision of agencies, separate identification of structured-finance ratings) and the creation of a regional supervisor at A+7.
- Stated reasons: overreliance on ratings and conflicts of interest exposed by the crisis.
- Relief applied to: all agencies operating in [BLOC-1]; regulated institutions.
- Sources: S33.

E25. **[A+10] Rule-making by the national securities regulator ([REGULATOR-1]) under [STATUTE-2].**
- Brought by: [REGULATOR-1]. Funded or run by: [REGULATOR-1].
- Sought: rules implementing the reform legislation's rating-agency provisions.
- Outcome: adopted rules requiring internal control structures over rating methods, policies for approving and changing models and procedures, disclosure of performance statistics, a standard form disclosing assumptions and limits with each rating, look-back reviews when analysts leave for issuers, analyst training and testing, and disclosure of third-party due-diligence reports on asset-backed securities. Separately, the regulator and banking regulators removed references to ratings from many rules over A+7 to A+10.
- Stated reasons: to address conflicts and the quality and transparency of ratings, especially of asset-backed securities.
- Relief applied to: all recognised agencies and issuers of asset-backed securities.
- Sources: S34.

E26. **[A+7 to A+21] Annual examinations of rating agencies by the regulator's dedicated rating-agency office ([REGULATOR-1]).**
- Brought by: [REGULATOR-1], as required by [STATUTE-2]. Funded or run by: [REGULATOR-1].
- Sought: annual examination of each recognised agency, including adherence to rating methods and internal controls over models.
- Outcome: published annual staff reports identifying deficiencies at the larger agencies, including in model governance and in following stated methods, with recommendations; some findings led to enforcement (e.g. E28).
- Stated reasons: varied by year.
- Relief applied to: the examined agencies.
- Sources: S35.

E27. **[A+13] Settlement between [AGENCY-B] and the national justice department ([AGENCY-1]) with about twenty states and one federal district.**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1] (no suit filed) and state attorneys general, three of which had suits pending (E19). Funded or run by: governments.
- Sought: civil penalties for alleged misconduct in rating mortgage-backed securities and debt obligations before the crisis.
- Outcome: [AGENCY-B] and its parent paid hundreds of millions of dollars (roughly half as a penalty to [AGENCY-1] under [STATUTE-3], the rest to the states), agreed to a statement of facts and to compliance measures, without admitting violation of law.
- Stated reasons: per the government, the agency did not follow its own rating standards, used more lenient standards than it published, and departed from its published methods without disclosure, while its issuer-pays model created potential conflicts.
- Relief applied to: payments to federal and state governments; compliance commitments for the agency.
- Sources: S28.

E28. **[A+14] Enforcement action by the national securities regulator ([REGULATOR-1]) against [AGENCY-B].**
- Brought by: [REGULATOR-1]. Funded or run by: [REGULATOR-1].
- Sought: sanctions for internal-control failures over models used to rate residential mortgage-backed securities after the crisis, and for inconsistent use of rating symbols.
- Outcome: settled without admitting or denying; [AGENCY-B] paid over ten million dollars in total and agreed to retain an independent consultant.
- Stated reasons: the agency lacked effective controls to detect errors in a model it used to rate these securities over about four years; it later corrected hundreds of ratings covering tens of billions of dollars.
- Relief applied to: the agency (sanctions and remediation of ratings).
- Sources: S36.
