# Evidence packet G05

- **Domain:** insurance rate filing (residential property, windstorm)
- **What the model does:** simulates large sets of possible storms and estimates expected annual windstorm losses for insured homes; insurers use the output as the windstorm loss component of their premium rate filings.
- **Scale:** millions of insured homes in one state; tens of insurers filing rates on it.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = after a catastrophic storm, insurers in [STATE-A] begin basing residential rate filings on commercial catastrophe models ([MODEL]) supplied by [VENDOR-1], [VENDOR-2] and [VENDOR-3], instead of the older method based on historical loss ratios.
- **De-identification:** state, agencies, vendors, insurers, statutes and people tokenised; not fully feasible, as the setting is distinctive.

## Context (not episodes)
- A+0: an insurer, [INSURER-1], cited computer model results as the basis for cutting back coverage in storm-prone parts of [STATE-A]; rate hearings in this period focused on its actuarial analysis (S3).
- A+4: a national academy report noted that how far the state insurance department would approve model-based filings was still unsettled (S4).
- A+7: by this point the state commission had accepted five models; their average annual statewide loss estimates differed roughly threefold (S2).
- A+13: [VENDOR-3] released a model variant using a five-year forward-looking view of elevated storm frequency rather than the long-run historical average; insurers began using it in filings (S6, S7).
- A+13: one insurer's filing was reduced after its figures were checked against the state's public model (S11).
- A+18: the state commission declined to accept the state's own public model version after finding typographical and calculation errors (S13). (The public model is used by the regulator to review filings, not by insurers as [MODEL].)

## Episodes

E1. **[A+2] Legislative act of [STATE-A] ([STATUTE-1]).**
- Brought by: [STATE-A] legislature. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: a state body to set standards for hurricane loss projection methods used in rate filings.
- Outcome: enacted; created [AGENCY-1] (a state commission of actuaries, statisticians, a meteorologist, computer and insurance-finance specialists, the consumer advocate and others) to adopt standards and make findings on whether models are accurate or reliable. Insurers "may" use methods the commission finds acceptable; such findings are admissible in a rate proceeding only if the insurance regulator and the consumer advocate have access to all the assumptions and factors behind the model. Amended in A+3.
- Stated reasons: state policy to encourage the most actuarially sophisticated methods so that consumers are charged lawful rates.
- Relief applied to: all residential property insurers filing rates in [STATE-A].
- Sources: S1, S2, S8.

E2. **[A+3 to A+4] First model standards and acceptance reviews by [AGENCY-1].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1], on submissions by vendors. Funded or run by: the commission, with a professional team (actuary, statistician, meteorologist, computer scientist, engineer) hired by a state board that audits vendors on site.
- Sought: findings on whether each model met the commission's standards.
- Outcome: by the end of A+4 the three principal vendors' models were accepted; the commission continued to revise standards and run acceptance reviews in cycles thereafter.
- Stated reasons: compliance with published standards, verified by on-site audit.
- Relief applied to: insurers using accepted models.
- Sources: S2, S4, S5.

E3. **[A+7] State appropriation and contract for a public hurricane model.**
- Brought by: [STATE-A] legislature and the state insurance department ([AGENCY-2]). Funded or run by: state funds (about a million dollars in the first year, more requested the next); contract with a state university, [UNIV-1].
- Sought: a public, non-proprietary model so the regulator could check insurers' model-based filings.
- Outcome: funded and contracted; completion expected about three years later.
- Stated reasons: commercial models are proprietary and their inner workings not open to the regulator or the public; a public model was intended to ensure proper rate regulation.
- Relief applied to: the regulator's review of all residential filings.
- Sources: S2.

E4. **[A+8] Bill in the [STATE-A] legislature (house bill).**
- Brought by: two members of the state house. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: to require residential rate filings to rest on the average losses of the most recent 30 years, allowing models only to supplement that experience and only if the model was the public model or one whose assumptions, data and methods the regulator could inspect or challenge in closed session in any hearing.
- Outcome: passed the house insurance committee unanimously with an amendment; did not become law (no matching change appears in later versions of the statute).
- Stated reasons: model results varied widely and were proprietary; the regulator lacked access.
- Relief applied to: would have applied to all residential insurers.
- Sources: S2, S8.

E5. **[A+12] Legislative act of [STATE-A] ([STATUTE-2]).**
- Brought by: [STATE-A] legislature. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: to put the public model on a statutory footing and secure regulator access to model assumptions.
- Outcome: enacted; required insurers and rating organisations to supply loss and exposure data for the public model; required the public model to be submitted to [AGENCY-1]; allowed the regulator to use it in reviewing filings; required insurers to give the regulator and consumer advocate access to all assumptions and components of the model used in a filing.
- Stated reasons: regulator needed an independent check on proprietary model outputs.
- Relief applied to: all residential insurers and the regulator.
- Sources: S9, S10.

E6. **[A+14] Acceptance review of [VENDOR-3]'s short-term model by [AGENCY-1].**
- Brought by: [VENDOR-3] (submission for acceptance). Funded or run by: commission and its professional team.
- Sought: acceptance of a model version using a five-year elevated-frequency view of storm activity.
- Outcome: the professional team found the submission did not meet several standards that the vendor's long-term models had met; the vendor withdrew it and did not seek a second audit. A revised version using the long-run historical average of storms covering roughly the past century, as the standards require, was accepted later that year. Another version of the vendor's model was also pulled back for adjustment before review.
- Stated reasons: failure to meet commission standards; standards required the long-run historical storm record.
- Relief applied to: insurers that would have used the short-term version in filings.
- Sources: S6, S7.

E7. **[A+14] Rate hearing and disapproval by the state insurance regulator ([AGENCY-3]).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-3] on filings by [INSURER-1]'s group (five companies, increases of roughly 27% to 43%). Funded or run by: the regulator.
- Sought: review of the requested increases at a public hearing.
- Outcome: the regulator issued notices of intent to disapprove all the filings the day after the hearing; previous rates stayed in force; the insurer could seek an administrative hearing.
- Stated reasons: rates did not pass on savings from expanded state reinsurance; among other concerns, the hearing panel noted that the filings relied on short-term adjustments to the hurricane model that [AGENCY-1] had not accepted, and that modelling had not delivered the expected market stability.
- Relief applied to: the insurer group's policyholders.
- Sources: S12.

E8. **[A+14 to A+15] Regulator subpoena and licence suspension, with appeal to [COURT-1 (appellate)].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-3] (subpoenas and an immediate order suspending the insurer's licences for new business); [INSURER-1] appealed. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: the regulator sought documents including the insurer's communications with catastrophe modelling firms, rating organisations and trade associations; the insurer sought to block the suspension.
- Outcome: the appellate court stayed the suspension, then held that the commissioner had not exceeded his authority; rehearing denied and suspension took effect; the commissioner then stayed the suspension on condition of continued cooperation. The regulator later filed an administrative complaint alleging, among other things, failure to produce documents and witnesses, false trade-secret labelling and false certification of rate filings. Final outcome of the complaint not reported.
- Stated reasons: the insurer said its communications were protected by state and federal law; the regulator said the documents were needed to review its filings.
- Relief applied to: [INSURER-1]'s group in [STATE-A].
- Sources: S14, S15.

E9. **[A+15] Legislative act of [STATE-A] ([STATUTE-3]).**
- Brought by: [STATE-A] legislature. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: to tie rate filings to commission-accepted models.
- Outcome: enacted; insurers must use methods and models found accurate or reliable by [AGENCY-1] for the hurricane loss component of residential filings and may not modify or adjust their outputs (with a timing rule for probable maximum loss figures).
- Stated reasons: not reported in sources reviewed.
- Relief applied to: all residential insurers in [STATE-A].
- Sources: S8.

E10. **[A+15 to A+16] Rate disapproval and administrative hearing, [INSURER-2].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-3] (disapproval); [INSURER-2] petitioned for a hearing before [COURT-2] (state administrative hearings division). Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: the insurer sought an average increase of about 47% (its indicated need was about 67%).
- Outcome: regulator disapproved, giving over twenty reasons, two of which questioned the insurer's catastrophe-modelling method of averaging the outputs of three accepted models; the commissioner first declined a formal hearing, but an administrative law judge later issued a recommended order denying both the requested and indicated increases. About a year later the parties settled: an increase of roughly 15% and permission for the insurer to drop its most exposed policyholders over 18 months (later tens of thousands of non-renewals).
- Stated reasons: the regulator's counsel argued that averaging three models did not produce a reliable answer; the judge found the insurer had not shown by a preponderance of evidence that the rates were not excessive, inadequate or unfairly discriminatory.
- Relief applied to: [INSURER-2]'s policyholders in [STATE-A].
- Sources: S11, S16.

E11. **[A+16] Acceptance review of [VENDOR-3]'s model by [AGENCY-1].**
- Brought by: [VENDOR-3]. Funded or run by: the commission.
- Sought: acceptance of a new model version.
- Outcome: not accepted, because the commissioners present split evenly on a meteorological question bearing on two of several dozen standards; the vendor requested reconsideration. Outcome of that request not reported in sources reviewed.
- Stated reasons: the disputed meteorological treatment (how upper-level winds translate to surface winds and losses).
- Relief applied to: insurers that would use that version.
- Sources: S17.

E12. **[A+17] Suspension of acceptance of [VENDOR-1]'s model by [AGENCY-1].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1] at an emergency meeting. Funded or run by: the commission.
- Sought: to suspend an already-accepted model version.
- Outcome: acceptance suspended; professional team to audit the vendor's corrections on site before an expedited further meeting. Later outcome not reported in sources reviewed.
- Stated reasons: a software implementation error was found in the accepted version.
- Relief applied to: insurers relying on that version in filings.
- Sources: S18.
