# Evidence packet G01

- **Domain:** criminal sentencing; correctional risk assessment (also used in parole and probation decisions)
- **What the model does:** a proprietary questionnaire-and-records tool that gives each defendant or offender decile scores for pretrial misconduct risk, general recidivism risk and violent recidivism risk, plus a profile of "criminogenic needs"; the scores are attached to presentence reports read by sentencing judges and are used for supervision, programme placement and parole.
- **Scale:** tens of thousands of people assessed per year in [STATE-A]; the same tool is used by corrections and courts in several other states.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = [OPERATOR] (the state corrections department of [STATE-A]) adopts [MODEL], licensed from [VENDOR], as its statewide risk-and-needs assessment, including for presentence investigation reports.
- **De-identification:** names of model, vendor, operator, states, courts, agencies and parties tokenised; not fully feasible (the leading court decision is widely known).

## Context (not episodes)
- A−1: [AGENCY-2], the state auditor of [STATE-C], at the request of a legislative audit committee, reported that the benefits of [STATE-C]'s use of [MODEL] in prisons were uncertain and recommended suspending it until the state could show a way to measure its effect on recidivism. Follow-up status reviews ran to A+4 (S7). This predates A+0.
- A+0: when the tool was sold to [OPERATOR], the vendor's documentation said the tool would be normed or validated on [STATE-A]'s own population; by A+4 this had not been done (S1, S6).
- A+1 to A+2: a series of [STATE-B] intermediate appellate decisions ordered new parole hearings because the parole board had not used the [MODEL] risk-and-needs instrument that an amended state statute required it to use. These suits sought the model's use, not relief against it (S9).
- A+4: [ORG-1], a non-profit newsroom, published its own analysis of [MODEL] scores from a county in another state, reporting that people of one race who did not reoffend were scored high-risk at roughly twice the rate of people of another race; [VENDOR] published a rebuttal (S10).
- A+4: an article in a peer-reviewed federal court-system journal re-analysed the same data and disputed the newsroom's conclusion of racial bias (S11).
- A+6: a peer-reviewed study reported that untrained online participants predicted recidivism about as accurately as [MODEL] on the same data, and that a two-variable model matched it (S11).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+0] Official validation study by a state criminal-justice agency ([AGENCY-1], [STATE-B]).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1], the state's criminal-justice services agency. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-1] research office.
- Sought: to test whether the probation version of the [MODEL] recidivism scale predicted two-year rearrest among people on probation in [STATE-B].
- Outcome: report found the scale predicted rearrest with accuracy it described as satisfactory (area under the curve about 0.7); rearrest rates rose steadily with each decile score; the agency later relied on the study for continued use.
- Stated reasons: observed rearrest rates tracked expected rates across score levels for the overall probation population.
- Relief applied to: none (no relief sought); informed the agency's continued use statewide.
- Sources: S5.

E2. **[A+2] Criminal appeal, state intermediate appellate court ([COURT-2 (appellate)], [STATE-A]), published decision.**
- Brought by: [APPELLANT-1], a defendant convicted of a sexual assault offence, appealing the denial of his postconviction motion. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: resentencing or sentence modification, arguing the sentencing judge misused the [MODEL] report.
- Outcome: judgment and postconviction order affirmed.
- Stated reasons: the court held the sentencing judge did not misuse its discretion when it treated the "criminogenic needs" section of the [MODEL] report as a sign of danger to the community, even though the recidivism section rated the defendant's risk as low. The defendant did not raise a due-process objection to the tool itself.
- Relief applied to: none; affirmance of this defendant's sentence. The decision was later cited as precedent approving a [MODEL]-informed sentence.
- Sources: S3, S2.

E3. **[A+3 to A+5] Criminal appeal, certified by [COURT-2 (appellate)] to the state supreme court ([COURT-3], [STATE-A]); petition to the national supreme court ([COURT-4]) denied.**
- Brought by: [APPELLANT-2], a defendant who pleaded guilty to two lesser driving-related offences linked to a shooting incident and was sentenced to several years in prison; his presentence report included a [MODEL] assessment rating him high risk on all three scales. Funded or run by: not reported. At the national stage the federal government's chief appellate lawyer filed a brief at the court's invitation.
- Sought: resentencing, on the grounds that use of [MODEL] at sentencing breached due process because (a) its proprietary method prevented him from checking the accuracy of the score, (b) it rested on group data rather than an individual sentence, and (c) it took gender into account.
- Outcome: at A+3 the intermediate court certified the questions to [COURT-3]. At A+4 [COURT-3] unanimously affirmed (two concurring opinions). It held that a sentencing court may consider [MODEL] scores without breaching due process if it observes stated limits: scores may not be used to decide whether to imprison someone or how severe the sentence is, nor be the determinative factor, and any presentence report containing a [MODEL] score must carry a written advisement warning judges that (1) the method is proprietary so the weighting of factors is not disclosed; (2) the scores come from group data and identify groups of high-risk offenders, not a particular high-risk individual; (3) some studies have questioned whether the scores disproportionately place minority offenders in higher-risk categories; (4) the tool was normed on a national sample and no validation study for [STATE-A]'s population had been completed; (5) the tool was designed for post-sentencing decisions about treatment, supervision and parole. At A+5 [COURT-4] declined to hear the case; the federal government's brief had recommended denial, saying the questions might merit review in a future case but this case was a poor vehicle because the sentencing judge would have imposed the same sentence without the score.
- Stated reasons: the defendant could check the accuracy of the questionnaire answers and public criminal history that feed the score, even if not the weighting; the sentencing judge had relied on other independent factors, so the score was not determinative; using gender was found to improve the tool's accuracy and the defendant did not show the judge relied on gender. One concurrence stressed that courts may consider but not rely on the score; the other urged courts to explain on the record how they used it.
- Relief applied to: the defendant's sentence was upheld; the written-advisement requirement and the limits on use apply to all future sentencings in [STATE-A] that use [MODEL].
- Sources: S1, S2, S4, S6.

E4. **[A+7, with follow-up status reviews to A+11] State audit ([AGENCY-2], [STATE-C]) of the prison system's rehabilitation practices.**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-2], the state auditor of [STATE-C]. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-2].
- Sought: review of the state corrections department's assessment and rehabilitation-programme practices, including its use of [MODEL].
- Outcome: the auditor recommended that the department validate [MODEL] (and its other risk tool) by A+8 and revalidate all assessment tools at least every five years. Follow-up reviews to A+11 rated the recommendation only partially implemented: the other tool had been validated, [MODEL] had not, with revalidation repeatedly deferred.
- Stated reasons: the department needed reliable tools for assessing its prison population and had not validated [MODEL] on it.
- Relief applied to: recommendation to the [STATE-C] corrections department; no change ordered for individuals.
- Sources: S8.
