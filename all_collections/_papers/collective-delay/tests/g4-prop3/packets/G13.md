# Evidence packet G13

- **Domain:** school examination grading (national qualifications awarded without exams)
- **What the model does:** a statistical standardisation model that took teacher-estimated grades and teacher rank orders for each student and adjusted them so that each school's grade distribution matched a prediction based mainly on that school's own past results; the output was the grade awarded to each student.
- **Scale:** hundreds of thousands of students in one national results round.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = the government decision, after exams were cancelled, that grades in that year would be awarded by teacher estimates standardised through [MODEL], operated by the qualifications regulator [OPERATOR]. Most events below fall within the same year, so fractional years are used (results day is roughly A+0.35).
- **De-identification:** model, regulator, ministry, minister, firms, campaigners, committees, statutes and places tokenised; not fully feasible, as the case is widely known.

## Context (not episodes)
- [A+0] The education minister of [COUNTRY-A] directed [OPERATOR] to develop a standardisation approach after exams were cancelled; [OPERATOR] has said it had favoured holding socially distanced or delayed exams instead (S12).
- [A+0.3] A neighbouring jurisdiction, [REGION-B], issued grades under its own statistical moderation a few days before [COUNTRY-A]'s results; after public outcry its government apologised and restored teacher estimates within about a week. This is a parallel case, not part of [MODEL] (S9).
- [A+0.35] Results day: large numbers of students received grades below their teacher estimates; street protests and a petition with hundreds of thousands of signatures followed (S1, S9).
- [A+0.35] The data protection regulator [AGENCY-3] issued a public statement noting [OPERATOR]'s position that no solely automated decision-making took place, and directing concerned people to the exam boards first. No investigation or decision is reported (S19).
- [A+0.35] The minister publicly stated two days after results that the system would stay unchanged (S9).
- [A+0.4] The head of [OPERATOR] resigned, followed by the ministry's most senior civil servant (S9).
- [A+0.7] [OPERATOR] published the code used to calculate grades (S9).
- [A+0.7] The chair of [OPERATOR] announced he would step down, citing the events of that summer (S12).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+0.3 to A+0.45] Parliamentary select committee inquiry ([COMMITTEE-1]).**
- Brought by: [COMMITTEE-1], a standing committee of the national legislature, as part of a wider inquiry into the effects of the pandemic on education. Funded or run by: the legislature.
- Sought: scrutiny of [OPERATOR]'s and the ministry's decisions on cancelled exams and calculated grades, tested against fairness, transparency and accessibility.
- Outcome: Before results, the committee issued a report (about A+0.3) calling on [OPERATOR] to publish the standardisation model at once, warning that anchoring grades to a school's past results might be unfair to improving schools, and warning of a risk of bias against already disadvantaged groups, ethnic-minority students, children in care and students with special educational needs. It recommended published evidence thresholds for appeals and a free advice and appeals helpline. [OPERATOR] did not publish the model until results day. After results, the committee took oral evidence from [OPERATOR] (about A+0.4) and from the minister (about A+0.45). At the first hearing the regulator's chair apologised, called the belief that such a model could ever be acceptable to the public a fundamental mistake, and said the minister had decided to cancel exams and had asked for the regulator's published mock-exam appeal guidance to be withdrawn. At the second hearing the minister suggested he had first examined the model in detail only once results were published and declined to say whether he was satisfied with the advice he had received.
- Stated reasons: the committee said that without support it would be almost impossible for an individual student to prove bias or discrimination through the appeal route, and that the model's reliance on historic school data put some schools and groups at risk.
- Relief applied to: no binding relief; recommendations addressed to the regulator and ministry.
- Sources: S10, S11, S12, S13, S14, S15.

E2. **[A+0.35] Regulator decision on appeal route ([OPERATOR] board).**
- Brought by: [OPERATOR] itself. Funded or run by: [OPERATOR]; the regulator's chair later told [COMMITTEE-1] the withdrawal was made at the minister's request.
- Sought: to set out when a student's mock-exam result could be used to appeal a grade produced by [MODEL] (an appeal route the minister had announced days earlier).
- Outcome: [OPERATOR] published eight criteria for mock-exam appeals (under which a mock result above the teacher estimate would yield only the teacher estimate), then withdrew them within hours, stating only that its board was reviewing the policy. The guidance was overtaken two days later by E5.
- Stated reasons: none given at the time beyond the board review; the published criteria were reported as narrower than the minister's earlier assurances.
- Relief applied to: all students awarded grades by [MODEL] (the appeal route that would have applied to them).
- Sources: S7, S8, S12.

E3. **[A+0.35] Threatened judicial review in [COURT-1] (pre-action letter), student and campaign group.**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-1], a student awaiting results, through the law firm [ORG-2] and the technology-justice group [ORG-1]. Funded or run by: [ORG-1] and [ORG-2] ran the challenge with barristers instructed; a public campaign and petition ran alongside. Funding not reported beyond donations sought by [ORG-1].
- Sought: a pre-action letter (to [OPERATOR], about A+0.35) argued that the model went beyond the regulator's statutory objectives under [STATUTE-1], produced irrational, arbitrary and unfair results, and breached data protection law ([STATUTE-2]) including the rules on profiling and automated decision-making; it asked that students be graded on their own merits rather than their school's history, with more weight to teacher grades and a free merits-based appeal.
- Outcome: no claim was issued. Three days after the letter, the government and [OPERATOR] replaced model grades with the higher of teacher-estimated and model grades (E5). Counsel later reported that the reversal came less than a day before the claim was due to be filed. No court ruling.
- Stated reasons: as above (grounds of the letter); no ruling.
- Relief applied to: the change in E5 applied to all affected students; no relief specific to the claimant.
- Sources: S1, S2, S3, S20.

E4. **[A+0.35] Threatened judicial review in [COURT-1] (pre-action letter), group of students backed by a litigation charity.**
- Brought by: several students (reported as six) who had been downgraded by at least two grades from their teacher estimates, with the campaigning legal organisation [ORG-3]. Funded or run by: [ORG-3], via public crowdfunding (tens of thousands in local currency pledged).
- Sought: a review and revision of the scheme, proposing either a merits-based appeal right for all downgraded students or a cap so that no grade could be lowered by more than one grade. Grounds: unfairness at system level in how grades were set and appealed, breach of natural justice, irrationality, breach of the regulator's statutory duties, and breach of equality law ([STATUTE-3]), including likely discrimination against disabled students.
- Outcome: no court ruling is reported; the challenge was overtaken by E5, announced the day the claim was publicised. Formal withdrawal and any costs outcome not reported.
- Stated reasons: as above (grounds of the claim).
- Relief applied to: the change in E5 applied to all affected students; no relief specific to the claimants.
- Sources: S4, S5, S6.

E5. **[A+0.37] Executive and regulatory decision by the minister and [OPERATOR].**
- Brought by: the minister of [AGENCY-1] and [OPERATOR], jointly. Funded or run by: government.
- Sought: to replace grades issued under [MODEL].
- Outcome: students in both the upper-secondary and lower-secondary qualifications received the higher of their teacher-estimated grade and their model grade; upper-secondary grades already issued were reissued, and lower-secondary results days later were issued on the same basis. Vocational qualification grades from a major awarding body were withdrawn and re-graded in the following days.
- Stated reasons: the minister later cited the evidence presented after results showing the model's outcomes; the regulator's chair said the level of fairness achieved was not felt to be acceptable to the public.
- Relief applied to: all students awarded grades that year in the affected qualifications in [COUNTRY-A].
- Sources: S1, S9, S12, S15.

E6. **[A+0.95] Official review by the statistics regulator ([AGENCY-2]).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-2], the national statistics regulator, on its own initiative (commissioning body not stated). Funded or run by: [AGENCY-2].
- Sought: a review of how the models for awarding grades were developed in [COUNTRY-A] and three other jurisdictions, against the national statistics code (trustworthiness, quality, value), covering the process from ministerial direction to appeals. It expressly did not rule on individual outcomes or on government policy choices.
- Outcome: report published. Findings on the four approaches together: full method details were not released in advance; limits and uncertainty of the models were not fully communicated; the method was not exposed to the widest range of experts and had limited professional consensus; acceptability testing focused on the calculation rather than its effect on individuals; there was extensive checking at aggregate level but little individual-level human review before results; equality analyses showed gaps did not widen, but a public perception that lower-income students were disadvantaged drove dissatisfaction. Recommendations: joined-up leadership across government analysis and digital functions; a guidance directory for bodies using such models; guidance on testing public acceptability; clear accountability between the commissioning minister and the developing officials; consulting the national statistician for high-value advanced models. It warned that public bodies might become less willing to use statistical models for fear of a public backlash.
- Stated reasons: public confidence in a model depends on transparency, communication and acceptability across the whole process, not only on technical design.
- Relief applied to: none binding; recommendations to government bodies generally.
- Sources: S16, S17.

E7. **[A+5.5] National public inquiry hearings ([INQUIRY-1]).**
- Brought by: [INQUIRY-1], a statutory public inquiry into the pandemic response, within its investigation of effects on children and young people (exam grading was one topic among many). Funded or run by: government-established inquiry.
- Sought: evidence on decisions affecting children and young people, including the grading approach.
- Outcome: the former head of government gave evidence; a contemporaneous message was shown in which he described his anger over the results and said he had considered dismissing the education minister. He accepted the system had failed and that the government had the wrong initial model, while saying it was well intended and arose from exceptionally difficult circumstances. No report findings on this topic are reported by the end of the window.
- Stated reasons: not reported (no findings yet).
- Relief applied to: none.
- Sources: S9, S18.
