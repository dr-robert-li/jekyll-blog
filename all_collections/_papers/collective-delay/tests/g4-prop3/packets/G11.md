# Evidence packet G11

- **Domain:** welfare benefits (childcare and housing allowances) fraud and error detection; recovery of allowance debts
- **What the model does:** a self-learning risk-scoring model run each month by the tax authority's benefits department ([OPERATOR]) before allowance payments; it weighted indicators (including, for some years, nationality, plus household make-up, income and others) to give each new application or change a risk score and selected the highest-scoring ones for manual handling. The scores were also used more widely in fraud investigations. The model made no automatic decision on entitlement; staff did. Related practices by the same body (a fraud-signal register, all-or-nothing recovery, investigation teams) form part of the record.
- **Scale:** tens of thousands of parents later registered as harmed; registrations for redress numbered in the tens of thousands.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = the benefits department introduced the risk-scoring model for childcare and housing allowance applications, as one of a set of measures against systematic allowance fraud.
- **De-identification:** names of the model, bodies, courts, committees, statutes, firms and people are tokenised; not fully feasible, as the case is widely known.

## Context (not episodes)
- A+0 to A+6: the highest administrative court ([COURT-1]) followed, in its rulings on individual appeals, the benefits department's line that if a parent could not show payment of all childcare costs for a year, the whole year's allowance could be recovered (S5).
- A+1: the benefits department ended the allowance of about two hundred families who used one childcare agency, after an investigation by its fraud team (S3).
- A+4 to A+7: media investigations and questions by individual members of parliament drew attention to the treatment of parents (S8).
- A+6.6 to A+7.2: the benefits department stopped selecting with the model at A+6.9 (per S1) and later stopped it entirely (see E7).
- A+7.4: the junior finance ministers themselves filed a criminal complaint against the benefits department alleging extortion by officials and discrimination in office (S12).
- A+12: the deadline for claims for additional damages under the redress scheme was extended to the start of A+13 (S19).

## Episodes

E1. **[A-3 to A+6.8] Individual appeals against childcare allowance decisions in district administrative courts and on further appeal to the highest administrative court ([COURT-1]) (aggregated).**
- Brought by: individual parents. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: annulment of decisions stopping allowances or recovering advance payments, often for a whole year.
- Outcome: over the ten years ending A+6, district courts received more than ten thousand childcare allowance cases and gave appealable judgments in several thousand; thousands of these went on appeal to [COURT-1], which upheld the district court in most appeals and set it aside in about a fifth. Most district judges followed [COURT-1]'s all-or-nothing reading; a small minority, mainly at two district courts, departed from it or sought proportionate outcomes, but those judgments were set aside when appealed, and from A+2 departures almost ceased until E3. The district-court judgments do not show that the cases arose from the fraud teams' group investigations.
- Stated reasons: [COURT-1] held that a parent who could not prove payment of all childcare costs had no right to any allowance for that year, so the whole advance could be recovered.
- Relief applied to: individual appellants, case by case.
- Sources: S5, S6, S15.

E2. **[A+4.6] Report of the national ombudsman ([OMBUDS-1]) on the ending of allowances for about two hundred families.**
- Brought by: [OMBUDS-1], after complaints by parents. Funded or run by: [OMBUDS-1].
- Sought: investigation of the benefits department's conduct.
- Outcome: the ombudsman found that ending the allowances of the whole group was a disproportionate reaction to a signal of possible irregularity and that many parents fell into serious financial trouble; four recommendations, including re-checking rejected objections against the court's case law. The junior finance minister accepted that the balance had tipped too far towards fighting abuse.
- Stated reasons: the department acted on a group signal without enough regard for the individual parent's position.
- Relief applied to: the families of that agency.
- Sources: S3, S4.

E3. **[A+6.8] Two rulings of the highest administrative court ([COURT-1]) on all-or-nothing recovery.**
- Brought by: individual parents (appellants). Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: annulment of decisions recovering the whole year's childcare allowance.
- Outcome: the court abandoned the all-or-nothing line; the allowance is to be set in proportion to costs actually and timely paid. The government later extended revision to final decisions taken before the rulings.
- Stated reasons: the statute leaves room for a proportionate setting of the allowance; full recovery for any shortfall was not required.
- Relief applied to: the appellants, and by subsequent government policy, parents with earlier final decisions.
- Sources: S5, S6.

E4. **[A+6.9 to A+7.2] Government-appointed advisory committee on the implementation of allowances ([COMMITTEE-1]), interim and final advice.**
- Brought by: the government (finance ministry). Funded or run by: the government.
- Sought: advice on the handling of the affected parents and on the benefits department's implementation.
- Outcome: interim advice at A+6.9 and final advice at A+7.2; recommended compensation for parents treated unfairly, a review of further case files from A+1 to A+3, and earlier detection of errors.
- Stated reasons: the committee found that parents had been treated with unwarranted severity and that past decisions needed redress.
- Relief applied to: parents within the committee's scope (initially the agency group in E2, then broader).
- Sources: S7.

E5. **[A+7.2] Review by the central government audit service ([AUDITOR-1]) of fraud-team cases involving allowances.**
- Brought by: the tax authority, which asked [AUDITOR-1] to check whether other fraud-team cases since A+0 had been handled like the case in E2. Funded or run by: [AUDITOR-1].
- Sought: whether comparable handling occurred in other allowance-related fraud-team cases.
- Outcome: report delivered to the head of the benefits department; it recorded that allowances of over a thousand people had been stopped on the basis of findings at intermediaries before any investigation of the person, and of several thousand more after investigation; it also established that dual nationality had been used as a criterion for enforcement action. A parliamentary committee discussed the report with the audit service.
- Stated reasons: as found.
- Relief applied to: not applicable directly; its findings formed the basis for a later external re-assessment of these cases.
- Sources: S15, S23.

E6. **[A+7.5 to A+8.9] Data-protection authority ([AGENCY-1]) investigation and fine on the use of nationality.**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1], on its own investigation. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-1].
- Sought: determination whether the processing of applicants' nationality complied with data-protection law.
- Outcome: investigation report at A+7.5 found that using nationality as an indicator in the risk-scoring model (from A+3 to A+5) was unlawful and discriminatory and that dual-nationality data had been kept long after it should have been deleted; at A+8.9 the authority fined the finance minister a sum in the low millions in local currency. The minister did not contest the fine (reported).
- Stated reasons: the nationality data were not necessary for the purpose, less intrusive means were available, and processing them in the model was discriminatory and so unfair; dual-nationality data of over a million people were retained unlawfully.
- Relief applied to: all applicants (finding and fine against the operator; no individual relief).
- Sources: S1, S8, S9.

E7. **[A+7.5] Benefits department management decision to stop the model permanently (executive act).**
- Brought by: the management team of [OPERATOR]. Funded or run by: not applicable.
- Sought: not applicable.
- Outcome: use of the risk-scoring model was permanently stopped.
- Stated reasons: the model did not meet the requirements of the regional data-protection regulation.
- Relief applied to: all future applicants.
- Sources: S1.

E8. **[A+7.9] Parliamentary interrogation committee ([COMMITTEE-2]) report on childcare allowance.**
- Brought by: the lower house of parliament. Funded or run by: parliament.
- Sought: inquiry into how the problems in implementing the childcare allowance arose.
- Outcome: report found that basic rule-of-law principles had been violated, that tens of thousands of parents had suffered grave injustice, and that it resulted from a series of tightened fraud measures; the cabinet called it a dark chapter in the history of government and announced apologies and measures.
- Stated reasons: legislator, government, implementing body and courts each failed to protect parents; strict fraud policy and all-or-nothing recovery were applied without proportionality.
- Relief applied to: not applicable directly (recommendations).
- Sources: S10, S11.

E9. **[A+7.9 to A+8.4] Cabinet redress decision: flat-rate payment to harmed parents (executive act).**
- Brought by: the cabinet. Funded or run by: the state.
- Sought: not applicable.
- Outcome: the cabinet decided to pay a flat sum of tens of thousands in local currency to every parent found harmed, formalised by ministerial decrees at A+8.2 and A+8.4, on top of an earlier compensation scheme.
- Stated reasons: to do justice to parents faster.
- Relief applied to: all parents who applied and were found to have been harmed.
- Sources: S13.

E10. **[A+8] Resignation of the cabinet (executive act).**
- Brought by: the prime minister on behalf of the cabinet. Funded or run by: not applicable.
- Sought: not applicable.
- Outcome: the cabinet offered its resignation to the head of state and continued as a caretaker government.
- Stated reasons: the analysis in the report of E8 was so serious that the cabinet drew the most far-reaching political conclusion.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S11.

E11. **[A+8 to A+8.2] Criminal complaint against former and serving ministers; advice of the prosecutor-general at the supreme court ([PROSECUTOR-2]).**
- Brought by: about twenty parents, later joined by several dozen more, through a lawyer. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: a criminal investigation of ministers and junior ministers (including the prime minister) for offences in office.
- Outcome: [PROSECUTOR-2] advised against a criminal investigation; no order to prosecute was reported.
- Stated reasons: the problems flowed mainly from recovery rules set by legislation that put the burden on applicants and lacked a hardship clause and proportionality test, not from chargeable offences by office holders; the complaint relied heavily on parliamentary hearings that cannot serve as evidence; political accountability comes first.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S14.

E12. **[A+8 to A+12] Individual applications and objections under the redress scheme (aggregated).**
- Brought by: individual parents. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: recognition as harmed parent and compensation; objections to decisions went to an objections advisory committee.
- Outcome: by the registration deadline at the end of A+10, tens of thousands of parents had registered; by A+11 a first test had been done for nearly all; the advisory committee issued many published opinions on objections. Totals of objections and court appeals are not reported in the sources consulted.
- Stated reasons: per individual case.
- Relief applied to: individual parents.
- Sources: S13, S19.

E13. **[A+8.8] Self-review by a working group of district administrative judges ([JUDICIAL-COUNCIL-1]).**
- Brought by: a national consultative body of district administrative judges ([JUDICIAL-COUNCIL-1]), which commissioned a working group of judges with two external academic advisers. Funded or run by: the judiciary.
- Sought: review of how the injustice could occur under the oversight of administrative judges at district level.
- Outcome: report found that most district administrative judges had followed the strict all-or-nothing reading of [COURT-1], seeing little room for a less strict approach even when they thought the result disproportionate; recommended that judges actively examine the facts and concrete consequences for the citizen and give more weight to individual legal protection than to uniformity where outcomes are disproportionate.
- Stated reasons: judges expected the tax authority to appeal and win, so did not want to give parents false hope, and valued uniform application of the law; the working group found this hampered protection in individual cases and reduced signals of disproportionate outcomes reaching the appeal court. It also noted that allowances of over a thousand people had been stopped on the basis of findings at intermediaries before they themselves were investigated, and that this did not visibly reach the courts.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S15.

E14. **[A+8.8] Opinion of a regional constitutional-law advisory body ([INTL-1]) on legal protection of citizens.**
- Brought by: request of the lower house of parliament following a motion by a member. Funded or run by: [INTL-1].
- Sought: assessment of legal protection of citizens and checks and balances in light of the childcare allowance case.
- Outcome: the body found the shortcomings in protection of individual rights in this case serious and systemic, while concluding that rule-of-law mechanisms ultimately worked; thirteen recommendations, including better legislative drafting, room for dissent in parliament and the civil service, and a stronger position for citizens; it also raised constitutional review of statutes.
- Stated reasons: as above.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S16.

E15. **[A+8.9] Self-review by the highest administrative court ([COURT-1]).**
- Brought by: [COURT-1]. Funded or run by: [COURT-1].
- Sought: review of its own case law on childcare allowance.
- Outcome: the court stated it regretted returning too late from its strict line, which it had followed for several years before E3, and that it should have offered parents better legal protection.
- Stated reasons: it had accepted the benefits department's all-or-nothing line without enough attention to proportionality.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S5, S16.

E16. **[A+9.3] Data-protection authority ([AGENCY-1]) fine concerning the tax authority's fraud-signal register ([SYSTEM-2]).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1]. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-1].
- Sought: determination whether the register complied with data-protection law.
- Outcome: a fine of a few million in local currency, made up of six separate penalties; the register had been switched off at A+7.
- Stated reasons: no legal basis; purpose not specified in advance; incorrect and outdated data; signals kept too long; poor security; late involvement of the internal data-protection officer. Persons could be listed on minimal grounds, including anonymous tips. The authority weighed the seriousness, the large numbers affected, the long duration and earlier violations.
- Relief applied to: all persons listed (finding and fine against the operator).
- Sources: S17.

E17. **[A+9.3] Defence challenge in a criminal fraud prosecution before a district court ([COURT-2]).**
- Brought by: defence counsel for accused persons in an allowance-fraud prosecution. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: a finding that the right to a fair trial was breached because it was unclear why an applicant was selected and whether discriminatory algorithms were used; exclusion of evidence leading to acquittal; further inquiry into the selection.
- Outcome: rejected; the accused were convicted.
- Stated reasons: the applicant held only domestic nationality, so the nationality indicator added no risk; other indicators (single parent, low income, debts, many childcare hours claimed) plausibly explained the selection; no sign in the file that name or appearance played a role.
- Relief applied to: not applicable (relief refused).
- Sources: S18.

E18. **[A+9.3] Government analysis of the risk-scoring model at parliament's request.**
- Brought by: the junior finance minister, following publication of the model at parliament's request. Funded or run by: the finance ministry.
- Sought: analysis of how the model worked and was used.
- Outcome: found that an age-related identifier indicator also had a discriminatory effect, so that those selected for manual handling were often single, on low incomes and without the national nationality; and that risk scores had been used more widely than reported, including in joint investigation teams and the department's own fraud investigations, contrary to an earlier letter.
- Stated reasons: as found.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S1, S8.

E19. **[A+9.5] Complaint to an appellate court ([COURT-3]) against the decision not to prosecute the tax authority.**
- Brought by: a large number of parents, using the statutory complaint route against non-prosecution. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: an order to prosecute the tax authority for discrimination in office, extortion by officials and coercion through abuse of authority.
- Outcome: refused; no prosecution.
- Stated reasons: the court found a reasonable suspicion of discrimination in office, but the tax authority, as part of the State, is immune from criminal prosecution; for the other offences there were insufficient grounds; discrimination before A+3 was time-barred.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S12, S20.

E20. **[A+9.8] Statute on the allowance redress operation ([STATUTE-1]) (legislative act).**
- Brought by: the government; enacted by parliament. Funded or run by: the state.
- Sought: not applicable.
- Outcome: entered into force at A+9.8, with retroactive effect to A+8; replaced the earlier compensation scheme and decrees and provided in law for the flat-rate payment, a first quick test of harm and a full assessment.
- Stated reasons: legal basis for redress of harmed parents.
- Relief applied to: parents who registered and were found harmed.
- Sources: S13, S19.

E21. **[A+10.5] Report by an external audit firm ([FIRM-1]) on use of the model's risk scores.**
- Brought by: the finance ministry (commissioning). Funded or run by: the ministry.
- Sought: whether use of the risk scores for joint investigation teams led to corrections or stopped allowances.
- Outcome: found that use of the model and scores for those teams did not directly lead to corrections or stopped allowances.
- Stated reasons: as found.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S1, S21.

E22. **[A+11.1] Parliamentary inquiry committee on fraud policy and services ([COMMITTEE-3]).**
- Brought by: the lower house of parliament. Funded or run by: parliament.
- Sought: inquiry into how government fights fraud and how public bodies used discriminatory risk profiles.
- Outcome: report concluded that all three branches of state had failed to see both the people affected and the law; for the model, it found an inadequate technical set-up, indicators lacking statistical relevance, no correction for bias, breaches of the rights to equal treatment and private life, and spread of unlawfully processed data and risk scores, leading to unequal treatment and stigmatisation. Nineteen recommendations, including abolishing the allowance system.
- Stated reasons: as found.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S1, S22.

E23. **[A+11.1] Complaint to an appellate court ([COURT-3]) against non-prosecution of individual tax officials.**
- Brought by: parents, through a lawyer, after the regional prosecutor decided not to prosecute unnamed officials. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: an order to prosecute individual officials for discrimination in office, alleged to have entered ethnicity and nationality as fraud signals by hand.
- Outcome: refused.
- Stated reasons: the offences that could be charged were time-barred; the file gave insufficient leads for a successful prosecution of individual officials; the investigation needed would be out of proportion.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S20.

E24. **[A+11.9] Benefits service study of the effects of manual handling after selection by the model.**
- Brought by: the benefits service (successor of [OPERATOR]), after parliamentary requests; an external supervisory committee of two professors validated the design. Funded or run by: the finance ministry.
- Sought: whether manual handling of applications selected by the model was harsher than other checks, and whether it caused later harm.
- Outcome: found no indication that handling after selection by the model was harsher than in other checks; outcomes closely resembled comparison groups, sometimes more favourable; selected applicants were more often flagged as handled differently, rarely with follow-up; other possible harms outside the service's work could not be assessed. Results to be weighed with earlier reports in deciding on any compensation for use of the model.
- Stated reasons: as found.
- Relief applied to: not applicable (decision on compensation pending).
- Sources: S1.
