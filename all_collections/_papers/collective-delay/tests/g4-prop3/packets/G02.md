# Evidence packet G02

- **Domain:** teacher evaluation; teacher performance pay and dismissal in a public school district
- **What the model does:** a proprietary statistical value-added model that uses students' standardised test results to estimate each teacher's contribution to student progress; the scores fed teacher bonuses, then the district's teacher appraisal and decisions not to renew or to terminate teacher contracts.
- **Scale:** thousands of teachers (several thousand teachers in tested grades and subjects had individual scores).
- **Adoption:** A+0 = [OPERATOR], a large urban school district in [STATE-A], begins district-wide use of [MODEL], supplied by [VENDOR], to score teachers (first for a performance-pay programme; extended to appraisal and dismissal from A+3).
- **De-identification:** names of model, vendor, district, union, state, courts and people tokenised; not fully feasible (the federal ruling is widely known).

## Context (not episodes)
- A−1 to A+0: [OPERATOR]'s board approved, then re-adopted, a teacher performance-pay programme using value-added data; bonuses were paid each year from A+1 (S5, S6).
- A+3: [OPERATOR]'s board voted to add student value-added progress as a listed ground for not renewing a teacher's contract and allowed the data to be used in terminations alongside other factors; two teachers' unions opposed it, and critics noted the formula was shielded from public-records requests as proprietary (S7).
- A+3: at a [STATE-A] senate education committee hearing a state senator questioned district officials about the transparency of the scores; no formal finding or action followed (S8).
- A+4 to A+5: [OPERATOR] signed a new licence with [VENDOR] and built the scores into its teacher appraisal system, announcing a goal of dismissing most teachers rated ineffective (S1).
- A+5: a peer-reviewed study by university researchers described teachers in the district who had been dismissed partly on value-added scores and criticised the model's reliability (S10).
- A+9: [OPERATOR] had been receiving federal grants that required value-added measures in teacher evaluation (S9).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+7 to A+10] Federal civil-rights lawsuit, federal trial court ([COURT-1]); resolved by settlement.**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-1], the local teachers' union, together with several individual teachers (six or seven at filing; nine current or former teachers in the amended complaint). Funded or run by: not reported directly; the settlement paid the plaintiffs' legal fees and costs to the union's state affiliate ([ORG-1]), which belongs to a national teachers' union.
- Sought: relief against [OPERATOR]'s use of [MODEL] scores in teacher appraisal and in terminating teachers, under the federal constitution's due-process and related guarantees.
- Outcome: at A+10 the federal magistrate judge refused [OPERATOR]'s motion for summary judgment on the teachers' due-process claim that they had no fair chance to contest dismissals based on the scores, letting it go to trial; he granted judgment to [OPERATOR] on the claims that the system was unconstitutionally vague or had no rational link to the district's goal of having an effective teacher for every pupil. He also held that the district's ending of its vendor contract (E2) did not make the case moot, because the district was still considering using the model or similar tools. Later the same year the parties settled: [OPERATOR] agreed not to use value-added scores, including [MODEL] scores, to terminate any teacher for as long as teachers cannot independently test or challenge their scores; to restrict its wider use of value-added measures; to set up a consultation panel of district and teacher representatives to recommend changes to teacher appraisal; and to pay about a quarter of a million dollars in fees and costs. The district made no admission reported in the sources consulted.
- Stated reasons: the court found [MODEL] scores could be miscalculated for many reasons and that such algorithms are subject to error; because the formula and underlying data were held as the vendor's trade secret, teachers had no meaningful way to make sure their own scores were correctly calculated, and this denied them a fair chance to contest a dismissal that rested on the scores. It relied on an earlier federal appeals decision allowing dismissed employees to test independently the evidence used against them. On the other claims, the court found the system was rationally related to the district's legitimate aim.
- Relief applied to: all [OPERATOR] teachers going forward (settlement bars termination on unverifiable value-added scores district-wide); no reinstatement or damages for individual teachers reported.
- Sources: S1, S2, S3, S4.

E2. **[A+9] Decision of the operator's governing board ([OPERATOR] board of trustees) on the vendor contract.**
- Brought by: [OPERATOR]'s elected board of trustees, on a proposal to renew the [VENDOR] contract. Funded or run by: [OPERATOR].
- Sought: renewal of the annual contract (reported cost several hundred thousand dollars a year) under which [VENDOR] calculated [MODEL] scores.
- Outcome: the board split evenly, so the contract was not renewed and the district stopped obtaining [MODEL] scores; the following year the district amended its performance-pay programme to replace [MODEL] with a different comparative-growth measure, and did not replace the model in teacher appraisal with other vendor software.
- Stated reasons: as reported, some trustees concluded that about five years of use had not improved performance; the union's pending lawsuit (E1) and its view that the system was seriously flawed were part of the public debate. No official written reasons located.
- Relief applied to: all district teachers (scores no longer produced).
- Sources: S9, S5, S4.
