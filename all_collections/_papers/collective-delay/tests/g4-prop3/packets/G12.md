# Evidence packet G12

- **Domain:** unemployment insurance benefits; fraud (misrepresentation) determinations and debt collection
- **What the model does:** a state benefits computer system ([MODEL]) that, using programmed decision rules, flagged discrepancies in claimants' records (for example between reported and employer-reported earnings), sent questionnaires, and issued fraud determinations, in many cases with no human involvement; a fraud finding triggered repayment plus penalties of up to four times the amount, and automated collection such as tax-refund intercepts and wage garnishment.
- **Scale:** tens of thousands of fraud determinations in the first two years; tens of thousands later reversed.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = the state unemployment agency ([OPERATOR]) switched on [MODEL], built with private contractors, including its automated fraud-adjudication function.
- **De-identification:** names of the system, agency, state, vendors, courts, statutes, people and advocacy bodies are tokenised; not fully feasible, as the case is widely reported.

## Context (not episodes)
- A+0 to A+2: [OPERATOR]'s staff and an agency lawyer recommended changes to the fraud decision rules that were not adopted (per later court record) (S6).
- A+2: a member of the federal legislature from [STATE-A] wrote to the governor asking for review of cases and reimbursement (S8).
- A+2: media reports on false fraud accusations; bankruptcy filings by affected claimants (S5, S9).
- A+2 (mid-year): [OPERATOR] switched off automated fraud adjudication after staff said the system was finding fraud too often; since then a staff member must review and sign off before action (S6, S9).
- A+9 to A+12: [OPERATOR] procured a replacement benefits system from another contractor, with the existing vendor's contract running to A+12 (S16).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+0 to A+3] Individual administrative appeals against fraud determinations (aggregated).**
- Brought by: individual claimants, before state administrative law judges and on further review. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: reversal of fraud determinations, penalties and collections.
- Outcome: the backlog of appeals peaked in the tens of thousands; in fraud cases that reached a judge, the agency's position was upheld in under a tenth, and the rest were reversed, dismissed, modified or remanded.
- Stated reasons: per case; not reported in aggregate.
- Relief applied to: individual appellants.
- Sources: S10.

E2. **[A+2] Federal labour department ([AGENCY-2]) monitoring review of [OPERATOR].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-2], which oversees state unemployment programmes. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-2].
- Sought: compliance of [OPERATOR]'s fraud determination practice with federal rules.
- Outcome: an [AGENCY-2] administrator told [OPERATOR] that every fraud determination requires a complete investigation including a chance to rebut before issue, and may not be made by an automated system; [OPERATOR] replied that it found no such limit in federal guidance. A formal monitoring report later that year found the practice violated federal regulations. A federal legislator reported that [AGENCY-2] advised [OPERATOR] to re-adjudicate all fraud cases. Shortly after the action was filed in E3, [AGENCY-2] reminded all state agencies that automated systems cannot by themselves make overpayment or fraud determinations (reported).
- Stated reasons: fraud determinations require individual investigation and an opportunity to rebut.
- Relief applied to: all claimants (policy direction to [OPERATOR]).
- Sources: S6, S8, S9.

E3. **[A+2 to A+4] Federal civil-rights lawsuit against the agency director ([COURT-1]).**
- Brought by: individual claimants, a national industrial labour union ([ORG-2]) and a non-profit law centre ([ORG-1]). Funded or run by: run by [ORG-1] with private counsel; funding not reported.
- Sought: an order stopping fraud determinations and collections made without adequate notice and hearing, as a breach of the federal constitutional right to due process.
- Outcome: the case was dismissed at A+4 under an agreement between the state and the plaintiffs that included safeguards so that claimants are told of fraud accusations and given time to respond; the state agreed to suspend collections tied to the automated determinations and reviewed earlier determinations (per later court records).
- Stated reasons: settlement; no final judgment on the merits.
- Relief applied to: all claimants subject to [OPERATOR] fraud determinations.
- Sources: S5, S6, S7.

E4. **[A+2 to A+11] State-court action for damages against [OPERATOR] ([COURT-3]; highest decided level [COURT-4 (appellate)]).**
- Brought by: claimants, as a class action under state law. Funded or run by: a private plaintiffs' law firm ([FIRM-1]); funding not reported.
- Sought: money damages for breach of the state constitution's due process guarantee through automated fraud determinations and seizure of money.
- Outcome: an intermediate appellate court dismissed the action as untimely; at A+6.3 [COURT-4] reversed, holding the action timely but limited to persons from whom money was first collected after a date early in A+2. At A+9.5 [COURT-4] held that a damages remedy is available against the State for violations of the state constitution. The state settled at A+9.8 for about twenty million dollars; the trial court approved it preliminarily at A+10 and finally at A+11. Thousands of eligible claimants were identified and a few thousand filed claims.
- Stated reasons: [COURT-4] held that the claim accrued when money was taken, not when the determination was issued, and later that the plaintiffs may seek money damages from the State for the alleged violation of the state constitution.
- Relief applied to: eligible claimants falsely accused of fraud within the period.
- Sources: S12, S13, S14.

E5. **[A+3] Performance audit by the state auditor general ([AGENCY-1]).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1]. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-1].
- Sought: audit of [OPERATOR]'s use of [MODEL].
- Outcome: found that [MODEL] approved and paid benefits and then reversed those approvals, leading to fraud charges with penalties of up to four times the amount; an appeals backlog peaked in the tens of thousands cases; in appealed fraud (misrepresentation) cases, administrative law judges upheld the agency in under a tenth of cases; system security and access-control weaknesses. Recommended that [OPERATOR] better explain to people why they are accused of fraud and gather better information before a fraud finding. [OPERATOR] agreed with most findings.
- Stated reasons: as found.
- Relief applied to: not applicable directly.
- Sources: S10, S11.

E6. **[A+3 to A+4] Agency review of past fraud determinations and refunds (executive act).**
- Brought by: [OPERATOR] and its parent state agency. Funded or run by: the state.
- Sought: not applicable.
- Outcome: at A+3, a review of over twenty thousand fraud determinations made with no human involvement found that most were wrong (over nine in ten reported); thousands of people began receiving refunds. At A+4, after reviewing further determinations, including tens of thousands with some human involvement, [OPERATOR] reported reversing tens of thousands of fraud findings and refunding about twenty million dollars, alongside changes in procedure.
- Stated reasons: the programming allowed [MODEL] to make discretionary fraud findings without human intervention (agency testimony). A later summary put the error share at about 85% for determinations made by the system without human action and about 44% for those with some human involvement.
- Relief applied to: persons with determinations in the reviewed period.
- Sources: S9, S15, S17.

E7. **[A+4] State statute barring automated fraud determinations ([STATUTE-1]).**
- Brought by: the state legislature; signed by the governor. Funded or run by: not applicable.
- Sought: not applicable.
- Outcome: enacted; [OPERATOR] may not determine that a person committed benefits fraud unless a state employee verifies it; this codified the agency's change made at A+2.
- Stated reasons: response to the many mistaken fraud accusations by the computer system.
- Relief applied to: all future claimants.
- Sources: S18.

E8. **[A+4 to A+11] Federal civil-rights lawsuit against the system's vendors and agency officials ([COURT-1]; [COURT-2 (appellate)]).**
- Brought by: five named claimants, filed as a proposed class action. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: damages from three private contractors ([VENDOR-1], [VENDOR-2], [VENDOR-3]) and agency officials for breach of due process and other federal constitutional rights, and for negligence.
- Outcome: at A+6 [COURT-2] held that the due process claim was adequately pleaded and the right to a hearing before deprivation was clearly established, but dismissed the equal protection and search-and-seizure claims. At A+8, [COURT-1] dismissed one plaintiff and three agency officials but let claims against [VENDOR-1], [VENDOR-2] and two senior officials proceed; it found that the system's notices were insufficient to inform claimants of fraud accusations. At A+10.5 [COURT-2] held that the two senior officials had qualified immunity because the plaintiffs had not shown that the system's notice, hearing and appeal safeguards violated clearly established law. The docket closed at A+11; how [VENDOR-3] left the case and the final disposition of the claims against [VENDOR-1] and [VENDOR-2] are not reported in the sources consulted.
- Stated reasons: as above.
- Relief applied to: named plaintiffs only (no relief reported).
- Sources: S6, S19, S20, S21.

E9. **[A+4] State statute amending fraud penalties and procedures ([STATUTE-2]).**
- Brought by: the state legislature as part of an eight-bill package; signed by the governor. Funded or run by: not applicable.
- Sought: not applicable.
- Outcome: enacted, effective at A+5; reduced the maximum penalty multipliers for fraud (reported figures differ between the bill as introduced and the enacted text) and amended rules on recovery and damages for willful violations.
- Stated reasons: response to the agency's false fraud findings.
- Relief applied to: future claimants.
- Sources: S22, S23.

E10. **[A+10] Performance audit by the state auditor general ([AGENCY-1]) of fraud handling in a later period.**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1]. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-1].
- Sought: audit of [OPERATOR]'s handling of fraudulent claims and penalties over a period from A+7 to A+9.
- Outcome: found that [OPERATOR] had not correctly programmed [MODEL] for fraud penalties and undercalculated penalties in about half of cases; it also often failed to identify, pursue or refer fraudulent claims; most open fraud investigations had not been resolved.
- Stated reasons: as found; [OPERATOR] planned to replace the software.
- Relief applied to: not applicable.
- Sources: S24.
