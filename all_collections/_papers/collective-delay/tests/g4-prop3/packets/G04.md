# Evidence packet G04

- **Domain:** bank capital regulation
- **What the model does:** lets approved banks estimate their own credit-risk parameters (probability of default, loss given default, exposure at default) and feed them into a supervisory formula that sets the risk weights, and so the minimum capital, held against loans.
- **Scale:** hundreds of large banks in dozens of jurisdictions; the loans and capital of those banks rest on it.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = an international standard-setting committee publishes a revised bank capital framework that allows banks, with supervisory approval, to use [MODEL] as the basis for credit-risk capital (national implementation followed in A+2 to A+4).
- **De-identification:** bodies, jurisdictions, statutes and named reports tokenised; not fully feasible, as the case is widely known in the domain.

## Context (not episodes)
- A+0 to A+2: [ORG-1] ran impact studies to calibrate [MODEL] before implementation (S1).
- A+4 to A+5: during the global financial crisis, academic and market commentators questioned whether model-based risk weights understated bank risk (S1, S19).
- A+9: [ORG-1] issued a discussion paper on trading off risk sensitivity against simplicity and comparability in the capital framework (S19).
- A+9 to A+21: no court case challenging [MODEL] outputs or use was found in any jurisdiction searched (see notes).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+1] Regulatory decision by banking agencies in [COUNTRY-A].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-3] (the banking agencies of [COUNTRY-A]), acting on their own impact study. Funded or run by: the agencies.
- Sought: to decide whether to proceed on schedule with domestic rules for [MODEL].
- Outcome: the agencies delayed their proposed rule after their impact study showed large drops in minimum required capital and wide spread in results across banks for similar exposures.
- Stated reasons: the size and spread of the capital reductions required further analysis before going ahead.
- Relief applied to: the large banks that would have used [MODEL] in [COUNTRY-A].
- Sources: S2.

E2. **[A+3] Final rule by banking agencies in [COUNTRY-A].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-3]. Funded or run by: the agencies.
- Sought: to implement [MODEL] domestically with safeguards.
- Outcome: final rule adopted [MODEL] for the largest banks but kept a separate leverage ratio and imposed transitional floors limiting how far model-based capital could fall below the older requirements during the first years; the agencies stated they would review results before removing the floors.
- Stated reasons: uncertainty about how far model estimates could reduce capital and the need to retain a capital backstop independent of models.
- Relief applied to: banks in [COUNTRY-A] required or choosing to use [MODEL].
- Sources: S3.

E3. **[A+6] Legislative act in [COUNTRY-A] ([STATUTE-1], capital-floor section).**
- Brought by: legislature of [COUNTRY-A] (provision sponsored by a senator, [PERSON-1]). Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: a permanent floor on model-based capital.
- Outcome: enacted; banks using [MODEL] may not hold less capital than the generally applicable (non-model) requirements; agencies implemented this by rule in A+7.
- Stated reasons: to stop large banks' model-based requirements falling below those applied to other banks.
- Relief applied to: all banks in [COUNTRY-A] using [MODEL].
- Sources: S4, S5.

E4. **[A+9] Supervisory consistency review by [ORG-1] (first banking-book risk-weight study).**
- Brought by: [ORG-1] under its implementation-assessment programme. Funded or run by: [ORG-1] member supervisors.
- Sought: to measure how far risk weights for credit risk differ across banks and why.
- Outcome: report drew on supervisory data from more than a hundred banks and a hypothetical portfolio exercise in which tens of large banks rated the same sovereign, bank and corporate borrowers. Most variation in risk weights reflected differences in banks' assets, but a material part came from differences in bank and supervisory practice; banks ranked borrowers similarly but gave different absolute default and loss estimates. For outlier banks this could shift reported capital ratios by up to about two percentage points around a 10% benchmark. Policy options were to be developed.
- Stated reasons: concern about comparability of model-based capital ratios.
- Relief applied to: none directly; fed into later standard changes.
- Sources: S6.

E5. **[A+9] Supervisory benchmarking studies by [AGENCY-2] (regional banking authority).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-2]. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-2] with national supervisors.
- Sought: to explain differences in model-based risk weights across banks in [REGION-1].
- Outcome: interim and summary reports (A+9) found that a substantial share of differences in risk weights could not be explained by differences in portfolio risk alone, and pointed to differences in supervisory and bank practice; recommended more supervisory consistency and benchmarking.
- Stated reasons: lack of confidence among market participants and supervisors in comparability of risk weights.
- Relief applied to: banks in [REGION-1] using [MODEL] (via later supervisory work).
- Sources: S7.

E6. **[A+9] Mortgage risk-weight floor, supervisor of [COUNTRY-B].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-4] (national supervisor). Funded or run by: the supervisor.
- Sought: a minimum average risk weight on domestic mortgages for banks using [MODEL].
- Outcome: floor of 15% set in A+9 via individual supervisory capital requirements; raised to 25% in A+10; moved in A+14 into a measure under the regional capital regulation's national-flexibility article; extended again in A+21.
- Stated reasons: model-based mortgage risk weights were judged too low relative to the risks, including systemic risks from household debt and house prices.
- Relief applied to: banks using [MODEL] for domestic mortgages in [COUNTRY-B].
- Sources: S8, S9, S10.

E7. **[A+9] Mortgage risk-weight floor, supervisor of [JURISDICTION-C].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-5]. Funded or run by: the supervisor.
- Sought: minimum risk weight on new residential mortgages for banks using [MODEL].
- Outcome: 15% floor introduced.
- Stated reasons: to make banks hold more capital against property-price risk than their models produced.
- Relief applied to: banks using [MODEL] in [JURISDICTION-C].
- Sources: S11.

E8. **[A+9] Mortgage risk-weight add-on, supervisor of [COUNTRY-D].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-6] (central bank / supervisor). Funded or run by: the supervisor.
- Sought: an add-on to model-based mortgage risk weights.
- Outcome: a 5 percentage-point add-on to risk weights produced by [MODEL] on domestic mortgages, later notified under the regional capital regulation's national-flexibility article and extended or modified in later years.
- Stated reasons: model risk weights were low relative to real-estate risks.
- Relief applied to: banks using [MODEL] in [COUNTRY-D].
- Sources: S10, S12.

E9. **[A+9] Capital adequacy exercise by authorities of [COUNTRY-E].**
- Brought by: the macroprudential committee of [COUNTRY-E]'s central bank, which directed the prudential supervisor [AGENCY-7]. Funded or run by: the authorities.
- Sought: to assess major banks' capital after adjusting for over-optimistic asset valuations, conduct costs and less prudent risk-weight calculations.
- Outcome: the supervisor found an aggregate capital shortfall at several major banks, part of which came from applying more prudent risk weights than banks' own calculations; banks were required to submit plans to close the shortfall.
- Stated reasons: concern that banks' reported capital overstated their resilience, including because of model-based risk weights.
- Relief applied to: the major banks of [COUNTRY-E].
- Sources: S13.

E10. **[A+9] Parliamentary inquiry in [COUNTRY-E].**
- Brought by: a joint commission of both houses of parliament. Funded or run by: the legislature.
- Sought: an inquiry into banking standards and culture, including capital rules.
- Outcome: the final report criticised reliance on banks' internal risk-weight models, found that model outputs were open to optimisation by banks, and recommended that the supervisor give greater weight to a leverage ratio and to backstops to models.
- Stated reasons: model complexity and the scope for banks to lower their capital through modelling choices.
- Relief applied to: none directly; recommendations to government and supervisor.
- Sources: S14.

E11. **[A+10] Report of [ORG-1] to [ORG-2] (group of major economies) on capital-ratio variability.**
- Brought by: [ORG-1] at the request of [ORG-2] leaders. Funded or run by: [ORG-1].
- Sought: proposals to reduce excessive variability in model-based risk weights.
- Outcome: set out a programme of policy options: constraints on the use of internal models, input floors, a capital floor based on standardised approaches, and improved disclosure.
- Stated reasons: variability not explained by risk undermines confidence in capital ratios.
- Relief applied to: none directly.
- Sources: S15.

E12. **[A+10] Government inquiry into the financial system of [COUNTRY-F].**
- Brought by: an inquiry appointed by the government of [COUNTRY-F]. Funded or run by: the government.
- Sought: a review of the financial system, including competitive effects of model-based capital.
- Outcome: recommended that the supervisor raise average mortgage risk weights for banks using [MODEL] to narrow the gap with banks on the standardised approach.
- Stated reasons: model-based mortgage risk weights gave large banks a funding cost advantage and were low relative to standardised weights.
- Relief applied to: none directly; recommendation to supervisor.
- Sources: S16.

E13. **[A+10 / A+16] Mortgage loss-given-default floor and later risk-weight floor, supervisor of [COUNTRY-G].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-8] and the finance ministry of [COUNTRY-G]. Funded or run by: the authorities.
- Sought: higher minimum inputs and outputs for model-based mortgage risk weights.
- Outcome: loss-given-default floor for residential mortgages raised from 10% to 20% (A+10); a minimum average risk weight of 20% on residential mortgages introduced in A+16.
- Stated reasons: model risk weights were too low given household debt and house-price risk.
- Relief applied to: banks using [MODEL] in [COUNTRY-G].
- Sources: S16, S17.

E14. **[A+11] Supervisory decision in [COUNTRY-F].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-9] (prudential supervisor of [COUNTRY-F]). Funded or run by: the supervisor.
- Sought: to implement the inquiry recommendation in E12.
- Outcome: required banks using [MODEL] to raise average mortgage risk weights from about 17% to at least 25% by A+12 (this followed an earlier A+4 decision to set a mortgage loss-given-default floor of 20%, double the international minimum).
- Stated reasons: to narrow the gap with standardised risk weights and to respond to the inquiry.
- Relief applied to: the handful of large banks using [MODEL] in [COUNTRY-F].
- Sources: S16.

E15. **[A+11 onward] Annual supervisory benchmarking by [AGENCY-2].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-2] under a duty in the regional capital directive. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-2] and national supervisors.
- Sought: yearly comparison of model outputs on common portfolios, for supervisors to act on outliers.
- Outcome: recurring reports from A+11 to at least A+21 identifying dispersion of risk parameters and outlier banks; national supervisors required to assess outliers.
- Stated reasons: statutory requirement to monitor consistency of internal models.
- Relief applied to: banks using [MODEL] in [REGION-1].
- Sources: S7.

E16. **[A+12] Supervisory consistency review by [ORG-1] (second banking-book risk-weight study).**
- Brought by: [ORG-1]. Funded or run by: [ORG-1] member supervisors.
- Sought: to examine variation in retail and small-business risk estimates and exposure-at-default modelling.
- Outcome: report found differences in bank practices, including in how banks independently validate their models, and described sound practice.
- Stated reasons: as in E4.
- Relief applied to: none directly.
- Sources: S18.

E17. **[A+12 to A+13] Revision of the international standard by [ORG-1].**
- Brought by: [ORG-1]; consultation in A+12, final standard in A+13 agreed by its oversight body of central bank governors and heads of supervision. Funded or run by: [ORG-1].
- Sought: to reduce unwarranted variability of model-based risk weights.
- Outcome: final standard removed the most advanced form of [MODEL] for exposures to large corporates, banks and other financial institutions, removed model use for equity exposures, set minimum values (input floors) for banks' default and loss estimates, and introduced an aggregate output floor so that model-based risk-weighted assets cannot fall below 72.5% of the standardised amount (phased in over several years).
- Stated reasons: excessive variability in risk weights not explained by risk; low-default portfolios could not be modelled robustly.
- Relief applied to: all internationally active banks using [MODEL] in member jurisdictions, once implemented nationally.
- Sources: S15, S20.

E18. **[A+12 to A+17] Supervisory review of internal models by [AGENCY-1] (regional bank supervisor).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1]. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-1] with national supervisors.
- Sought: to check whether significant banks' internal models complied with regulatory requirements and to reduce unwarranted variability.
- Outcome: about two hundred on-site investigations at several dozen banks; thousands of findings; binding measures requiring remediation by deadlines; risk-weighted assets for the investigated models rose by roughly a tenth (in the hundreds of billions in local currency) and banks' common equity ratios fell on average by under one percentage point. Banks were allowed to keep using models if they fixed shortcomings.
- Stated reasons: inconsistent and in some cases non-compliant modelling practices.
- Relief applied to: significant banks in [REGION-1] using [MODEL].
- Sources: S21.

E19. **[A+12 to A+14] Regulatory standards and guidelines programme by [AGENCY-2].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-2]. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-2].
- Sought: to harmonise how banks build and supervisors approve [MODEL] estimates.
- Outcome: issued technical standards on the supervisory assessment method, guidelines on the definition of default and guidelines on estimating default and loss parameters, with application dates in later years.
- Stated reasons: to reduce variability in risk parameters arising from differences in practice rather than risk.
- Relief applied to: banks using [MODEL] in [REGION-1].
- Sources: S7.

- Outcome: 15% floor for banks using [MODEL], under the regional capital regulation's national-flexibility article.

E22. **[A+19] Proposed rule by banking agencies in [COUNTRY-A].**

- Relief applied to: would have applied to large banks in [COUNTRY-A].
