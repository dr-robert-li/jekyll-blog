# Evidence packet G09

- **Domain:** welfare debt
- **What the model does:** an automated data-matching programme that compares annual employment income reported to the tax authority with fortnightly income reported to the welfare agency; where the recipient does not supply other evidence, it spreads the annual figure evenly across fortnights (income averaging) to calculate an overpayment and raise a debt, with recovery fees and tax-refund garnishee.
- **Scale:** hundreds of thousands of welfare recipients had debts raised.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = [AGENCY-1] (national welfare agency) launched [MODEL], an online compliance programme that raised debts automatically using income averaging, after a budget measure by the national government.
- **De-identification:** model, agencies, people, courts, statutes and places tokenised; not fully feasible (the case is widely known).

## Context (not episodes)
- [A+0.5 onwards] Extensive media coverage, an online protest campaign, and complaints to members of parliament about debts sent to welfare recipients (S1).
- [A+0.9] The national auditor-general declined a parliamentarian's request to audit [MODEL], noting that [AGENCY-2] had already examined it (S6).
- [A+2.5] The auditor-general reported on [AGENCY-1]'s compliance strategies generally; no specific findings on [MODEL] verified (S6).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+0 to A+6] First-tier merits review decisions of [TRIBUNAL-1] (administrative appeals tribunal), aggregated.**
- Brought by: individual welfare recipients appealing debts after internal review. Funded or run by: mostly self-represented or assisted by community legal centres; not reported in aggregate.
- Sought: to have individual debts set aside or recalculated.
- Outcome: a later judicial speech reported that between A+0 and A+6 the first tier of the tribunal made several hundred decisions that questioned the legal basis for income averaging and set aside or varied the debts, and about a hundred decisions that accepted averaging as appropriate on the facts. [AGENCY-1] did not appeal any of the adverse decisions to the second tier; the first-tier decisions were not published, so they did not bind or inform other cases. Evidence to the later [INQUIRY-1] (E11) indicated the department chose not to appeal partly to avoid a published ruling; an internal email warned that failing to appeal could open a wide range of further challenges.
- Stated reasons: the tribunal members found that an annual income figure spread evenly across the year did not establish the actual income in each fortnight, so the debt was not proven.
- Relief applied to: each appellant only (individual debts).
- Sources: S2, S3, S4.

E2. **[A+0.8] Own-motion investigation by [AGENCY-2] (national ombudsman).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-2], prompted by complaints. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-2].
- Sought: to investigate the design and operation of [MODEL].
- Outcome: report with eight recommendations, all accepted by [AGENCY-1] and the policy department. [AGENCY-2] found that the programme could calculate debts accurately where recipients supplied correct information, but that it had problems of usability and transparency; and that many implementation problems could have been reduced by better planning and risk management. It did not find the programme less accurate or fair than manual processes in principle.
- Stated reasons: as above.
- Relief applied to: system-wide recommendations (clearer letters, help in obtaining income evidence, support for vulnerable people, review of the recovery fee).
- Sources: S5.

E3. **[A+0.9] Upper-house parliamentary committee inquiry (social policy) into the programme.**
- Brought by: [COMMITTEE-1] of the upper house, on referral by the upper house. Funded or run by: parliament.
- Sought: to examine the design, scope, cost-benefit analysis, contracts and implementation of the compliance initiative including [MODEL].
- Outcome: report tabled with recommendations (detail not verified; see notes). The programme continued.
- Stated reasons: not verified in detail.
- Relief applied to: recommendations to government.
- Sources: S7.

E4. **[A+2.6 to A+3.0] Judicial review in [COURT-1] (federal court) by an individual recipient; debt withdrawn before hearing.**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-1], a welfare recipient, represented by [ORG-1] (a state legal aid commission) as a test case. Funded or run by: [ORG-1].
- Sought: a declaration that a debt raised by income averaging was unlawful.
- Outcome: [AGENCY-1] wiped the debt (about 4,000 in local currency) before a hearing; its counsel told the court the government accepted it had been wrong after receiving information from the applicant and argued there was no case left to answer. No judgment on lawfulness was given.
- Stated reasons: [AGENCY-1] recalculated the debt using information from the applicant.
- Relief applied to: [PLAINTIFF-1] only.
- Sources: S8, S9.

E5. **[A+2.8] Follow-up report by [AGENCY-2] (national ombudsman).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-2]. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-2].
- Sought: to check implementation of its earlier recommendations.
- Outcome: the policy department had implemented its recommendation and [AGENCY-1] had made significant progress on the rest; four further recommendations made. [AGENCY-2] continued to receive complaints and monitor the programme.
- Stated reasons: as above.
- Relief applied to: system-wide recommendations.
- Sources: S5.

E6. **[A+3.0 to A+6] Second upper-house committee inquiry into the compliance programme.**
- Brought by: [COMMITTEE-1], on referral by the upper house. Funded or run by: parliament.
- Sought: to examine the income compliance programme including [MODEL] and its successor arrangements.
- Outcome: five interim reports and a final report whose title called for a royal commission; an early recommendation to end the programme immediately was rejected by the government in its A+4.6 response. The government responded to the fifth interim and final reports at A+6.3.
- Stated reasons: not verified in detail.
- Relief applied to: recommendations to government.
- Sources: S10.

E7. **[A+3.3] Federal court proceeding by an individual recipient, resolved by consent orders ([COURT-1]).**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-2], a welfare recipient, represented by [ORG-1] as a test case. Funded or run by: [ORG-1].
- Sought: declarations that her averaged debt, the 10% recovery fee and the seizure of her tax refund were unlawful; repayment with interest.
- Outcome: consent orders; the court declared that averaged tax-authority income information was not capable of satisfying a decision-maker that a debt existed, so the debt decision was not validly made. The government conceded the averaging-based debt, the penalty fee and the tax-refund seizure were unlawful and paid interest on the amount taken.
- Stated reasons: averaged annual income does not show what was earned in each fortnight, so it cannot by itself found a debt.
- Relief applied to: [PLAINTIFF-2] only (by the orders); the declaration's reasoning applied to debts raised in the same way.
- Sources: S11, S12.

E8. **[A+3.3] Executive decision to stop raising debts on averaged data without other evidence.**
- Brought by: the responsible minister/[AGENCY-1], after legal advice that the practice was unlawful. Funded or run by: government.
- Sought: to halt debt raising and recovery that relied on income averaging with no other evidence.
- Outcome: announced in the same month as E7; debts already raised remained under review.
- Stated reasons: it was reported that the decision came around the time the public service advised that the practice was unlawful.
- Relief applied to: future debt decisions.
- Sources: S13.

E9. **[A+3.3 to A+4.9] Representative proceeding (class action) in [COURT-1] (federal court), settled.**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-3] and other lead applicants for a group of hundreds of thousands of people. Funded or run by: [ORG-2] (a plaintiff law firm); funding terms not reported.
- Sought: declarations that the debts were invalid, repayment, compensation for unjust enrichment and negligence.
- Outcome: settled at A+4.3; approved at A+4.9. The government admitted it had no proper legal basis for debts totalling over a billion in local currency raised against hundreds of thousands of people; agreed to pay over a hundred million (including interest and costs), not to recover the debts, and the court declared that debt decisions based on income averaging from tax data were invalid. The judge described the scheme as a disgraceful episode in the administration of social security and a very large failure of public administration. He allowed some objecting group members a further chance to opt out.
- Stated reasons: the government's admission; the court found the settlement fair and reasonable given litigation risk on the damages claims.
- Relief applied to: all group members (hundreds of thousands).
- Sources: S14, S15.

E10. **[A+3.9] Executive decision to refund averaging-based debts.**
- Brought by: the responsible minister. Funded or run by: government.
- Sought: to refund debts raised wholly or partly by income averaging.
- Outcome: hundreds of thousands of debts (owed by hundreds of thousands of people) to be refunded, hundreds of millions in local currency, including interest and recovery fees.
- Stated reasons: the government accepted it could not rely on averaging to establish debts, following the court proceeding in E7.
- Relief applied to: everyone with an averaging-based debt.
- Sources: S16.

E11. **[A+6.1 to A+7.0] National royal commission ([INQUIRY-1]).**
- Brought by: the national government (established after a change of government). Funded or run by: government; led by a former chief justice of a state.
- Sought: to inquire into the establishment, design and implementation of [MODEL], its handling of concerns, and its human impact.
- Outcome: report with 57 recommendations. It found the scheme was a harsh, unsophisticated mechanism that was neither fair nor lawful; that income averaging was a central flaw; that a former departmental head knew of the scheme's legal problems and failed to act, and that ministers had failed to properly examine income averaging; that the department did not appeal adverse tribunal decisions; and that individuals should be referred for possible civil or criminal action (in a sealed chapter). Recommendations included reinstating a six-year limit on debt recovery and a body to oversee automated decision-making.
- Stated reasons: as above.
- Relief applied to: recommendations to government; referrals of unnamed individuals to [AGENCY-3], [AGENCY-4] and other bodies.
- Sources: S17.

E12. **[A+7.9 to A+8.6] National anti-corruption commission decision on referrals, with oversight review and reconsideration.**
- Brought by: referrals from [INQUIRY-1] to [AGENCY-4] (national anti-corruption commission). Funded or run by: [AGENCY-4]; review by its inspector.
- Sought: investigation of six referred individuals for corrupt conduct.
- Outcome: [AGENCY-4] decided at A+7.9 not to investigate. After complaints, the commission's inspector found at A+8.3 that the head of the commission had not fully removed himself from the decision despite a conflict, amounting to officer misconduct without intentional wrongdoing, and recommended independent reconsideration. A former senior judge reconsidered and at A+8.6 found each referral raised a corruption issue; [AGENCY-4] began investigating the six. No outcome by the end of the period.
- Stated reasons: initial decision: the conduct had already been exhaustively examined and a further investigation was unlikely to find much new; reconsideration reasons not published.
- Relief applied to: none to date.
- Sources: S18.

E13. **[A+8.2] Centralised public-service code-of-conduct inquiry.**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-5] (public service commissioner), on referrals arising from [INQUIRY-1]. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-5].
- Sought: to determine whether current and former public servants breached the public service code of conduct in connection with [MODEL].
- Outcome: of 16 people examined, 12 were found to have breached the code (nearly a hundred breaches in total, ranging from moderate to very serious); four current employees sanctioned (reprimands, fines, demotions); former agency heads could not be sanctioned.
- Stated reasons: findings on individual conduct; detailed reasons not verified.
- Relief applied to: the individual officials.
- Sources: S19.

E14. **[A+8 to A+9.2] Appeal to set aside the class action settlement ([COURT-1]), settled.**
- Brought by: two group members of E9, represented by [ORG-2]. Funded or run by: [ORG-2].
- Sought: orders setting aside the A+4.9 settlement so that new claims (including for harm caused) could be brought.
- Outcome: settled at A+9.2; the government agreed to pay a further several hundred million in local currency in compensation (plus capped legal and administration costs), subject to court approval, which was pending at the end of the period.
- Stated reasons: at the time of the first settlement, group members and their lawyers lacked important evidence later produced to [INQUIRY-1].
- Relief applied to: eligible group members.
- Sources: S20.
