# Evidence packet G14

- **Domain:** public health-insurance home-care benefits (in-home attendant care for elderly and physically disabled people eligible for nursing-home placement)
- **What the model does:** after a nurse completes a long standardised needs assessment (hundreds of items), the algorithm sorts each person into one of about two dozen resource-utilisation groups based on diagnoses and answers; each group carries a fixed number of weekly attendant-care hours. Nurses lost discretion to set hours.
- **Scale:** thousands of recipients (a programme of several thousand people; the agency's own review found nearly half had their hours cut, in the thousands).
- **Adoption:** A+0 = start of the merged home-care programme, in which [AGENCY-1] began setting hours with [MODEL] instead of nurse judgement.
- **De-identification:** model, agency, state, courts, statutes, firms and people tokenised; partly feasible, though the case is well known.

## Context (not episodes)
- [A+0] The tool was run through software supplied by a third-party vendor [VENDOR]; senior agency officials later testified they could not explain the algorithm. The agency stated in court that it had lost the data on which group hours were based (S5).
- [A+0.4] An internal agency review found nearly half of recipients had their hours cut (S5).
- [A+1 to A+3] Media coverage and advocacy by [ORG-1] (legal aid provider) on cuts of 20–60% for severely disabled clients (S5, S6).
- [A+3 onward] After [MODEL] was dropped (E5), a new contractor-run assessment began. Further federal suits by [ORG-1] (A+3.4, A+4.1) concerned that successor process and the agency's handling of appeals and continued benefits, not [MODEL] (S9).
- [A+3.5] Committees of the legislature on aging voted to review the successor assessment contract (S8).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+0 to A+3] Individual administrative fair hearings.**
- Brought by: individual recipients whose hours were reduced, through the agency's appeals and hearings office, with possible further appeal to state circuit court. Funded or run by: mostly self-represented; [ORG-1] assisted about a hundred people in the first year.
- Sought: restoration of pre-reduction hours.
- Outcome: aggregate numbers and outcomes not reported. A hearing request within ten days kept services at the prior level pending the hearing; recipients who lost could be billed for services received meanwhile.
- Stated reasons: not reported in aggregate.
- Relief applied to: individual appellants only.
- Sources: S5.

E2. **[A+0.8] Federal district court individual suit, [COURT-1].**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-1], a very elderly recipient with advanced dementia whose weekly hours fell from the mid-40s to the mid-30s. Funded or run by: [ORG-1], a legal aid nonprofit.
- Sought: restoration of hours and adequate notice of why hours were cut under the new system.
- Outcome: after a three-day trial the judge ruled for the plaintiff (A+0.8): the agency must restore her hours unless and until it explained in writing the specific reasons for the change. The agency stated on the record that it would fix its notices system-wide and later revised them. The plaintiff died days after the ruling. The court later awarded [ORG-1] about half its requested fees and costs (A+1.5). At the trial, the agency's software and the developer of the underlying grouping system produced different results for the same person.
- Stated reasons: the agency did not give adequate notice of how hours were determined under the new method; hours could not be cut arbitrarily.
- Relief applied to: the named plaintiff by order; the agency's commitment on notices applied to all recipients.
- Sources: S1, S2, S5, S9.

E3. **[A+1.0 to A+3.3] State court suit, [COURT-2] (trial), with appeals to [COURT-3 (state supreme)].**
- Brought by: seven severely physically disabled recipients, lead plaintiff [PLAINTIFF-2] (whose hours fell from the mid-50s to the low 30s with no change in condition). Funded or run by: [ORG-1].
- Sought: an order barring use of [MODEL] to set hours because the agency adopted it without the public notice and comment required by [STATUTE-1], the state rulemaking law; restoration of the plaintiffs' hours.
- Outcome:
  - A+1.1: temporary order reversing cuts for the seven plaintiffs. A+1.9: [COURT-3] unanimously affirmed it on the agency's interlocutory appeal.
  - During the litigation, plaintiffs' counsel found that the vendor's implementation failed to properly account for certain diagnoses (diabetes and cerebral palsy), lowering hours for hundreds of people.
  - A+2.3: the trial court refused to let a disability-rights protection and advocacy body and three further recipients intervene, saying their claims needed separate fact-finding and could proceed in their own action.
  - A+2.4: permanent injunction barring use of [MODEL] to allocate hours until a rule was properly adopted. The agency then adopted an emergency rule using the same method (see E4); the trial judge suspended it, called it a deliberate disobedience of the injunction and a manufactured emergency, and held the agency in contempt.
  - A+3.3: [COURT-3], 5–2, reversed the contempt finding (holding that adopting an emergency rule did not violate the injunction) and dismissed the remaining appeal as moot, because the agency had by then adopted a new rule and the injunction had been dissolved (E5).
- Stated reasons: the trial court found the agency failed to give adequate public notice before adopting the method; the ruling rested on how the method was adopted, not on whether its outputs were correct. The state supreme court majority held the emergency-rule route was available under [STATUTE-1].
- Relief applied to: temporary relief to the seven plaintiffs; the permanent injunction applied to all programme recipients (several thousand) until replaced.
- Sources: S3, S4, S6, S7, S10, S11, S12.

E4. **[A+2.4] Legislative oversight body approval of emergency rule, [LEGISLATURE-1 committee].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1], seeking approval of an emergency rule to keep using [MODEL] after the E3 injunction. Funded or run by: state government.
- Sought: authority to continue allocating hours by [MODEL] without public notice and comment, on the grounds of imminent peril to recipients' welfare and the federal waiver's requirements.
- Outcome: the legislature's interim governing council (via its rules subcommittee) approved the emergency rule within days of the injunction. The trial court then suspended the rule (E3); the state supreme court later held its adoption was not contempt.
- Stated reasons: the agency said it needed continuity of assessments and enrolment and that the federal waiver required the algorithm; [ORG-1] disputed that any federal requirement existed.
- Relief applied to: all programme recipients (rule of general application).
- Sources: S6, S7, S11.

E5. **[A+3] Legislative oversight body approval of replacement rules, [LEGISLATURE-1 committee].**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1], proposing permanent rules. Funded or run by: state government.
- Sought: approval of new rules for the home-care programme.
- Outcome: approved by divided voice vote of the legislature's interim governing council after an attempt to block them in its rules subcommittee; effective A+3. The rules dropped [MODEL] and its resource-utilisation groups; hours were instead to be set from estimated minutes of help needed for daily tasks, with annual dollar caps by level of need (with exceptions for exceptional circumstances and protection for some existing high-cost recipients). A new contractor-run assessment began at the same time.
- Stated reasons: the agency said the new method allowed more flexibility for individual needs; the overall package was also tied to slowing growth in health-insurance spending.
- Relief applied to: all programme recipients.
- Sources: S8, S13.
