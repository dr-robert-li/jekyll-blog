# Evidence packet G08

- **Domain:** branch accounting / attribution of cash shortfalls to franchise operators
- **What the model does:** a computerised branch accounting system that records transactions and calculates each branch's expected cash and stock; discrepancies it shows are treated by [OPERATOR] as debts owed by the branch operator, and its records were used as evidence in recovery demands, contract terminations and criminal prosecutions (theft, fraud, false accounting).
- **Scale:** thousands of branch operators had shortfalls attributed to them; hundreds were prosecuted.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = national rollout of [MODEL] (supplied by [VENDOR]) to [OPERATOR]'s branches.
- **De-identification:** model, operator, vendor, people, courts, statutes, inquiries and places tokenised; not fully feasible (the case is widely known).

## Context (not episodes)
- [A+0 to A+16] [OPERATOR] acted as private prosecutor in several hundred prosecutions in which the system's evidence may have featured; other prosecuting bodies brought further cases (S13).
- [A+10] Trade press published accounts of branch operators who said the system produced unexplained shortfalls; a campaign group of former branch operators was formed and lobbied members of parliament (S5).
- [A+20.9] The trial judge in E7 referred material about the vendor's witness evidence to the national prosecution authority (S9).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+5] Civil claim and counterclaim in the civil courts (court level not reported), settled.**
- Brought by: [OPERATOR] sued [PLAINTIFF-1], a former branch operator, to recover branch equipment and alleged losses (about 25,000 in local currency); she counterclaimed, contending her dismissal was unlawful and that the system was unreliable. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: by the operator, recovery; by the defendant, damages and a finding that the system was at fault.
- Outcome: settled by [OPERATOR] before trial, with a confidentiality agreement; amount reported inconsistently. A jointly instructed IT expert had produced an interim report, never finalised.
- Stated reasons: the expert's interim report said the equipment installed at the branch was clearly defective in hardware, software or interfaces and that most errors in the fault logs could not have been caused by the operator. Later evidence to [INQUIRY-1] showed [OPERATOR] recognised the weakness of its case before settling.
- Relief applied to: [PLAINTIFF-1] only.
- Sources: S14.

E2. **[A+8] Civil claim for a branch shortfall, decided at trial in [COURT-1] (first-instance civil court).**
- Brought by: [OPERATOR] against [PLAINTIFF-2], a branch operator, who defended without a lawyer and counterclaimed. Funded or run by: defendant self-funded; [OPERATOR] used external solicitors and counsel.
- Sought: by the operator, about 25,000 in local currency for shortfalls shown by the system; by the defendant, a finding that the shortfalls were not real and were caused by system faults.
- Outcome: judgment for [OPERATOR]; counterclaim dismissed; the defendant was ordered to pay the sum and costs reported at over 300,000, and was made bankrupt. At A+26 he issued a new claim in [COURT-1] against [OPERATOR] and [VENDOR] to set the judgment aside as obtained by fraud (evidence of known system faults allegedly withheld); undecided at the end of the period.
- Stated reasons: the judge held the shortfall was real, finding that since the logic of the system was correct, the system had been working properly in all material respects; he accepted the vendor's and operator's witnesses and treated the burden as on the operator to show the accounts were wrong.
- Relief applied to: [PLAINTIFF-2] only.
- Sources: S15, S16.

E3. **[A+13 to A+16] Independent forensic review and mediation scheme set up by [OPERATOR] after pressure from members of parliament.**
- Brought by: [OPERATOR] appointed [ORG-1], a forensic accountancy firm, at the request of members of parliament and a branch operators' campaign group; a mediation scheme followed. Funded or run by: [OPERATOR] funded both.
- Sought: to investigate complaints that the system caused unexplained losses, then to review individual cases (about 136 accepted into the scheme) and mediate settlements.
- Outcome: an interim report (A+14) found no evidence of system-wide defects in the software but identified two software anomalies (bugs) affecting branches and said [OPERATOR] could have given more training and support. [OPERATOR] disputed parts of the findings. [OPERATOR] gave notice to end [ORG-1]'s engagement at A+16; [ORG-1]'s final report on individual cases was given to applicants and published at A+16. Few cases were resolved through mediation (number not verified).
- Stated reasons: the interim report's findings as above; [OPERATOR] disputed the reviewers' claims that the system's data was not always timely, accurate or complete.
- Relief applied to: individual applicants to the scheme where settlements were reached.
- Sources: S1, S2, S3.

E4. **[A+16 to A+17] Lawyer-led review commissioned by the new chair of [OPERATOR] at a minister's request.**
- Brought by: the incoming chair of [OPERATOR], after a government minister asked for the concerns to be examined; carried out by two senior barristers ([ORG-2]). Funded or run by: [OPERATOR].
- Sought: to examine [OPERATOR]'s handling of the complaints and of prosecutions based on the system's evidence.
- Outcome: report delivered to the chair at A+17; not disclosed to the minister or to the later litigants and not published until much later. It reported no evidence of a system-wide problem but said that the vendor could remotely access and alter branch accounts, possibly without branch operators' knowledge, and recommended further work.
- Stated reasons: as above; later academic and inquiry evidence criticised its limited evidence base.
- Relief applied to: none.
- Sources: S4.

E5. **[A+17 to A+20.9] Group litigation in [COURT-1] (first-instance civil court), with appeals to [COURT-2 (appellate)].**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-3] and over five hundred branch operators under a group litigation order. Funded or run by: a commercial litigation funder ([ORG-3]), led by a branch operators' campaign group.
- Sought: damages for losses (repaid shortfalls, lost businesses), declarations on the contract terms, and findings that the system was not reliable.
- Outcome: (a) [A+20.2] first trial on contract terms: the judge found the contract was relational, carrying duties of good faith, and that [OPERATOR] could not simply treat system figures as a debt; [OPERATOR]'s application to remove the judge for apparent bias was refused and [COURT-2 (appellate)] refused permission to appeal both that and the contract judgment. (b) [A+20.9] second trial on the system: the judge found the system had numerous bugs, errors and defects with the potential to cause apparent shortfalls in branch accounts, that the vendor had remote access to branch data, and that the system was far from robust in its earlier versions. (c) Settled in mediation for tens of millions in local currency, with no admission of liability; after funding and legal costs the claimants received a small fraction (reported at about a fifth of the settlement in total).
- Stated reasons: as found in (a) and (b).
- Relief applied to: the group claimants only.
- Sources: S5, S6, S7, S8, S9.

E6. **[A+21 onwards] Police criminal investigation into evidence given in prosecutions.**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1] (a large police force), after the referral noted in Context. Funded or run by: police.
- Sought: to investigate possible perjury and perverting the course of justice by people involved in the prosecutions (including vendor and operator staff); later widened.
- Outcome: by A+26 seven suspects and over forty persons of interest had been reported; some interviewed under caution; no charging decision by the end of the period. Police said charging decisions would wait for the final report of [INQUIRY-1].
- Stated reasons: not reported beyond the offences under consideration.
- Relief applied to: none to date.
- Sources: S17.

E7. **[A+21.2 onwards] Miscarriage-of-justice review and referrals by [AGENCY-2] (criminal cases review body).**
- Brought by: applications from convicted branch operators (from A+16). Funded or run by: [AGENCY-2] (statutory, government-funded).
- Sought: referral of convictions to the appeal courts.
- Outcome: 39 convictions referred at A+21.2 (the largest single referral the body had made); further referrals took the total to about 60 by A+22 and to about 77 later. Most referred convictions were then quashed (see E8, E9).
- Stated reasons: there was a real possibility the convictions would be held unsafe as an abuse of process, because they depended on the system's evidence in light of the group-litigation findings and failures in [OPERATOR]'s investigation and disclosure.
- Relief applied to: the referred individuals.
- Sources: S10, S13.

E8. **[A+21.9] Criminal appeals against lower-court convictions, [COURT-3] (criminal court hearing appeals from the lowest criminal courts).**
- Brought by: six former branch operators on [AGENCY-2] referral. Funded or run by: represented by a claimant law firm; funding not reported.
- Sought: quashing of convictions.
- Outcome: all six convictions quashed; [OPERATOR] did not contest the appeals.
- Stated reasons: as in E7; [OPERATOR] had said it would not oppose most of the referred appeals.
- Relief applied to: the six appellants.
- Sources: S11.

E9. **[A+22.3] Criminal appeals, [COURT-4 (criminal appellate)].**
- Brought by: 42 appellants (39 on [AGENCY-2] referral, others directly). Funded or run by: various defence firms; funding not reported.
- Sought: quashing of convictions for theft, fraud and false accounting.
- Outcome: 39 convictions quashed; three appeals dismissed. [OPERATOR] conceded most appeals on the first ground but contested the second.
- Stated reasons: the court held that in cases where the system's data was essential to the prosecution and there was no independent evidence of loss, the prosecutions were an abuse of process on both grounds: a fair trial was not possible, and prosecuting was an affront to the public conscience. It found that [OPERATOR] knew of serious questions about the system's reliability yet failed to investigate or disclose them, and in effect reversed the burden of proof by treating shortfalls as proof of loss. The three dismissed appeals had other evidence of guilt.
- Relief applied to: the 39 successful appellants.
- Sources: S12.

E10. **[A+22 to A+26] Further criminal appeals in [COURT-3] and [COURT-4 (criminal appellate)] (aggregated).**
- Brought by: further convicted branch operators, by [AGENCY-2] referral or direct appeal. Funded or run by: various; not reported.
- Sought: quashing of convictions.
- Outcome: further convictions quashed in batches (e.g. 12 more in one appellate judgment at A+22.8); [OPERATOR] reported that the total quashed by the courts reached 100 by A+25.1, including eight cases brought by other prosecutors. Some appeals were dismissed where independent evidence of loss existed.
- Stated reasons: same reasoning as E9 applied to each case.
- Relief applied to: the individual appellants.
- Sources: S12, S13.

E11. **[A+21.3 onwards] Compensation scheme for unconvicted branch operators outside the group litigation, run by [OPERATOR].**
- Brought by: [OPERATOR] (with government oversight), following the settlement in E5. Funded or run by: [OPERATOR]; independent panel assessed claims.
- Sought: to compensate current and former branch operators who repaid or suffered losses from shortfalls related to earlier versions of the system.
- Outcome: applications opened at A+21.3 and closed at A+21.9 (late applications later accepted); thousands of settlement offers made by A+25.4, most accepted. [COMMITTEE-3] and [INQUIRY-1] later criticised its speed and fairness (E12, E18).
- Stated reasons: redress following the group-litigation findings.
- Relief applied to: eligible applicants (thousands); excluded those convicted and the group claimants.
- Sources: S18, S19.

E12. **[A+22.4 onwards] Statutory public inquiry ([INQUIRY-1]).**
- Brought by: the government; converted from a non-statutory review (begun A+21.7) to a statutory inquiry at A+22.4, chaired by a retired senior judge. Funded or run by: government.
- Sought: to establish what went wrong with the system's implementation and failures, [OPERATOR]'s and [VENDOR]'s knowledge and conduct, the prosecutions, and redress.
- Outcome: interim report on compensation at A+24.5; first volume of the final report at A+26.5 on human impact and redress, with 19 recommendations. It found at least 13 people may have taken their own lives in connection with the matter, that senior and less senior staff of [OPERATOR] and [VENDOR] knew or should have known the system was capable of errors, and that the four compensation schemes had in many cases failed to deliver full, fair and prompt compensation. The second volume (causes) was pending at the end of the period.
- Stated reasons: as above.
- Relief applied to: recommendations to government (including compensation for close family members).
- Sources: S20, S19.

E13. **[A+23.1] Lower-house select committee inquiry (business) on compensation.**
- Brought by: [COMMITTEE-2] (lower-house business committee), with evidence sessions at A+22.9 to A+23.0. Funded or run by: parliament.
- Sought: to examine compensation for the group claimants, those with overturned convictions, and the shortfall scheme.
- Outcome: interim report with 29 conclusions and recommendations, including that the group claimants should receive compensation comparable to others. Government responded at A+23.3.
- Stated reasons: the group claimants had been excluded from the shortfall scheme and most of their settlement went to litigation costs.
- Relief applied to: recommendations to government.
- Sources: S21.

E14. **[A+23.2 onwards] Government compensation scheme for group-litigation claimants.**
- Brought by: the responsible government department. Funded or run by: government.
- Sought: to compensate the group claimants on a basis similar to non-group peers.
- Outcome: funding announced A+23.2; interim payments A+23.5; full scheme details A+23.9 (claims-based, alternative dispute resolution, independent facilitators).
- Stated reasons: the group claimants had received little after costs.
- Relief applied to: the group claimants without convictions.
- Sources: S18, S22.

E15. **[A+23.9 to A+25] Miscarriage-of-justice referrals and appeals in [STATE-A] (separate jurisdiction).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-3] (that jurisdiction's criminal cases review body), on applications from convicted branch operators. Funded or run by: [AGENCY-3].
- Sought: quashing of convictions by [COURT-5] (that jurisdiction's appeal court).
- Outcome: six cases referred at A+23.9; [COURT-5] quashed the convictions referred after the prosecution conceded each appeal; further cases under review. The review body held tens of names of people whose convictions may have relied on the system's evidence.
- Stated reasons: the prosecution conceded; the court set out the factors behind the concessions.
- Relief applied to: the individual appellants.
- Sources: S23.

E16. **[A+25.1] Act of the national parliament on compensation.**
- Brought by: the government, as a statute ([STATUTE-1]).
- Sought: to give the minister power to make compensation payments to affected branch operators.
- Outcome: enacted at A+25.1, in force at once.
- Stated reasons: not reported beyond the Act's purpose.
- Relief applied to: eligible branch operators under the schemes.
- Sources: S24.

E17. **[A+25.4 and A+25.5] Acts quashing convictions by statute in the national parliament and in [STATE-A].**
- Brought by: the government ([STATUTE-2], covering two jurisdictions) and [STATE-A]'s devolved government ([STATUTE-3]).
- Sought: to quash, in bulk, convictions for specified offences committed while the system was in use and prosecuted by [OPERATOR] or the national prosecution service, where not already considered by the appeal court.
- Outcome: both enacted; hundreds of convictions quashed on commencement. A government redress scheme for those with quashed convictions followed.
- Stated reasons: the scale of unsafe convictions and the time individual appeals would take.
- Relief applied to: all convictions meeting the statutory criteria (hundreds of people).
- Sources: S25, S26.

E18. **[A+26.0] Lower-house select committee report (business and trade) on redress.**
- Brought by: [COMMITTEE-3] (successor committee). Funded or run by: parliament.
- Sought: to assess progress on the compensation schemes.
- Outcome: report found redress had been a complete failure and that [OPERATOR] was not fit to run any of the schemes; recommended transferring the shortfall scheme to the government department, an independent advice body, binding time limits, removing the cap on legal costs and standard tariffs. The government rejected the transfer.
- Stated reasons: delays and low offers in the schemes.
- Relief applied to: recommendations to government.
- Sources: S27.
