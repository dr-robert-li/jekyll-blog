# Evidence packet G06

- **Domain:** environmental health standard-setting (national ambient air-quality standard for fine particles)
- **What the model does:** two long-running cohort studies ([MODEL]: [STUDY-1], a cohort of several thousand adults in a handful of cities, and [STUDY-2], a cohort of hundreds of thousands of adults across many cities) estimate how much long-term exposure to fine particulate air pollution raises death rates; the national regulator used these estimates as a principal basis for a new annual fine-particle standard.
- **Scale:** the standard applies nationally; hundreds of millions of residents, thousands of regulated pollution sources and hundreds of counties judged against it.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = the national environmental agency [AGENCY-1] of [COUNTRY-A] adopts a new fine-particle air-quality standard relying on [MODEL] for its estimates of mortality effects.
- **De-identification:** agency, studies, institutions, courts, statutes, parties and people tokenised; not fully feasible, as the case is well known.

## Context (not episodes)
- A+0: chairs of committees in [LEGISLATURE-1] and industry groups asked [AGENCY-1] and the study authors for the underlying data; the authors' institutions declined to release individual-level health records, citing confidentiality promises to participants (S1, S2).
- A+0: the head of government issued a memorandum on phased implementation of the new standard (S3).
- A+1 to A+7: a national academy committee, funded at [LEGISLATURE-1]'s direction, reviewed priorities for particulate research in a series of reports (S4).
- A+9: an extended follow-up of [STUDY-1] by its original investigators reported continued associations, and mortality declines where pollution fell (S5).
- A+16 to A+24: advocacy groups and commentators on both sides publicly described the data-access fight as being about undisclosed data behind regulation versus patient confidentiality (S9).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+0 to A+5] Litigation: petitions for review in [COURT-1 (appellate)], then [COURT-2 (supreme)], then back in [COURT-1].**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-1] (a trucking trade association) with other industry associations, business groups and several states. Funded or run by: industry trade associations (as reported).
- Sought: to set aside the fine-particle standard (and an ozone standard), arguing among other things that the agency's reading of the statute gave it unlimited discretion, that it should weigh costs, and that the scientific basis, including reliance on [MODEL], was inadequate.
- Outcome: [COURT-1] (A+2) held that the agency's interpretation of the statute delegated legislative power without an intelligible limiting principle and remanded; it also vacated a coarse-particle indicator. [COURT-2] (A+4) reversed on the delegation point and held that the statute bars considering implementation costs when setting standards; remanded. On remand [COURT-1] (A+5) rejected the remaining challenges and upheld the fine-particle standards.
- Stated reasons: [COURT-2]: the statute's requirement of a standard needed to protect public health with an adequate margin of safety supplied a sufficient limit. [COURT-1] on remand: the agency reasonably relied on the epidemiological studies even though the biological mechanism was not established, and was entitled to act on uncertain but credible evidence; it was not arbitrary in choosing the levels it did.
- Relief applied to: none for petitioners; standard remained in force nationally.
- Sources: S6, S7, S8.

E2. **[A+0 to A+3] Formal reanalysis by [ORG-1] (an independent research institute jointly funded by the agency and the motor-vehicle industry).**
- Brought by: [ORG-1], at the request of [AGENCY-1], members of [LEGISLATURE-1] and industry, with agreement of the original investigators to provide the data. Funded or run by: [ORG-1]; analysis by an independent team led by [PERSON-1], overseen by an expert panel.
- Sought: an audit of the data and an independent reanalysis of both studies.
- Outcome: the audit found the data were of generally high quality and the original results could be reproduced; extensive sensitivity tests found the associations between fine particles and mortality robust to alternative statistical models, though they were weakened when sulphur dioxide and spatial patterns were taken into account, and varied with education.
- Stated reasons: to answer criticism that the data and analyses had not been open to independent checking.
- Relief applied to: none (scientific report).
- Sources: S10.

E3. **[A+1 to A+2] Legislative act ([STATUTE-1]) and executive implementation.**
- Brought by: [LEGISLATURE-1], by a rider in an omnibus appropriations law sponsored by Senator [PERSON-2]; implemented by the central budget office [AGENCY-2]. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: public access, through the freedom-of-information law, to data from federally funded research.
- Outcome: enacted; the budget office revised its grants circular in A+2 to make data from federally funded research available on request where the published findings were used by the government in developing an action with the force and effect of law. Whether it reached [MODEL]'s existing data is not reported in sources reviewed.
- Stated reasons: public should be able to see data behind federally funded research used to justify regulation; the data dispute over [MODEL] was cited as the impetus.
- Relief applied to: recipients of federal research grants.
- Sources: S11, S12.

E4. **[A+9 to A+12] Litigation over the revised fine-particle standard in [COURT-1].**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-2] (farm and industry groups) and, separately, several states and public-health groups. Funded or run by: not reported.
- Sought: industry sought to set aside parts of the revised standards; states and health groups argued the annual fine-particle standard (left unchanged) was too weak given the long-term exposure evidence.
- Outcome: [COURT-1] remanded the annual fine-particle standard to the agency without vacating it, and rejected other challenges.
- Stated reasons: the agency had not adequately explained why the annual standard protected public health with an adequate margin of safety, given the long-term exposure studies and its scientific advisory committee's advice to lower it.
- Relief applied to: national standard (agency required to reconsider).
- Sources: S13.

E5. **[A+12] Formal reanalysis/extension by [ORG-1].**
- Brought by: [ORG-1]. Funded or run by: [ORG-1]; investigators including members of the earlier reanalysis team.
- Sought: extended follow-up and spatial analysis of [STUDY-2].
- Outcome: report confirmed associations between long-term fine-particle exposure and all-cause and cardiopulmonary mortality, with stronger associations in some intra-city analyses.
- Stated reasons: to update and test the robustness of the earlier findings with longer follow-up.
- Relief applied to: none (scientific report).
- Sources: S14.

E6. **[A+16] Subpoena by a committee of [LEGISLATURE-1].**
- Brought by: the science committee of the lower chamber of [LEGISLATURE-1], chaired by [PERSON-3], on a party-line vote. Funded or run by: the committee.
- Sought: all underlying data, including participants' health information, from both studies, to be given to the committee for evaluation by outside scientists.
- Outcome: the agency produced material the committee largely already had and did not produce the confidential participant-level data. Further enforcement not reported.
- Stated reasons: committee: regulations should not rest on data withheld from the public. Agency: release would breach confidentiality agreements with participants; the agency did not hold the data. Minority members' amendment to protect participant identities was rejected.
- Relief applied to: none.
- Sources: S9, S15.

E7. **[A+16 to A+17] Litigation over the further revised annual standard in [COURT-1].**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-3] (a manufacturers' association) with other business groups. Funded or run by: business associations.
- Sought: to vacate the lowered annual fine-particle standard.
- Outcome: petitions denied; standard upheld.
- Stated reasons: the agency reasonably relied on epidemiological studies (by then a broader body including multi-city and long-term studies) and reasonably concluded there was evidence of effects at lower concentrations; the court deferred to the agency's scientific judgment.
- Relief applied to: none for petitioners.
- Sources: S16.

E11. **[A+21 to A+24] Executive rulemaking by [AGENCY-1] ("transparency" rule).**
- Brought by: [AGENCY-1] under a new administration. Funded or run by: the agency.
- Sought: to require the agency to give greater weight to "pivotal" studies whose underlying data and models are available for independent validation, especially dose-response data, and less weight to others.
- Outcome: proposed (A+21), supplemented (A+23), finalised in the first days of A+24 and made effective on publication; vacated within weeks (see E12); text removed from the regulations later in A+24.
- Stated reasons: agency: transparency and reproducibility of science underlying significant regulations. Critics: it would exclude studies, such as [MODEL], that rely on confidential health records.
- Relief applied to: would have applied to the agency's significant regulatory actions and influential scientific information.
- Sources: S18, S19.

E12. **[A+24] Litigation in [COURT-3] (a federal district court).**
- Brought by: [PLAINTIFF-4] (an environmental organisation) with two regional environmental and citizens' groups. Funded or run by: the plaintiff organisations.
- Sought: to block and set aside the transparency rule.
- Outcome: the court first ruled the rule could not take immediate effect; then, on the agency's own unopposed motion under the new administration, vacated and remanded the rule.
- Stated reasons: the rule governed how the agency would weigh scientific evidence in decisions affecting the public, rather than the agency's internal housekeeping, so it could not be issued under the housekeeping statute it cited or made effective without the usual waiting period; the agency then conceded it lacked authority under that statute.
- Relief applied to: nationally (rule vacated).
- Sources: S20, S21.
