# Evidence packet G10

- **Domain:** welfare fraud detection (social security, benefits, tax and labour-law enforcement)
- **What the model does:** links and pseudonymises files held by several public bodies about residents of selected neighbourhoods, then applies a non-public risk model and indicators to flag addresses or persons as raising an elevated risk of benefit, tax or labour-law fraud; flagged persons are passed to agencies for investigation.
- **Scale:** each project covered whole neighbourhoods of a municipality; the total number of persons screened or flagged is not reported in the sources consulted.
- **Adoption:** A+0 = an implementing decree under a social-security administration statute entered into force, giving the data-linking instrument [MODEL] its legal basis (the statutory provision itself was adopted the year before).
- **De-identification:** names of the model, ministry, courts, bodies, places, statutes and parties are tokenised; de-identification is not fully feasible because the case is widely known.

## Context (not episodes)
- A-1 to A+0: before the decree was adopted, the national data-protection supervisor ([AGENCY-3]) advised against the draft in that form, citing an unclear legal basis, proportionality, and the risk that profiles built on negative traits would intrude on privacy more than needed; the government's senior legislative advisory body ([ADVISORY-1]) found the listed data categories so broad that hardly any personal data fell outside them. The minister said both opinions were taken into account (S2, S3).
- A+3 to A+4: a freedom-of-information request for the risk model was refused on the ground that disclosure would let offenders evade detection (S5).
- A+4: members of the lower house of [COUNTRY-A]'s parliament filed a motion asking for publication of the files, algorithms and analysis methods and a technical audit for discrimination; the junior minister advised against it, citing the pending lawsuit. Vote outcome not reported in the sources consulted (S5).
- A+4 to A+6: parliamentary written questions about individual projects; media coverage; a coalition of civil-society groups campaigned under a common banner (S6, S9).
- A+6 (after E3): the government tabled a broader data-sharing bill; the litigating coalition wrote that it was even less bounded than [MODEL] (S8).

## Episodes

E1. **[A+4 to A+6] Civil lawsuit against the State before a first-instance civil court ([COURT-1]).**
- Brought by: several civil-society bodies (a human-rights lawyers' section [ORG-1], a civil-liberties platform [ORG-2], a privacy foundation [ORG-3], a federation of mental-health practitioners [ORG-4], a national clients' council for social security [ORG-5]) and two individual writers; a national trade-union federation [ORG-6] joined as a party. Funded or run by: coordinated by a public-interest litigation project of [ORG-1]; funding not reported. An independent human-rights expert mandated by an intergovernmental body ([INTL-1]) filed an amicus brief at A+5.7 stating that the system posed significant potential threats to human rights, especially for the poorest.
- Sought: a declaration that the statutory provision and decree establishing [MODEL] breach the right to private life under a regional human-rights convention (and other norms), and that they be declared non-binding.
- Outcome: at A+6 the court held that the legislation breached the right to private life and declared the statutory provision and the decree chapter non-binding. The State did not appeal (see E3), so the judgment became final.
- Stated reasons: the court held that the State bears a special responsibility when applying new technologies and must strike a fair balance between their benefits and the intrusion into private life; the legislation was insufficiently transparent and verifiable, gave no information on the risk model, the indicators or how they were weighted, did not require that the people concerned be informed, and so provided insufficient safeguards against abuse and arbitrariness. The court noted that the instrument had been deployed in poorer neighbourhoods and that the risk of unintended discriminatory effects could not be checked. The court did not rule on the claimants' remaining grounds.
- Relief applied to: everyone; the legal basis of the instrument as a whole ceased to bind.
- Sources: S1, S4, S7, S8.

E2. **[A+5.5] Municipal executive decision to halt a project ([CITY-1] mayor and executive).**
- Brought by: the mayor of [CITY-1], in a letter to the city council, after two council parties (one in the governing coalition, one in opposition) called for an end to the city's use of [MODEL]. Funded or run by: not applicable.
- Sought: not applicable (executive decision).
- Outcome: the city stopped using [MODEL] in two neighbourhoods; preparation of planned home visits in the operational phase was postponed indefinitely.
- Stated reasons: it remained too unclear whether the system complied with privacy law; limited inspection capacity was also cited.
- Relief applied to: residents of the two project neighbourhoods.
- Sources: S6, S9.

E3. **[A+6.3] National government decision not to appeal and to stop using [MODEL] (executive act).**
- Brought by: the junior minister responsible for social affairs ([OPERATOR]), by letter to the lower house. Funded or run by: not applicable.
- Sought: not applicable.
- Outcome: the government did not appeal E1 and accepted the court's judgment; it reported that [MODEL] would not continue in its existing form, that no projects were running, and that no new projects would start.
- Stated reasons: the government stated it could agree with the court's judgment and accepted it.
- Relief applied to: everyone; all use of the instrument ended.
- Sources: S8, S10, S11.

E4. **[A+11.3] Draft government decree to delete the legal bases of [MODEL] from the implementing decree (executive act, at consultation stage).**
- Brought by: the ministry responsible for social affairs ([OPERATOR]). Funded or run by: not applicable.
- Sought: removal of the remaining legal bases for [MODEL] from the implementing decree, combined with new rules on proactive provision of benefits.
- Outcome: placed in public consultation at A+11.3; adoption and publication not confirmed in the sources consulted as of the cut-off.
- Stated reasons: not reported in the sources consulted beyond the decree's title (deletion of the bases for [MODEL]).
- Relief applied to: not applicable (repeal of a legal basis already declared non-binding).
- Sources: S12, S13.
