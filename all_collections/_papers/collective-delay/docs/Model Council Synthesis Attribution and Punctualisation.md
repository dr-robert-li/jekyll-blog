## Comprehensive Analysis

All three models agree on the core point. The paragraph's empirical anchors are broadly correct in chronology, but its two strongest theoretical moves are overstated. Those moves are the claim that a successful causal model becomes an untraceable obligatory passage point, and the claim that benefit and risk are "the same ANT process run in opposite directions". The three reviews reached this independently, using different evidence: Claude Fable 5.1 used the record of catastrophe, bank and epidemic models; GPT 6.1 Sol used legal and governance cases; Gemini 3.7 Flash used a political-economy argument about capital and openness. That makes the convergence reliable. Each model also turned the inevitability claim into a conditional one: opacity and gatekeeping follow from exclusive control, weak archives and lost practice, not from scale or update rate as such.

The disagreements are mostly about how far the corrections go, and the evidence decides most of them. On pharmacovigilance, Claude Fable 5.1's finding should be preferred. A 2024 study showed spontaneous reporting caught almost none of the rofecoxib infarction signal before the label change. So "pharmacovigilance detects signals routinely" and "rofecoxib shows V binds" should not be presented as one case. Gemini 3.7 Flash's claim of PRR/BCPNN signals for rofecoxib in 1998–2001 had no drug-specific source and should be discounted. On carriers, Claude Fable 5.1's "category error" and GPT 6.1 Sol's "potential carrier, not a realised cost transfer" point the same way. WWA and post-marketing surveillance are instruments that raise attributability. The carriers are the litigants, insurers and regulators who use them. The paragraph currently lists the two together.

On ANT fidelity, Gemini 3.7 Flash's "faithful" verdict checks the definitions. The other two check the claim's actual use and catch three slippages. First, "de-punctualise" is not Law's verb. Second, Callon's OPP is achieved through problematisation and lasts only while enrolment holds. Third, ANT's symmetry is methodological, not a claim that processes reverse. Claude Fable 5.1 adds a sharp internal point: the report itself cites Callon 1991 on irreversibility, which contradicts the "opposite directions" framing. The two replacements on offer, case-by-case reopening against all-at-once closure (Claude Fable 5.1) and simultaneous selective repackaging (GPT 6.1 Sol), fit together. The first names the asymmetry, and the second names the political question: who gets inspection rights.

On atrophy, the council splits by counterexample set, and the split itself tells you something. Individual deskilling has evidence behind it where humans leave the loop: colonoscopy, maths students, novice coders. It is contradicted where humans keep doing the judgement: weather forecasters, patent lawyers, structured medical training. That suggests a better P23: atrophy depends on whether institutions keep humans in the loop, with forecasting as a negative control. On access disputes, Gemini 3.7 Flash reads the cases as displacement. Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT 6.1 Sol traced what happened next and found that winning access led back to causal contestation (HEI's reanalysis of the Six Cities study, CovidSim, Pickett). P24 should therefore be a sequence test, not a one-way shift.

The most valuable single-model findings sit with Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT 6.1 Sol. In Lliuya, the court subordinated the claimant's "climate factor" to its own experts. That suggests the real obligatory passage point may be the forum's control over which evidence it accepts, not the model's operators. ECMWF's bust-tracing shows that a continuously updating model can be traced when it is archived. The Florida commission and the Basel output floor show contestability institutions being built in response to model authority, which undercuts "mitigations are the first to go". GPT 6.1 Sol's point that Lliuya came before its key attribution study means P22's treatment is endogenous and needs a design that handles that. Its randomised counter-evidence on atrophy means P23 has to treat gains as falsifying evidence, not reclassify them.

Recommendation: keep the paragraph's structure and its core distinction, that attribution shifts perception without supplying veto conditions. Make four changes. First, separate instruments from carriers. Second, drop pharmacovigilance as a carrier anchor and keep rofecoxib only as the "V binds" case. Third, replace the symmetry sentence with an explicit asymmetry, conditioned on proprietary control and weak archives. Fourth, restate P22–P24 with defined dates, controls and falsifiers: P22 with low-attribution hazard classes as controls; P23 with human-in-the-loop domains as negative controls; P24 as a sequence test where access is either granted or refused. Claude Fable 5.1's rewritten paragraph is the strongest starting draft. It is the only one that includes the Hamm "climate factor" finding and the case-by-case versus all-at-once asymmetry. GPT 6.1 Sol's version is tighter on conditionality. Gemini 3.7 Flash's version keeps the symmetry thesis this synthesis advises against.

## Suggested Paragraph (merged)

Attribution tools loosen accreted wickedness in one specific way. They make distant links easier to attribute, which lets carriers such as litigants, insurers and regulators move downstream costs onto veto holders. Extreme-event attribution has won recognition of emitter liability in principle but no damages award. In Lliuya v RWE the court put its own site experts ahead of the claimant's statistical attribution. Rofecoxib stayed on the market four years after its trial signal. Attribution shifts perception, not the veto conditions under which root reform occurs. Success brings a separate risk. A model with closed data and exclusive control, adopted as the accepted basis for attribution, becomes a new black box. It is too large to read and is re-estimated faster than its outputs can be contested unless every version is archived. Where institutions let it, it displaces the human skill of tracing. Its controllers then hold the inputs and acceptance standards that fine-grained carriers must pass through. The two movements are not one process reversed. Carriers reopen black boxes case by case, while a shared model closes one over all cases at once. The counter-move is institutional: audit commissions, output floors, mandated access. Forward tests: P22 predicts that carriers accelerate once attribution is available while root reform still waits for a veto-side trigger. P23 predicts that unassisted tracing declines where humans leave the loop and holds where they keep doing the judgement. P24 predicts that after a shared model is adopted, disputes move first to access and acceptance standards, and return to causes only where access is won.

### Corrected paragraph

> Attribution tools loosen accreted wickedness in one specific way: they make distant causal links more attributable, which lets carriers (litigants, insurers, regulators) move downstream costs onto veto holders. The tools are not themselves carriers, and the record shows the limits. In *Lliuya v RWE* the court accepted emitter liability in principle on property-law grounds, but decided the case on its own experts' estimate of the site risk and never relied on the attribution evidence; no damages followed. The suit was filed in 2015, before the key attribution study of the glacier, so carriers can demand the science rather than follow it. Rofecoxib's cardiovascular signal appeared in a trial in 2000 and the drug was withdrawn in 2004: detection came four years before action. Attribution shifts perception, not the veto conditions under which root reform occurs.
>
> Success carries a separate, conditional risk. A model under exclusive control and without archived versions, adopted as the accepted basis for attribution, becomes a new black box: too large to read, re-estimated faster than its outputs can be contested, and, where institutions allow it, a replacement for the human practice of tracing. Its controllers then hold the inputs and acceptance standards that carriers must pass through. The conditions do the work. ECMWF characterised 584 European forecast busts by keeping a frozen model cycle beside its updating operational system, so tracing survived because a fixed reference was paid for. Skill loss is conditional too: in a randomised trial, AI access raised senior patent lawyers' unaided performance and left juniors' unchanged on average. The two movements are not one process reversed. Carriers reopen black boxes case by case; a shared model closes one over all cases at once. The counter-moves are institutional: archived versions, audit bodies, mandated access.
