# Attribution, blackboxing and the institutions of contestability
## Independent peer review of the Part H claim

**Reviewer:** GPT 6.1 Sol  
**Evidence checked:** 19 October 2026  
**Recommendation:** Major revision; retain the institutional-dependence hypothesis, reject the proposed inevitability.

### Overall assessment

The defensible contribution is a **conditional account of how attribution can redistribute power while creating new dependencies**, not a demonstrated law that successful causal modelling necessarily destroys traceability. Law describes punctualisation as a useful, precarious simplification, and Callon describes obligatory passage points as achievements that can fail—not automatic consequences of technical scale or adoption ([Law, 1992, especially the “Punctualisation and Resourcing” section](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf); [Callon, 1986](https://research.engineering.nyu.edu/~jbain/scitechsoc/readings/86Callon.pdf)).

Three corrections are especially important:

1. **The empirical anchors demonstrate different things.** WWA produces probabilistic hazard attribution; spontaneous-report pharmacovigilance produces signals requiring causal assessment; *Lliuya* establishes legal availability in principle but no successful transfer of flood-prevention costs to RWE ([Philip et al., 2020](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/6/177/2020/); [Cutroneo et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12443087/); [OLG Hamm, judgment of 28 May 2025](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html)).
2. **“Same process in opposite directions” is too neat.** Attribution itself builds a new network of validated measurements, expert representatives and legally usable statements while reopening selected relations elsewhere; it need not first dissolve a veto coalition and only later construct a black box ([Law, 1992](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf); [Jasanoff, 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2199272/)).
3. **The atrophy evidence is mixed, not merely incomplete.** There are genuine unassisted-performance deficits, but also randomized evidence of improved unassisted professional judgment after AI exposure and experimental evidence of improved unaided medical diagnosis ([Budzyń et al., 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40816301/); [Autor et al., September 2026, revised October 2026, working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35720); [He et al., arXiv version 4, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16411)).

I reviewed the three supplied context documents, with particular attention to H.1–H.5, Appendix D and the ANT mapping. The full P23 is substantially more careful than the compressed paragraph: it already separates failed skill acquisition, subsequent atrophy, engagement and fiscal maintenance, and explicitly excludes immediate automation bias as proof of atrophy. The recommendations below sharpen that existing structure rather than replace it with a simpler one.

## 1. Empirical anchors: largely accurate chronology, overextended inference

### 1.1 World Weather Attribution: accurate institution, wrong model of institutional dependence

The founding date **2014** is correct according to WWA itself, notwithstanding a partner page that uses 2015; WWA distinguishes formation from the event-attribution work begun at its first physical meeting in summer 2015 ([WWA About](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/about/); [WWA, “Pathways and pitfalls”](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/pathways-and-pitfalls-in-extreme-event-attribution/)).

The claim of more than 100 rapid studies and a published eight-step protocol is supported; WWA also explicitly says rapid studies precede individual peer review while relying on peer-reviewed methods, so Appendix D’s uncertainty about that policy can now be resolved using the organization’s own explanation ([WWA Methods](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/methods/); [WWA FAQs](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/faqs/); [Philip et al., 2020](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/6/177/2020/)).

**What WWA establishes:** observational analysis, multiple evaluated climate models, counterfactual comparisons, uncertainty synthesis and vulnerability/exposure assessment can make anthropogenic contributions to particular hazard classes more legible ([Philip et al., 2020](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/6/177/2020/)).

**What it does not establish:** an event probability ratio is not an estimate of an individual company’s liability, a direct allocation of actual losses, or evidence that a veto coalition has dissolved; WWA’s protocol distinguishes quantitative hazard attribution from the separate analysis of exposure and vulnerability ([Philip et al., 2020](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/6/177/2020/)).

WWA is also an awkward example for the proposed transition to an inscrutable universal causal engine: its protocol is explicitly multi-model and multi-method, requires reproducibility documentation, and treats incompatible observations and model trends as grounds for withholding synthesis rather than concealing disagreement ([Philip et al., 2020](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/6/177/2020/)).

Most distinctively, Friederike Otto explicitly explains why WWA does **not** simply automate hazard analysis or hand it to AI: model–observation agreement, physical consistency and judgment about misleading numbers require experienced human interpretation ([Otto, 30 October 2024](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/can-we-come-up-with-a-meaningful-number/)).

**Reviewer’s inference:** WWA supports the proposition that organized expertise can create actionable evidence; it does not yet support the proposition that successful attribution replaces—and thereby erodes—the relevant human expertise. Those are different empirical propositions.

### 1.2 *Lliuya v RWE*: liability available in principle, no realized liability

The primary authority is **OLG Hamm, 5 U 15/17, 28 May 2025**, not either litigant’s characterization of its victory; the court dismissed the appeal, ordered the claimant to bear appellate costs and did not admit revision ([original judgment](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html)).

The court held that climate-related liability under §1004 BGB is **not categorically excluded** by cumulative emissions, geographic distance or the claim that climate change belongs exclusively to political decision-making ([judgment, headnotes and section B.I.2](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html)).

The crucial distinction is between a legally sufficient pleaded causal theory and proof of that theory: the judgment uses formulations including “Diese Behauptung als zutreffend unterstellt” when assessing causation, and finds the claim admissible and sufficiently pleaded but unfounded because the necessary concrete property threat was not proved ([judgment, B.I.2.c.cc and B.I.2.d](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html)).

The accepted estimate was roughly **1% probability over 30 years of lake water reaching the claimant’s property**, with insufficient severity to establish the requisite concrete threat; this is not a judicial estimate of the probability that RWE caused climate change or of the probability of flooding anywhere in Huaraz ([judgment, B.I.2.d](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html); [court press release](https://www.justiz.nrw.de/presse/2025-05-28-0)).

There was substantial scientific adjudication of local flood danger, including disagreement over a proposed “climate factor,” so **“the court never assessed attribution evidence” should be narrowed to “the court did not determine the defendant-to-climate-impact attribution chain on its evidentiary merits”** ([judgment, B.I.2.d.cc](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html); [Cui and Zhang, 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02646811.2026.2631385)).

A useful factual update: the original **0.47%** emissions-share claim was revised to **0.38%** during the March 2025 hearing; these were asserted industrial-emissions shares, not a court-validated fraction of this property’s damage ([judgment, factual history and B.I.2.c.cc](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html)).

**Implication:** “opened legal standing” is imprecise; **“recognized that a transboundary property-protection claim against a major emitter is legally available in principle”** is better, because standing, admissibility, substantive liability and a successful remedy are separate questions ([judgment](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html); [Walker-Crawford et al., June 2026](https://www.lse.ac.uk/granthaminstitute/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Constructing-the-causal-chain_final.pdf)).

The case is evidence of a **potential liability carrier**, not of downstream financial harm successfully pushed onto a veto holder; moreover, the litigation began in 2015 while an important glacier-attribution study appeared in 2021, illustrating that legal mobilization can precede—and demand—specific attribution research ([Sabin Center case record](https://www.climatecasechart.com/document/luciano-lliuya-v-rwe-ag_dd33); [LSE account of the case](https://www.lse.ac.uk/research/research-for-the-world/sustainability/climate-lawsuits-global-warming-peru)).

That temporal direction matters for P22: science need not be the initiating event in the science–carrier–reform sequence.

### 1.3 Pharmacovigilance and rofecoxib: keep detection separate from causal confirmation

The historical use of proportional reporting ratios and Bayesian signal detection is sound, including the WHO programme’s pioneering Bayesian work in 1998; however, disproportionality identifies unusually frequent **reporting**, not causation, incidence or a population risk estimate ([CIOMS Working Group VIII, *Practical Aspects of Signal Detection*](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/WG8-Signal-Detection.pdf); [Cutroneo et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12443087/)).

Clinical review, biological plausibility, trials and analytical epidemiology remain necessary parts of signal assessment, and case narratives needed for validation can be unavailable in public structured exports ([Cutroneo et al., 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12443087/)).

The rofecoxib chronology is substantially correct:

| Date | Verified event and qualification |
|---|---|
| 20 May 1999 | FDA initially approved Vioxx ([FDA approval history](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=021042)). |
| 2000 | VIGOR showed excess myocardial infarction against naproxen; the proposed explanation was naproxen cardioprotection rather than rofecoxib toxicity, not an established resolution of the causal question ([Jüni et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15582059/)). |
| 8 February 2001 | An FDA advisory committee publicly reviewed VIGOR data ([Villalba and Witter’s FDA-affiliated correspondence, December 2004](https://cmgm-new.stanford.edu/biochem/biochem230/papers2005/week1/Kim_NEJM.pdf)). |
| 11 April 2002 | FDA approved revised labeling; the label describes excess serious cardiovascular thrombotic events, largely myocardial infarction, with rofecoxib 50 mg daily versus naproxen ([FDA approval history](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cder/daf/index.cfm?event=overview.process&ApplNo=021042); [2002 label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2002/21042s7lbl.pdf)). |
| 30 September 2004 | Merck voluntarily withdrew Vioxx after APPROVe, a placebo-controlled trial of 25 mg daily showing approximately doubled serious cardiovascular risk; this was not an FDA-ordered withdrawal ([FDA decisional memorandum, April 2005](https://www.fda.gov/media/74279/download)). |
| Late 2004 | Jüni et al.’s retrospective cumulative meta-analysis found that an increased MI risk was already statistically evident by the end of 2000—RR 2.30, 95% CI 1.22–4.33—and concluded that withdrawal should have occurred earlier ([Jüni et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15582059/)). |

The often-used figure of approximately **80 million exposed people** is an estimate, not a count of injuries; “an estimated 80 million” is preferable to “more than 80 million” ([Jüni et al., original article](https://cmgm-new.stanford.edu/biochem/biochem230/papers2005/week1/Juni_Lancet.pdf)).

**The important inferential correction:** VIGOR, APPROVe and the cumulative meta-analysis are not demonstrations that spontaneous-report data-mining algorithms discovered the decisive rofecoxib risk; presenting them as one seamless causal-AI success story conflates different evidence-generating technologies ([FDA memorandum](https://www.fda.gov/media/74279/download); [Jüni et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15582059/); [CIOMS report](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/WG8-Signal-Detection.pdf)).

The sequence supports **“credible evidence can coexist with delayed effective action”**, but cannot by itself establish that a specific shift in elite exposure, rather than the placebo-controlled evidence, alternative treatments, regulatory pressure or anticipated liability, was the necessary cause of withdrawal ([FDA memorandum](https://www.fda.gov/media/74279/download); [Jüni et al., 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15582059/)).

Finally, withdrawal stops the putative root exposure to this product; calling it “not root reform” because it did not transform pharmaceutical regulation would change the outcome definition after the fact. P22 must define the root at a fixed level before observing the result.

## 2. ANT: useful concepts, several stretched mappings

| Concept | Faithful use | Necessary correction |
|---|---|---|
| **Punctualisation, Law 1992** | A heterogeneous network appears and acts as one entity; routine model outputs can conceal the laboratories, data, assumptions and institutions producing them ([Law](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf)). | The thing being punctualised is most clearly **the model-producing sociotechnical network**, not literally the entire real-world causal chain it represents; Law also stresses usefulness, precarity and contestability, not permanent inability to reopen it ([Law](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf)). |
| **De-punctualisation** | A challenge can expose concealed associations sustaining an apparently unified actor ([Law’s breakdown examples](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf)). | Moving a financial cost onto RWE need not expose the internal associations of RWE’s veto coalition; **cost internalization, coalition change and reopening a black box require distinct observations**, rather than treating one as evidence of the others ([Law](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf); [Hamm judgment](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html)). |
| **Blackboxing, Latour** | Successful technical work can disappear behind accepted inputs and outputs; the cited formulation from *Pandora’s Hope*, p. 304, is accurately reproduced in scholarly discussion ([Oxford Academic discussion of Latour](https://academic.oup.com/book/6695/chapter/150753581); [scholarly quotation with page reference](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2042753017692429)). | Socially taking a result for granted is not equivalent to computational impossibility, secrecy, lost provenance or human deskilling; those are additional mechanisms requiring additional evidence ([Law](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf); [Burrell, 2016](https://www.ischool.berkeley.edu/research/publications/2016/how-machine-thinks-understanding-opacity-machine-learning-algorithms)). |
| **Obligatory passage point, Callon 1986** | Operators can become indispensable when the recognized route to others’ goals requires their programme, evidence or authorization ([Callon](https://research.engineering.nyu.edu/~jbain/scitechsoc/readings/86Callon.pdf)). | Widespread adoption alone does not establish indispensability; demonstrate that alternatives cannot obtain recognition, that operators can block access or acceptable evidence, and that this dependency is maintained through enrollment and representation ([Callon](https://research.engineering.nyu.edu/~jbain/scitechsoc/readings/86Callon.pdf)). |
| **Generalized symmetry** | Technical and social controversies should be described with a common repertoire, without deciding in advance which actors’ accounts deserve explanation ([Callon](https://research.engineering.nyu.edu/~jbain/scitechsoc/readings/86Callon.pdf)). | This is **not** a theorem that emancipation and domination are inverse processes or equal effects; Law explicitly distinguishes analytical symmetry from ethical equivalence and recognizes real inequalities in materials and methods ([Law](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf)). |

The report’s more extended ANT mapping is strongest where it asks who assembles the model, who speaks through it and what makes its authority durable; it is weakest where contingent achievements become automatic properties of “successful causal AI,” contrary to ANT’s insistence on locally maintained and contested arrangements ([Law, 1992](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf); [Callon, 1986](https://research.engineering.nyu.edu/~jbain/scitechsoc/readings/86Callon.pdf)).

**A further conceptual caution:** methodological relationality does not require treating the difference between cause and effect as moot; Callon’s symmetry instructs the analyst how to describe controversies, not to eliminate distinctions needed to test whether a particular intervention produces a particular consequence ([Callon, 1986](https://research.engineering.nyu.edu/~jbain/scitechsoc/readings/86Callon.pdf)).

## 3. Replace the symmetry thesis with a theory of contestability

### 3.1 Opening one network often requires closing another

In my assessment, the best ANT formulation is **simultaneous and selective repackaging**, not a reversal: a scientifically stabilized attribution statement can reopen relations of responsibility while blackboxing the measurement and inference that make the statement portable ([Law, 1992](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf); [WWA protocol’s production of scientific and policy summaries](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/6/177/2020/)).

The central political question is therefore **whose associations become inspectable, and whose expertise gains authority to inspect them**. An insurer, court, public laboratory and proprietary vendor can all carry an attribution while distributing inspection rights and burdens very differently.

### 3.2 Three counterweights to technological inevitability

**First: update frequency and historical traceability are separable.** A historical output can be reconstructed from its retained model state, inputs, preprocessing, software environment and applicable decision rules even if a successor model now exists; the real question is whether those materials are retained, accessible and affordable to execute, not whether updating occurred. As a practical governance counterexample, FDA’s change-control framework explicitly combines iterative AI-device improvement with specified modification protocols, validation, impact assessment, transparency and mechanisms to stop or revert failed changes ([FDA August 2025 final guidance](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/marketing-submission-recommendations-predetermined-change-control-plan-artificial-intelligence); [FDA/Health Canada/MHRA guiding principles](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/artificial-intelligence-enabled-medical-devices/predetermined-change-control-plans-machine-learning-enabled-medical-devices-guiding-principles)).

Reconstruction is not the same as causal validity or human comprehension: replay can faithfully reproduce a wrong result. Conversely, model stasis does not guarantee intelligibility. These distinctions make “frozen and traceable versus updating and untraceable” a false binary, although fast updates can increase documentation and assurance costs.

**Second: scale is not a sufficient measure of contestability.** Burrell distinguishes secrecy, technical illiteracy and opacity arising from algorithmic characteristics and scale; an explicit graph does not automatically remove the first two, and inability to mentally traverse every node does not establish inability to challenge a consequential subclaim ([Burrell, 2016](https://www.ischool.berkeley.edu/research/publications/2016/how-machine-thinks-understanding-opacity-machine-learning-algorithms)).

The appropriate audit target might be the outcome-sensitive assumptions, a local pathway, a counterfactual comparison or robustness across independently constructed models—not exhaustive inspection by one person.

**Third: open infrastructure provides a real alternative architecture, although not a guarantee of equal power.** WWA publishes a reproducible protocol and documents component-level disagreement; EPA’s BenMAP-CE is an open-source health-impact platform allowing alternative data and concentration-response relationships; Oasis provides an openly licensed, modular catastrophe-model execution framework supporting multiple suppliers and independent deployment ([Philip et al., 2020](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/6/177/2020/); [EPA BenMAP](https://www.epa.gov/benmap); [Oasis platform documentation](https://oasislmf.org/our-modelling-platform)).

These are **architectural counterexamples to inevitable exclusive operator control**, not measured demonstrations that gatekeeping has disappeared; an open protocol is not necessarily an open implementation, and an open execution platform does not make every supplier’s model or dataset open ([WWA protocol’s separate data-availability statement](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/6/177/2020/); [Oasis description of models from vendors and research groups](https://oasislmf.org/our-modelling-platform)).

### 3.3 The missing asymmetries

I recommend explicitly distinguishing:

- **Power to publish** from power to compel a court, regulator or insurer to recognize the publication.
- **Access to code** from access to data, historical versions, compute, case narratives and qualified experts.
- **Interpretability** from empirical validity, and both from legal admissibility.
- **Individual skill** from organization-wide investigative capacity.
- **The ability to inspect** from an enforceable right to challenge, receive a response and obtain correction.
- **Model dependence** from dependence on a particular operator.

The legal contrast is concrete: *Loomis* permitted constrained use of a proprietary risk assessment despite non-disclosure of its internal weighting, whereas *Pickett* required protected disclosure of proprietary DNA-analysis code and documentation to permit a reliability challenge ([Wisconsin Supreme Court, *Loomis*, 2016](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/wi-supreme-court/1742124.html); [New Jersey courts, *Pickett*, 2021](https://www.njcourts.gov/court-opinion/state-of-new-jersey-vs-corey-pickett-17-07-0470-hudson-county-and-statewide-published)).

Those are predictive/forensic analogues, not direct tests of causal-AI attribution, but they show why the surrounding rights and expert ecosystem cannot be derived from technical complexity alone ([*Loomis*](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/wi-supreme-court/1742124.html); [*Pickett*](https://www.njcourts.gov/court-opinion/state-of-new-jersey-vs-corey-pickett-17-07-0470-hudson-county-and-statewide-published)).

## 4. Atrophy of tracing: plausible mechanism, mixed outcomes and an unproved bridge

| Evidence | Finding | What it can and cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Bainbridge, 1983, “Ironies of Automation”** | Automation can remove routine practice while reserving difficult takeover and diagnosis for humans; the paper also advocates maintaining skills and simulator practice ([original article](https://davidjusth.com/s/Ironies-of-Automation_Bainbridge_1983.pdf)). | Strong mechanistic precedent, not a longitudinal estimate of causal-investigation skill loss or evidence that mitigation must fail ([Bainbridge](https://davidjusth.com/s/Ironies-of-Automation_Bainbridge_1983.pdf)). |
| **Goddard, Roudsari and Wyatt, 2012** | A 74-study systematic review identifies automation bias, workload and design moderators, and possible mitigators including training, accountability and presenting information rather than recommendations ([JAMIA review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21685142/)). | Supports immediate overreliance, not by itself durable unassisted skill loss; also qualifies a blanket statement that training or design cannot help ([review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21685142/)). |
| **Casner et al., 2014** | In 16 airline pilots, instrument scanning and control were mostly retained, but unaided navigation, sequencing and failure recognition showed greater problems associated with disengagement ([study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25509828/)). | Supports task-specific vulnerability rather than uniform deskilling; the small simulator study does not establish a universal decay rate ([study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25509828/)). |
| **Budzyń et al., 2025** | Unassisted adenoma detection fell from 28.4% to 22.4% after AI introduction in four Polish centres; the comparison was retrospective and spanned three months before and three months after implementation ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40816301/)). | A concerning unassisted-performance association, not proof of irreversible skill loss, a 12-month effect, or a randomized comparison of clinicians’ prior AI exposure; the investigators themselves call it hypothesis-generating ([University of Oslo investigators’ explanation](https://www.med.uio.no/helsam/english/research/groups/clinical-effectiveness/news/2025/ai-colonoscopy.html)). |
| **Bastani et al., 2025** | In a field experiment involving nearly 1,000 mathematics students, generic GPT access improved assisted practice but reduced subsequent unassisted exam grades by 17% relative to control; a guarded tutor largely eliminated that disadvantage ([PNAS study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/)). | Evidence of impaired acquisition under a particular design, not decay of established expert ability; importantly, the mitigation is part of the observed result, not just an untested recommendation ([study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/)). |
| **He et al., 2025, preprint v4** | Two randomized experiments involved 576 medical students; Study 2 reports that prior AI-assisted practice improved subsequent unaided diagnosis by 9.9 percentage points over control ([current arXiv abstract and version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16411)). | A direct counterexample to inevitable failure of novice skill formation, but a short-term diagnostic-training result, not evidence of durable protection over years ([preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16411)). |
| **Autor et al., 2026, working paper** | A three-month randomized trial assigned 133 patent lawyers to AI access or control; among 91 completing the unassisted redlining assessment, the estimated effect was +0.32 SD overall, +0.45 SD for seniors and −0.03 SD for juniors ([NBER paper](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35720/w35720.pdf)). | Genuine contrary evidence for established professionals, though from a selected, Google-funded sample with no baseline skills test, incomplete endline participation and possible AI use during nominally unassisted assessment; excluding flagged submissions weakens statistical precision ([paper and robustness analysis](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35720/w35720.pdf); [funding disclosures](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35720)). |

**Verification caveat:** Budzyń et al. has a registered correction, DOI 10.1016/S2468-1253(25)00294-8; I verified its existence but could not read its contents, so the numbers above are those in the currently indexed abstract, not an independently checked corrected full text ([PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40816301/)).

The additional Shen–Tamkin programming experiment strengthens the impaired-acquisition branch: developers learning an unfamiliar library with AI performed worse on an immediate comprehension/debugging assessment, while the authors identify cognitively engaged usage patterns associated with preserved learning; it remains a preprint and the usage-pattern moderation is not equivalent to separately randomized engagement treatments ([Shen and Tamkin, 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.20245)).

### The missing bridge

None of the studies above directly establishes that **policy institutions lose their ability to reconstruct and challenge cross-domain causal attributions**. The colonoscopy result primarily concerns detection, the education experiments concern acquiring new skills, and the lawyer experiment concerns professional judgment; transfer to institutional causal tracing must therefore remain a hypothesis ([Budzyń et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40816301/); [Bastani et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12232635/); [Autor et al.](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35720)).

I would distinguish four outcomes in P23:

1. **Failed formation:** novices never acquire a capability.
2. **Atrophy:** previously demonstrated capability declines.
3. **Temporary disengagement:** capability returns after a short reorientation or incentive change.
4. **Institutional hollowing:** staff, independent datasets, funding or practice opportunities disappear.

The decisive outcome should also not be solely unaided mental performance: if no person could previously trace the entire network, the relevant counterfactual is a human investigative team with its previous tools, not an imaginary unaided omniscient investigator.

## 5. “Disputes shift from causes to access”: plausible addition, weak displacement thesis

The strongest supporting examples are proprietary forensic or risk systems, where model access becomes a due-process issue; however, in *Loomis* and *Pickett*, access is sought **in order to contest validity and reliability**, so access and substance coexist rather than replace one another ([*Loomis*](https://caselaw.findlaw.com/court/wi-supreme-court/1742124.html); [*Pickett*](https://www.njcourts.gov/court-opinion/state-of-new-jersey-vs-corey-pickett-17-07-0470-hudson-county-and-statewide-published)).

The DSA is a useful access-governance analogue, but it does not meet P24’s key antecedent of one shared causal model: the 2025 delegated regime concerns independent researchers’ access to platform data for studying systemic risks across platforms and mitigation measures ([European Commission, 2 July 2025](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-adopts-delegated-act-data-access-under-digital-services-act)).

There is a closely relevant contrary case: van Beek, Oomen and Du’s study of the Shell litigation finds sustained contestation over the credibility, relevance and normative foundations of shared IPCC/IEA model scenarios, including disagreements about translating global pathways into company-specific duties, not simply who could access the models ([*Environmental Science & Policy*, 2025](https://research-portal.uu.nl/ws/files/272068218/Lisette_et_al_paper.pdf)).

Similarly, *Lliuya* turned on an evidential and normative threshold for concrete property risk despite a legally available causal theory; model availability does not settle what risk level warrants an injunction or how responsibility should be allocated ([Hamm judgment](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html)).

Jasanoff supplies the deeper alternative: courts and adversarial experts can **produce and refine causal evidence through litigation**, rather than only receive already settled facts from model operators; this makes the legal system a possible source of renewed tracing capacity as well as a gatekeeper ([Jasanoff, 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2199272/)).

**Recommended claim:** concentrated model authority can add access, audit and representation disputes; whether those disputes displace causal and distributive controversy is an open empirical question.

## 6. Sharper P22–P24: separate mechanisms, preregister failures

The proposals below are research-design recommendations, not established empirical thresholds. Numerical cutoffs are examples to be finalized through power calculations and domain consultation before data are inspected.

### P22: attribution, carriers and reform

**Problem:** a first attribution publication is not an exogenous treatment; litigation demand can induce the study, severe events can induce both, and “root reform” or “carrier” can be broadened until any outcome fits. The *Lliuya* chronology and the wider literature on litigation-generated science make this especially salient ([LSE case account](https://www.lse.ac.uk/research/research-for-the-world/sustainability/climate-lawsuits-global-warming-peru); [Jasanoff, 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2199272/)).

**Proposed operationalization for 2028–2030:**

- Register a cohort of hazard–jurisdiction pairs by the end of 2027, including hazards without later litigation or reform.
- Define treatment as the first **independently reproducible, decision-relevant attribution capability**, distinguishing general method availability, event-specific publication and legally usable expert evidence.
- Identify carriers as organizations with pre-existing leverage; separately date their **mobilization**, a filed claim, admissibility, a binding order, settlement or realized repricing.
- Define root reform ex ante by a domain-specific reduction in the originating exposure or practice, not by whether a broad social transformation occurred.
- Estimate 24-month cumulative incidence of carrier mobilization, implementation and root reform, with hazard-level exposure and contemporaneous events recorded.
- Measure veto conditions before treatment: formal blocking rights, incumbent rents, coalition commitments, elite exposure and substitute costs.
- Allow attribution to **change** veto conditions; do not control away that post-treatment mediator while claiming to estimate attribution’s total effect.

**Sharper statements:**

> **P22a:** Attribution capability increases carrier mobilization within 24 months relative to comparable not-yet-treated units.
>
> **P22b:** Its effect on implemented root reform is smaller where independently measured pre-treatment veto strength is higher.

For example, preregister a minimum meaningful carrier effect of +10 percentage points at 24 months: an adequately precise estimate whose upper confidence bound is below that level disconfirms the proposed substantive acceleration. A positive, meaningful reform effect in high-veto units **without an independently observed veto change** challenges P22b; if credible controls are unavailable, report a chronology/process-tracing study rather than a causal event study.

Do not require “reform without any carrier” as the sole disconfirmation: if every enforcing institution counts as a carrier, that condition is impossible by definition.

### P23: tracing skill and institutional capacity

**Strength of the existing version:** its unassisted probes, novice/expert separation, engagement branch and 12-month horizon are already appropriate.

**Further specification:**

- Use a three-arm cluster-randomized design where feasible: usual practice; model-first delegation; model use with mandatory independent hypotheses, adversarial checks and scheduled practice.
- Establish baseline competence for the atrophy cohort; run a separate novice acquisition cohort.
- Test at baseline, 3, 6, 12 and 24 months with novel, blinded cases, separating immediate removal effects from performance after reorientation.
- Measure **causal tracing directly**: detect an omitted confounder; distinguish observational association from an intervention claim; identify a harmful model update; reconstruct a disputed historical output; propose a test that could refute the model’s attribution.
- Test both model-free capability and **operator-independent capability using ordinary alternative tools**.
- Measure organization-level resilience: independently competent staff, time to reconstruct an attribution, successful challenges, access to archived data and ability to switch models.

**Sharper statement:**

> At 12 months, model-first delegation reduces model-free causal-tracing performance relative to usual practice by at least a preregistered margin—for example 0.2 baseline SD—and the practice-preserving arm materially attenuates that loss.

A confidence interval excluding losses of that magnitude counts against the substantive atrophy prediction; a nonsignificant difference with a wide interval does not. Improvements in unassisted or operator-independent performance must count as contrary evidence, rather than being reclassified as a different kind of dependence.

**Separate P23d:** the proposition that maintenance is cut “first” under fiscal pressure is an institutional-budget hypothesis, not a cognitive hypothesis. Define comparable budget lines, mandatory obligations, the consolidation event and real spending reductions; test whether tracing/practice/audit capacity receives earlier or proportionally larger cuts than prespecified comparators. It could instead be legally protected or cut less, which should disconfirm the claim.

### P24: concentration, access and substantive contestation

**Problem:** “widely adopted” is not necessarily monopoly; “shift” could mean an absolute rise, a proportional rise, or genuine substitution; and access challenges can contain substantive causal challenges.

**Proposed specification, assessed by 31 December 2029:**

- Define dominance before measuring controversies—for example, one model supplies at least 70% of decision-relevant attributions in a preregistered policy domain for 12 consecutive months.
- Independently measure operator control: rights over code, data, historical versions, execution, licensing and recognized certification.
- Code claim units, not just whole documents, into **causal validity**, **access/procedure**, **distribution/justice** and **remedy**, allowing multiple labels.
- Report both absolute dispute rates per 100 decisions and category shares; a falling denominator must not manufacture an apparent access shift.
- Compare concentrated proprietary arrangements with open, multi-model or independently auditable arrangements.
- Record whether outsiders can obtain a consequential decision using an alternative attribution route.

**Split the prediction:**

> **P24a, gatekeeping:** dominant-model operators can block or materially delay independently generated, decision-eligible attribution.
>
> **P24b, agenda change:** access/procedure claims increase after dominance.
>
> **P24c, displacement:** direct causal-validity claims decline, rather than merely acquiring an access component.

Specify minimum meaningful changes—for example +10 percentage points in access-claim share **and** −10 points in causal-validity share, together with the absolute rates. An increase in both categories supports added procedural controversy but **refutes displacement**; continued acceptance of independent alternative models challenges the proposed obligatory-passage-point mechanism even if the leading model remains popular.

## 7. Proposed replacement paragraph

> Attribution tools can make diffuse harms easier to contest, but evidence is not enforcement: WWA estimates changes in weather hazards, and the 2025 *Lliuya* ruling recognized possible emitter liability without awarding relief ([WWA protocol](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/6/177/2020/); [Hamm judgment](https://nrwe.justiz.nrw.de/olgs/hamm/j2025/5_U_15_17_Urteil_20250528.html)). Pharmacovigilance likewise shows why detecting a signal and securing effective action are different tasks ([CIOMS](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/WG8-Signal-Detection.pdf)). Models can help reopen networks of responsibility while becoming trusted packages whose production disappears from view—Law’s punctualisation—but neither dependence nor permanent opacity follows automatically ([Law, 1992](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf)). Exclusive control, weak archives and lost investigative practice can make operators gatekeepers; reproducible methods, independent experts and enforceable audit rights can limit that risk ([WWA protocol](https://ascmo.copernicus.org/articles/6/177/2020/); [*Pickett*](https://www.njcourts.gov/court-opinion/state-of-new-jersey-vs-corey-pickett-17-07-0470-hudson-county-and-statewide-published)). The benefit and danger are related processes of reorganizing authority, not necessary opposites. P22 tests carrier formation and reform in 2028–2030; P23 tests whether delegation weakens independent tracing; P24 tests by 2029 whether concentrated control adds access disputes and displaces causal debate.

**Bottom line:** keep the argument about the political organization of attribution, but replace “successful models necessarily become untraceable” with a conditional proposition about **concentrated control, inadequate provenance and weakened independent capacity**. The most informative comparison is not attribution versus no attribution, but attribution with and without durable institutions for contesting its production and use.
