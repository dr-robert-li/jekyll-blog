# Peer review of the Part H summary claim: attribution technologies, punctualisation and the "same process in opposite directions" thesis

**Reviewer:** Claude (council member), 19 October 2026
**Object reviewed:** the one-paragraph summary of Part H / Appendix D of *Collective Delay in the Face of Foreseen Global Challenges* (sections H.1–H.5, Appendix D tables AT1–AT8, AU1–AU17, ANT1–ANT24).

---

## 0. Verdict in brief

1. **Empirical anchors are mostly accurate but two are miscategorised.** WWA's founding date, study count and protocol check out. The *Lliuya v RWE* summary is accurate as far as it goes, but omits the most instructive fact for this argument: the Hamm court explicitly *rejected* the claimant's statistical "climate factor" in favour of site-specific engineering expertise, i.e. the court's own expert black box out-ranked the attribution tool. The rofecoxib case is a poor fit for "pharmacovigilance as carrier": the signal came from a randomised trial and later from an insurer's claims database, not from spontaneous-report disproportionality, and 99.8% of US spontaneous AMI reports for rofecoxib arrived *after* the label change. More fundamentally, WWA and pharmacovigilance are *instruments*, not *carriers* in the report's own definition (G.1 point 5): the carriers were a litigant, trial lawyers and an FDA whistleblower.
2. **ANT usage is largely faithful but three terms are stretched.** "De-punctualise" is not in Law 1992; Law's reopening is breakdown, not a strategy, and the strategic reopening the report needs is Latour's "controversies reopen black boxes" and Callon's "dissidence". Callon's obligatory passage point is created by *problematisation* and only holds after interessement, enrolment and mobilisation; a model's operators are at most candidate OPPs. And "same ANT process run in opposite directions" conflicts with the report's own use of Callon's irreversibility: translation is not reversible in ANT, so de-punctualisation and re-punctualisation are not one process with a sign flipped.
3. **The symmetry thesis hides at least five asymmetries**: who controls each direction; open protocols versus proprietary models; existing contestability infrastructure (HEI reanalysis, Codecheck, Florida's hurricane-model commission, Basel's output floor, DSA Article 40 litigation); multiplicity of models versus the singular "the model"; and the fact that the strongest historical carriers used no causal model at all.
4. **Atrophy is supported at the individual level and underdetermined at the institutional level.** Bainbridge, Parasuraman and Manzey, Budzyń, Bastani and Shen–Tamkin support individual deskilling under specific conditions. Operational weather forecasting is a sixty-year counterexample: humans still add 20–40% skill over guidance, learn model biases, and the field routinely traces failures of a continuously updating model through archived reanalyses. The report conflates *update frequency* with *absence of a stable referent*; what destroys traceability is lack of archival and reforecast infrastructure, not updating as such.
5. **"Disputes shift from causes to access" has strong precedents but the wrong logical form.** Access disputes (Six Cities data, CovidSim code, DSA Article 40, Florida cat-model trade secrets) are a *phase* of causal contestation, not a substitute for it, and they pre-date causal AI by decades. P24 should measure sequence and ratio, not a one-way shift.
6. **P22–P24 are falsifiable in principle but under-specified** (no effect sizes, no controls, dates that appear only in the summary and not in H.5). Sharper operationalisations are given in §5, and a rewritten paragraph in §6.

## 1. Empirical anchors

### 1.1 World Weather Attribution: accurate

WWA's own site states it was founded in 2014 by Friederike Otto, Geert Jan van Oldenborgh and Climate Central, has performed more than 100 rapid attribution studies, and that 26 of them have additionally been published in peer-reviewed journals ([WWA, About](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/about/)); the event-papers page gives 29 ([WWA, Event papers](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/event-papers/)). Imperial College confirms the 2014 founding and the >100 figure ([Imperial College London](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/environmental-policy/research/research-centres-groups/world-weather-attribution-wwa/)). Two refinements worth adding: the first real-time study was July 2015, so "founded 2014, operating from 2015" is the exact form ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/friederike-otto-world-weather-attribution/)); and WWA's own protocol paper describes the eight-step method as developed over "about two dozen rapid and slow attribution studies" before 2020 ([Philip et al. 2020, ASCMO](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/247447623/ascmo_6_177_2020.pdf)). Otto also reports that WWA found climate change a significant factor in 25 of 30 events in 2024 and 23 of 28 in 2025 ([Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/q-a-friederike-otto-assesses-the-role-of-climate-change-in-extreme-weather-events)), a non-trivial fact for the symmetry argument because a tool that returns null results is harder to treat as a monolithic punctualisation.

**Category error, however.** In G.1 the report defines a carrier as "an actor who moves a downstream cost onto the root veto holders". WWA produces an attribution fraction; it does not move a cost. It raises \(a_j\) in the report's own notation. The carrier in the climate case is a litigant, insurer or attorney general who *uses* the fraction. The summary sentence "creating carriers that push downstream harm back onto veto holders (WWA, Lliuya, pharmacovigilance)" therefore mixes one carrier (Lliuya) with two instruments. This matters for P22, which needs carrier dates and instrument-availability dates as separate variables.

### 1.2 *Lliuya v RWE* (OLG Hamm, 28 May 2025, I-5 U 15/17): accurate but incomplete in the direction that matters

What the report says is confirmed: the appeal was dismissed because court-appointed experts put the probability of flood water reaching the property within 30 years at about 1%, and the court said there was therefore no reason to investigate the causal link between RWE's emissions and the alleged danger ([RWE press release, 28 May 2025](https://www.rwe.com/en/press/rwe-ag/2025-05-28-hamm-higher-regional-court-rejects-peruvian-plaintiffs-appeal-as-unfounded/)). The court nonetheless held that major emitters can in principle be liable under §1004 BGB in proportion to their share ([Noerr](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/lliuya-v-rwe-landmark-ruling-against-major-greenhouse-gas-emitters); [LSE Grantham Institute](https://www.lse.ac.uk/granthaminstitute/news/luciano-lliuya-v-rwe-a-major-step-forward-for-climate-justice/)). The judgment is final; no *Revision* was allowed ([OLG Hamm press release](https://www.olg-hamm.nrw.de/behoerde/presse/Pressemitteilungen/zt-Archiv/2025_Pressearchiv/14_25_PE_OLG_VT-Lliuya_RWE/index.php)).

Three points the report should add:

- **The court did reason on causation, in legal not scientific terms.** It applied *conditio sine qua non* limited by adequacy, affirmed adequate causation on the ground that "an optimal observer in the role of the defendant could have recognized as early as the mid-1960s that a significantly increased industrial CO₂ output would lead to global warming", and held that RWE's 0.38% share of industrial emissions is not insignificant given that no single emitter exceeds 3.6% ([C2LI Litigation Brief, Lexxion](https://www.lexxion.eu/en/cclr-blog/c2li-litigation-brief-2-2025-higher-regional-court-of-hamm-germany-lliuya-v-rwe-judgment-of-28-may-2025/)). Appendix D's "the court never reached the attribution evidence" is right about the *event* attribution step but wrong if read as "the court said nothing about causation".
- **The court rejected the claimant's statistical method.** The OLG press release states that the claimant's preferred "general statistical assessment, in particular the inclusion of a 'climate factor' to raise the probability of occurrence", was rejected in favour of the expert's site-specific hazard analysis, and that the true risk lay "well below" 1% ([OLG Hamm press release](https://www.olg-hamm.nrw.de/behoerde/presse/Pressemitteilungen/zt-Archiv/2025_Pressearchiv/14_25_PE_OLG_VT-Lliuya_RWE/index.php); [LTO](https://www.lto.de/recht/nachrichten/n/olg-hamm-5u1517-klimaklage-peruanischer-bauer-gegen-rwe-abgewiesen)). For the report's argument this is the most interesting fact in the case: the carrier's attribution-style instrument was subordinated to the forum's own expert black box (court-appointed engineers under German civil procedure). The OPP here was not the model's operators but the court's expert-appointment power. That is evidence *against* the claim that attribution models become the OPP "for every future carrier", and evidence *for* the report's weaker claim that attribution raises \(\varphi\) without displacing \(V\).
- **Two post-Appendix-D developments move the "standing in principle" line.** Callahan and Mankin argue in *Nature* (April 2025) that "end-to-end" attribution linking individual producers to heat-related losses is now feasible and that "science [is] no longer an obstacle to the justiciability of climate liability claims", estimating Chevron's emissions caused US$791 billion to $3.6 trillion in heat-related losses over 1991–2020 ([Callahan & Mankin 2025, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40269281/)). The ICJ's July 2025 advisory opinion distinguished whether an event can be attributed to climate change (addressable by science) from whether damage can be attributed to a particular state, which "must be established *in concreto*", while holding that it is possible to determine scientifically each state's emissions contribution ([AJIL case note](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-journal-of-international-law/article/obligations-of-states-in-respect-of-climate-change-advisory-opinion/02B74D32C41D0F9C83BE0FB8233DB128); [ICLQ](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-and-comparative-law-quarterly/article/2025-international-court-of-justice-advisory-opinion-on-obligations-of-states-in-respect-of-climate-change/DED109CE194CB420EA6C911F1005E620)). The report's "no damages award yet" remains true; the direction of travel in 2025 is from instrument availability to recognised justiciability, with the *in concreto* step still the binding constraint. That is exactly P22's claim, and it should be cited as its first data point.

### 1.3 Pharmacovigilance and rofecoxib: the facts are right, the pairing is wrong

The timeline in AT7 (VIGOR 2000, label change April 2002, withdrawal 30 September 2004 after APPROVe, >80 million patients, Jüni's cumulative meta-analysis) is standard and confirmed in reviews ([Cleveland Clinic Journal of Medicine](https://www.ccjm.org/content/ccjom/71/12/933.full.pdf); [Graham et al. 2005 summary, Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/538095)). The problem is that H.1 cites *spontaneous-report disproportionality* (PRR, BCPNN) as the attribution tool, and H.2 then uses rofecoxib as the case in which "detection is rarely the bottleneck". Rofecoxib was not detected by that tool:

- A 2024 study of US reporting data found that 99.8% of spontaneous reports of rofecoxib-associated acute myocardial infarction were submitted *after* the risk was added to the label and 93.2% after withdrawal; the authors title the paper "the futility of adverse drug event reporting systems for monitoring known safety issues" and note that events with high background rates are poorly captured by spontaneous reporting ([Pharmacoepidemiology and Drug Safety, 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/pds.5719)).
- The decisive post-marketing evidence was a nested case-control study in 1.4 million Kaiser Permanente members by FDA's David Graham, i.e. a claims/EHR database with denominators, not a reporting system ([Graham et al. 2005, Lancet, via Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/538095)).
- Retrospective reconstructions show that *active* surveillance would have flagged the signal within one to three years ([Drug Safety, 2013, Australia](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40264-013-0124-9); [PMC, EHR historical reconstruction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5997784/)), and a US analysis estimates about 8 million person-years of exposure occurred after the point an active programme would have detected it ([PMC, return on public investment in detecting adverse events](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5433260/)).

So the honest statement is: the signal existed in an RCT in 2000, the tool the report names would not have found it, a different tool (claims-based active surveillance) could have found it years earlier but was not institutionalised until Sentinel (2008 onward), and the actual carrier that moved cost onto Merck was product-liability litigation. This strengthens the report's "V binds" conclusion while weakening the "pharmacovigilance creates carriers" anchor. The reviewer recommends replacing "pharmacovigilance" with "post-marketing surveillance, where it has denominators" and keeping rofecoxib as the *V binds* example only.

## 2. Is the ANT usage faithful?

### 2.1 Punctualisation (Law 1992): faithful on the concept, loose on the verb

Law's text supports the report's reading: "if a network acts as a single block, then it disappears, to be replaced by the action itself and the seemingly simple author of that action"; "network patterns that are widely performed are often those that can be punctualised"; and "punctualisation is always precarious, it faces resistance, and may degenerate into a failing network" ([Law 1992, Lancaster copy](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/fass/resources/sociology-online-papers/papers/law-notes-on-ant.pdf)). Two corrections:

- **Law attributes the term to others** ("actor network theorists sometimes talk of such precarious simplificatory effects as punctualisations"). The formal definition is Callon's: punctualisation "converts an entire network into a single point or node in another network" ([Callon 1991, p. 153, quoted in SFU paper](https://summit.sfu.ca/_flysystem/fedora/sfu_migrate/13593/0901.pdf)). Since the report already cites Callon 1991 for irreversibility, the citation should be "Callon 1991; Law 1992".
- **"De-punctualise" is not Law's word, and Law's reopening is not a strategy.** In Law, a punctualisation "degenerates into a failing network"; it is a breakdown, not an act. The report needs an *agentive* reopening, which ANT supplies elsewhere: Latour's "controversies reopen black boxes" (*Science in Action*, ch. 1) and Callon's "dissidence" (1986), which the report already cites. Using "reopen the black box" rather than "de-punctualise" would be both more faithful and more precise, because it names the mechanism (controversy, with new allies) rather than the result.

### 2.2 Obligatory passage point (Callon 1986): stretched from "achieved through problematisation" to "held by infrastructure"

In Callon, the OPP is produced in the *problematisation* moment: the researchers define everyone's problem so that their own programme becomes the point through which all must pass, and this holds only if interessement, enrolment and mobilisation succeed; dissidence later destroys it (Appendix D ANT7, confirmed). Two consequences:

- A widely used model's operators do not *become* the OPP by scale alone. They become it only if they succeed in redefining policy disputes as questions the model answers *and* the other actors accept those roles. The report says this ("problematise policy disputes as questions the model answers") but the summary sentence drops the conditional and states "its operators then become the obligatory passage point for every future carrier". That is an outcome Callon's framework treats as contingent and reversible.
- Callon's OPP is a *problem definition*, not an infrastructural gate. The report's "epistemic veto" over data access, licensing and retraining is closer to Star and Ruhleder's or Bowker and Star's infrastructure, or to Pasquale's one-way mirror, than to Callon. The report has those references; it should route the "access" half of P24 through them and keep Callon for the "who defines the question" half.

### 2.3 Blackboxing (Latour): faithful, but it undercuts the novelty of P24

Latour's blackboxing as work "made invisible by its own success" is correctly cited. But Latour's companion claim is that black boxes are reopened by controversy, which is *why* disputes about a successful instrument take the form of disputes about its internals and inputs. P24, read against Latour, is a corollary rather than a novel prediction. Its empirical content lies in the quantitative shift and the timing, which is where the operationalisation must do the work (§5).

### 2.4 "The same ANT process run in opposite directions": not an ANT claim, and in tension with the report's own point 5

ANT's symmetry is methodological (Callon's generalised symmetry: analyse winners and losers, humans and non-humans, in the same vocabulary). It is not a claim that network processes are reversible. On the contrary, the report's own point 5 cites Callon 1991 on irreversibility: once links achieve longevity they "shape future processes of translation". If re-punctualisation by a shared model is irreversible in that sense, then de-punctualisation by carriers is *not* the same process with the sign changed; it is a different, weaker, case-by-case process fighting an accumulating one. The symmetry wording should be replaced with an explicit asymmetry: *carriers reopen black boxes one case at a time; a shared model closes one over all cases at once.* That is a stronger and more accurate sentence, and it is what §3 turns into testable asymmetries.

## 3. Asymmetries the symmetry thesis hides

### 3.1 Control: who can run each direction

Carriers are plural and uncoordinated (litigants, insurers, attorneys general, foreign regulators). A model operator is singular by hypothesis. The actors who can de-punctualise are not the actors who re-punctualise, and they have opposite incentives. The report's capture point in H.2 already implies this; the summary's symmetry sentence erases it.

### 3.2 Open protocol versus proprietary model

WWA publishes its protocol and uses public reanalyses and CMIP ensembles ([Philip et al. 2020](https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/247447623/ascmo_6_177_2020.pdf)); its results are regularly contested inside the field (the probabilistic versus storyline debate: Lloyd and Oreskes note that the storyline proposal "proved highly controversial, with the majority of D&A scientists reacting in a very negative and even personal manner", and that the risk-based approach tends to type II errors while storylines tend to type I) ([Lloyd & Oreskes 2018, Earth's Future](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/dspace/items/653d7f2e-9dbd-4435-b0ef-751a4666f011/full); [BAMS 2020 summary](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/101/8/bamsD200026.pdf)). An attribution field with two live methodological camps and a published protocol is not a punctualised black box; it is a visible controversy. The re-punctualisation risk is real for *proprietary* models with closed data, and the report should say so explicitly rather than treating "causal AI" as one object.

### 3.3 Contestability infrastructure already exists and has worked

The report lists mitigations (snapshots, provenance, practised tracing) as hypothetical costs that fiscal pressure will cut. Several have in fact been institutionalised for decades, in exactly the domains where a model became an OPP:

- **Florida Commission on Hurricane Loss Projection Methodology (1995).** Created by statute after proprietary catastrophe models became the basis of insurance rates; it audits each vendor model (Verisk/AIR since 1996, RMS since 1997, CoreLogic, ARA, and a public Florida model since 2006) against standards revised every odd year, in closed session for trade secrets; insurers may use only accepted model versions in rate filings and may not modify them ([NAIC materials](https://content.naic.org/sites/default/files/national_meeting/Materials_3rdPartyDMTF%203.16.2024_1.pdf); [South Carolina DOI report](https://www.doi.sc.gov/DocumentCenter/View/7964/Report-on-the-Feasibility-of-Developing-a-South-Carolina-Hurricane-Model-12-31-14)). This is a thirty-year-old regime that both confirms P24 (disputes moved to model acceptance) and refutes the "mitigations are the first to go" step: the audit body survived and expanded to flood models.
- **Basel III output floor (2017).** After finding that internal-model risk weights varied for reasons unrelated to risk ("some banks gamed model weaknesses"), supervisors imposed a floor of 72.5% of standardised risk-weighted assets "to reduce excessive variability of RWA" ([BIS, Basel III finalisation](https://www.bis.org/publications/201712-standards-basel-iii-finalising-post-crisis-reforms.pdf); [Bundesbank speech](https://www.bundesbank.de/en/press/speeches/to-count-or-not-to-count-the-future-of-internal-models-in-banking-regulation-711570)). This is regulators *re-punctualising around* proprietary models by bounding their authority with a simple public rule, a mitigation the report does not consider: not tracing the model, but capping what it may decide.
- **HEI reanalysis of the Six Cities study (1998–2000).** When industry and Congress demanded raw data behind the particulate standard, the investigators arranged an independent reanalysis by the Health Effects Institute, funded jointly by EPA and automakers, which cost US$900,000, audited the data, replicated the results and tested them against alternative models; it essentially confirmed the original findings ([National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/10302/chapter/3); [HEI comments to EPA](https://www.healtheffects.org/sites/default/files/HEI-comments-on-EPA-Suppl-Transparency-Rule-Docket-EPA-HQ-OA-2018-0259-May-18-2020.pdf)). The dispute moved to access and then *back* to causes, which were re-litigated and upheld.
- **Codecheck of CovidSim (2020).** When Imperial's model became the OPP for UK lockdown policy, critics attacked code quality and non-determinism; an independent Codecheck reproduced Report 9 within about 5% ([Imperial College](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/news/197875/codecheck-confirms-reproducibility-covid-19-model-results/)), and a UCL–CWI ensemble study later showed the model amplified input uncertainty by up to 300%, possible "only because Professor Neil Ferguson's team open sourced their code" ([CWI](https://www.cwi.nl/en/news/multiple-simulations-best-for-covid-19-predictions/); [Nature Computational Science](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-021-00028-9)). Again, access contestation was a route back to substantive contestation.
- **DSA Article 40 enforcement (2025–26).** The Commission fined X €120 million in December 2025, €40 million of it for researcher-access failures, and X's accepted corrective plan removes contractual bans on independent data collection and submits it to external audit ([Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/what-xs-corrected-action-under-the-dsa-fixes-on-data-access-and-whats-left-open/)); the Berlin Court of Appeal in February 2026 ordered X to give Democracy Reporting International election data under Article 40(12) ([GFF](https://freiheitsrechte.org/en/themen/freiheit-im-digitalen-zeitalter/x)). The report cites DSA access as a "near analogue" of P24; it is a better analogue of *successful* de-punctualisation by law.

The conclusion is not that the risk in H.3 is imaginary; it is that the record of OPP-type models in insurance, banking, air-quality epidemiology and epidemic policy shows contestability infrastructure being *built* in response to OPP status, not cut. The fiscal-erosion prediction (P23d) therefore has at least four prima facie counterexamples that must be coded.

### 3.4 Multiplicity: there is no "the model"

The summary says "a successful causal model becomes a new punctualisation". In every domain the report cites there are competing models and ensembles (CMIP, multiple catastrophe vendors, multiple pharmacovigilance databases). The realistic OPP is not a model but a *shared input*: a reanalysis dataset, an emissions inventory such as the Carbon Majors database used in *Lliuya* (0.38% share), or a vetted-researcher access regime. P24's "who may retrain it" should be reframed as "who controls the shared inputs and the acceptance standard".

### 3.5 Historical carriers used no model

The Medicaid cost-recovery suits, the 1998 Master Settlement, the US skin-cancer valuation that moved ozone policy, and France's rating downgrades (all App. C) required no causal AI. If the strongest carriers in the record are model-free, then the dependency "every future carrier would have to pass through" the model is overstated. A sharper claim: *carriers that depend on fine-grained attribution* (proportional liability, event-specific insurance repricing) would pass through shared attribution infrastructure; carriers that depend on coarse, uncontested causation (tobacco, lead) would not.

## 4. The atrophy claim and the access-shift claim against the literature

### 4.1 Support for atrophy, with scope conditions

- **Bainbridge (1983)** and **Parasuraman and Manzey (2010)** are correctly cited (Appendix D AU1, AU2).
- **Budzyń et al. 2025**: ADR in non-AI colonoscopy fell from 28.4% to 22.4% after AI introduction, OR 0.69 ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40816301/)). The erratum, which Appendix D could not reach, states that a covariate (indication for colonoscopy) was omitted from a supplementary multivariable analysis and that the correction "does not affect the interpretation of the data" ([correction text](https://www.studocu.com/es-mx/document/universidad-autonoma-benito-juarez-de-oaxaca/medicina-familiar/correction-to-lancet-gastroenterol-hepatol-2025-vol-10-updates/148078511)). The authors call it hypothesis-generating; commentators note fewer than 100 colonoscopies per physician and that ADR "may vary by 8% or more by random chance" at that size ([Medscape](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/ai-use-causing-endoscopists-lose-their-skills-2025a1000mcn)). A 2026 scoping review calls the clinical deskilling evidence "scarce" though consistent ([PubMed scoping review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41890350/)).
- **Bastani et al. 2025 (PNAS)**: in a ~1,000-student cluster-randomised trial, unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice scores 48% but lowered subsequent unassisted exam scores 17%; a guardrailed tutor eliminated the harm without producing a gain ([PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122)).
- **Shen and Tamkin 2026**: a 52-developer RCT found AI assistance cut post-task mastery of a new library by 17 percentage points (d = 0.74) with no significant speed gain, with the largest gap in *debugging* ([arXiv 2601.20245](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20245v1); [Anthropic summary](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)).

These are evidence for P23(a) (failed formation) and P23(c) (design moderates the effect). They are not evidence for P23(b) (decay in experienced practitioners over time), which only Budzyń addresses, weakly. The debugging result in Shen–Tamkin is the closest existing proxy for "tracing": the skill most impaired was finding out *why* an output was wrong.

### 4.2 Counterexamples the report does not engage

- **Operational weather forecasting** is the longest-running case of delegating prediction to an opaque, continuously re-initialised model. Verification over two decades shows Weather Prediction Center forecasters still achieve a 20–40% improvement over the deterministic NAM and GFS for day-1 heavy precipitation and 5–15% over ECMWF, with statistically significant temperature improvements that have been *increasing* ([Novak et al. 2014, Weather and Forecasting](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/wefo/29/3/waf-d-13-00066_1.xml)). Novak observes that "as forecasters become more familiar with a particular model, they begin to notice its biases and failings" ([Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/weather-forecasting-artifical-intelligence/)): exposure built a *new* tracing skill (model critique) rather than eroding one. The BAMS consensus statement anticipates humans remaining essential for quality control of model guidance, high-impact events and decision support ([Stuart et al. 2022, BAMS](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/103/8/BAMS-D-20-0326.1.xml)). The honest counter-reading is also in the literature: the human margin is small and diminishing for routine forecasts, and "forecaster apathy" is a recognised risk when value cannot be added ([BAMS 2007](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/88/12/bams-88-12-1893.pdf)). So forecasting supports a *conditional* atrophy claim: the human role narrows to rescuing busts and rare events, which is Bainbridge's irony, but the skill is retained where institutions keep humans issuing the forecast.
- **Tracing a continuously updating model is routine where archives exist.** ECMWF's model is re-initialised every cycle and upgraded several times a year, yet Rodwell et al. traced 584 European "forecast busts" over 1989–2010 using a fixed reanalysis forecast system, identified the common initial-condition pattern (a Rockies trough with convective instability) and the responsible process (mesoscale convection), and showed busts fell from about 70 per year in 1990 to about 5 in 2011 ([Rodwell et al. 2013, BAMS](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/94/9/bams-d-12-00099.1.xml)); the analysis has since been replicated in ERA5 reforecasts ([ECMWF workshop](https://events.ecmwf.int/event/383/contributions/4557/attachments/2723/4618/Diagnostics-WS_Hauser.pdf)). This directly refutes H.3(ii) as stated ("an updating model tracks and cannot be traced"). What makes tracing possible is not freezing the model but *archiving every analysis and reforecasting with frozen versions*. The trade-off is therefore not update-rate versus traceability but update-rate versus *archival and reforecast budget*, which relocates the argument to M-erosion where the report wants it anyway.
- **Institutional versus individual atrophy.** All cited deskilling evidence is individual. The report's institutional claim (M erodes under fiscal pressure: OTA, Qing granaries) is a different mechanism with different evidence, and §3.3 lists four institutions that grew rather than shrank. The paragraph should not slide from "operators deskill" to "society loses the capacity to trace".

### 4.3 "Disputes shift from causes to access"

Precedents are abundant: the Six Cities data demand and EPA's 2018 "Strengthening Transparency in Regulatory Science" proposal, which would have excluded studies whose raw data could not be supplied ([Nature editorial](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-019-03526-z)); the CovidSim code fight ([CACM](https://cacm.acm.org/blogcacm/the-software-that-led-to-the-lockdown/)); DSA Article 40 litigation; and Florida's closed-session trade-secret reviews. But in each, the access dispute was *instrumental* to a causal dispute and was followed by renewed causal contestation once access was granted (HEI reanalysis; CovidSim uncertainty quantification). Two implications:

- P24's dichotomy "causes vs access" is false. The defensible form is a *sequence*: adoption → access contestation → (if access is won) renewed causal contestation on the model's own terms; (if access is refused) displacement of causal contestation into procedural or political channels.
- Access disputes long pre-date causal AI; the mechanism is "any authoritative evidential infrastructure", so the test must show that *model adoption* raises the access share beyond the baseline that any regulatory science already carries (Jasanoff's regulatory-science deconstruction is the relevant prior literature and should be cited).

## 5. Are P22–P24 well specified? Sharper operationalisations

**General problems.** (i) The dates "2028–2030" and "2029" appear only in the summary claim, not in H.5; they must be either defined (what observation by what date falsifies) or removed. (ii) No prediction states an effect size or a baseline. (iii) Reverse causality is unaddressed: attribution studies are commissioned where litigation is foreseeable, and WWA uses a humanitarian-impact trigger ([Physics Today](https://physicstoday.aip.org/news/q-a-friederike-otto-assesses-the-role-of-climate-change-in-extreme-weather-events)), so instrument availability is endogenous to carrier potential.

**P22 (carriers before reform).**
- Unit: hazard class × jurisdiction. Treatment date: first WWA or peer-reviewed attribution for that class and region (WWA study tracker). Outcomes: time to first (a) filed suit citing attribution, (b) insurer rate filing or withdrawal citing climate attribution, (c) regulatory standing decision, (d) root reform (coded ex ante: emissions-side statute or price, not adaptation spending).
- Controls: hazard classes where attribution confidence is low (tornado, hail) and jurisdictions without standing doctrines.
- Falsifiers with numbers: the hazard ratio for carrier events after attribution availability is ≤1.0, or root reform follows attribution availability with no intervening carrier in more than a stated share of cases (say one third).
- First observations already available: Hamm 2025 (standing in principle, instrument subordinated), ICJ 2025 (*in concreto* requirement), Callahan–Mankin 2025 (instrument ahead of forum).

**P23 (atrophy of tracing skill).**
- Separate three dependent variables: formation (novices), decay (experts, time since last unassisted practice), and institutional maintenance (budget lines).
- Pre-specify effect sizes: e.g. a 6-point ADR drop (Budzyń) or d ≈ 0.7 on mastery (Shen–Tamkin) as the minimum detectable effect; stepped-wedge rollout with scheduled unassisted probes at 3, 12 and 24 months; washout arms.
- Add a *negative-control domain* where institutions keep humans in the loop (operational forecasting) and a *positive-control* where they do not (fully automated screening). If atrophy appears in both, the design claim (P23c) fails; if only in the second, it holds.
- For P23(d), code the four institutions in §3.3 plus OTA and the FAA manual-flying guidance as a panel: did oversight budgets rise or fall relative to the model's budget over ten years?
- Reclassify debugging/verification ability as the tracing proxy, following Shen–Tamkin.

**P24 (access over causes).**
- Coding unit: filings and submissions in a proceeding, coded into (i) contesting the causal claim on substance, (ii) contesting method/assumptions, (iii) demanding access to data/code/model, (iv) contesting acceptance standards or licensing.
- Prediction, sharpened: after a shared model is adopted, the share of (iii)+(iv) rises by at least a stated amount (e.g. 15 points) within two proceeding cycles, *and* the share of (i) falls; then, conditional on access being granted, (i)+(ii) recover within one cycle. The second clause distinguishes the report's "epistemic veto" (access refused, causes no longer contested) from ordinary regulatory science (access granted, causes contested again).
- Retrospective panels available now: Florida Commission dockets since 1996, Basel internal-model consultations 2012–2017, EPA Six Cities 1994–2000, SAGE/CovidSim 2020, DSA Article 40 proceedings 2024–26.

## 6. Proposed rewording of the claim paragraph

> Attribution tools loosen accreted wickedness in one specific way: they raise the attributability of distant links, which lets carriers (litigants, insurers, regulators) move downstream costs onto veto holders. Extreme-event attribution has produced recognised liability in principle but no damages award, and in *Lliuya v RWE* the court subordinated the claimant's statistical attribution to its own site-specific experts; post-marketing drug surveillance detects signals where it has denominators, yet rofecoxib stayed on the market four years after its trial signal. Attribution therefore shifts perception, not the veto conditions under which root reform occurs. A different risk follows from success rather than failure. A proprietary model with closed data that becomes the accepted basis for attribution in a domain is a new black box: too large to read, re-estimated faster than its outputs can be contested unless every version and input is archived, and liable to displace the human skill of tracing where institutions let it. Its controllers then hold the inputs and acceptance standards through which fine-grained carriers must pass. The two movements are not one process reversed. Carriers reopen black boxes case by case; a shared model closes one over all cases at once, and the record of catastrophe models, bank internal models and epidemic models shows that the counter-move is institutional (audit commissions, output floors, mandated access), not individual. Forward tests: P22, carriers accelerate after attribution becomes available while root reform still waits for a veto-side trigger; P23, unassisted tracing proficiency falls where humans are removed from the loop and holds where they are kept issuing the judgement; P24, after adoption of a shared model, disputes move first to access and acceptance standards, and return to causes only where access is won.
